## From the front to the stage

Mykola Hrynko was born in 1920 in Kherson. He went through the Second World War, and afterwards became an actor at the drama theatre in Zaporizhzhia, where he played from 1946 to 1955; he also worked at a theatre in Uzhhorod. Then he moved to Kyiv and spent several years performing on the variety stage.

## Five films with Tarkovsky

Hrynko acted in films from 1951, and in 1963 he joined the Oleksandr Dovzhenko Film Studio in Kyiv. He is best known for the films of the director Andrei Tarkovsky. Hrynko appeared in five of them: as Gryaznov in Ivan’s Childhood, the icon painter Daniil Chorny in Andrei Rublev, the hero’s father in Solaris, the head of a printing house in Mirror and the Professor in Stalker.

## Chekhov, a colonel and Papa Carlo

In A Subject for a Short Story Hrynko became Anton Chekhov; in [Leonid Bykov](/en/people/leonid-bykov/)’s Aty-baty, the Soldiers Were Going he was a colonel, and in Yaroslav the Wise he played Nikon. And for children he became Papa Carlo, who carves Buratino out of a log in The Adventures of Buratino (1975). He was later made a People’s Artist of the Ukrainian SSR.

## Why it matters today

Mykola Hrynko died in Kyiv in 1989. He lives on in films people still love, from the fairy tale about a wooden boy to Stalker. Ukraine’s parliament included the 100th anniversary of his birth among the dates the country marked in 2020.
