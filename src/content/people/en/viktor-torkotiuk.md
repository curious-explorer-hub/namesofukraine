## Horses from the Carpathians

Did you know that horses serve in a Ukrainian military intelligence unit? When Artan's fighters had to cross heavily mined ground, one of them suggested carrying the ammunition on horseback. “We took horses, strong ones, from the Carpathian mountains. […] They're part of the special unit,” says their commander, Viktor Torkotiuk, call sign Titan.

## A boxer who wanted to serve

Viktor Torkotiuk was born in 1980 in Azerbaijan, where his father, a soldier from the Vinnytsia region, was serving at the time. He grew up in Zhmerynka. He dreamed of being a soldier from childhood: in 1994 he entered a military school in Kryvyi Rih and later studied in Odesa. Alongside his training he boxed, then took up Thai boxing. In 2019, aged 39, he won bronze at the world championships and became a Master of Sport, International Class.

## From the east to Artan

From 2016 Torkotiuk fought in the east as a volunteer, and in 2018 he signed a contract with the 130th Separate Reconnaissance Battalion. His deep-reconnaissance company often crossed behind the front line.

After the full-scale invasion began, he led a small group in the Kyiv region. Athletes he knew began asking to join, and later Defence Intelligence (HUR) sent him more people. In late spring and early summer 2022 they were formed into the special unit Artan.

## At sea, in Crimea, at Bakhmut

Artan fought at Bakhmut, carried out ten missions in occupied Crimea and went out into the Black Sea in boats, ten fighters to a boat. Once a fighter called Conan fell overboard and spent 14 hours in the open sea. Their commander insisted the search go on, and the man was rescued. Torkotiuk became a full holder of the Order for Courage, and in early 2026 he was promoted to colonel. That same year Artan fought near Stepnohirsk in the Zaporizhzhia region.

## Why it matters today

Viktor Torkotiuk showed that a strong unit grows out of people who come to fight of their own accord: Artan began as a small group of volunteers and athletes he knew. The discipline he brought from the ring became part of how the unit fights. His rule of never leaving one of your own behind, even when the odds are poor, holds the team together. It is an example of how personal strength and responsibility for others become the strength of a whole army.
