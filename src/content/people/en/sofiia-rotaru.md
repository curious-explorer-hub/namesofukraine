## A song that became a name

Did you know that the band that made Sofiia Rotaru famous was named after a song? From October 1971 she sang with the band Chervona Ruta at the Chernivtsi Philharmonic, and [Volodymyr Ivasiuk](/en/people/volodymyr-ivasiuk/)’s song of the same name became her signature. That year the musical TV film Chervona Ruta came out, with her in the lead role.

## From Marshyntsi to Chernivtsi

Sofiia Rotaru was born in 1947 in the village of Marshyntsi in Bukovyna (now in the Chernivtsi region). As a girl she sang in amateur groups, won a republic-wide contest and after school went to the Chernivtsi music college. In 1968, the year she graduated, she became a laureate of the World Festival of Youth and Students in Sofia, Bulgaria. For a while she taught at the college.

## From Sopot to hundreds of songs

In 1973 Rotaru won the Golden Orpheus contest in Bulgaria, and in 1974 the festival in Sopot, Poland. That same year she graduated from the Chișinău Institute of Arts, and from 1975 she was a soloist of the Crimean Philharmonic. Her repertoire runs to more than 400 songs by Ukrainian, Moldovan and Russian composers, among them Ivasiuk, Levko Dutkovskyi and Eugen Doga. She toured the USSR, Australia, Europe, Asia and the Americas.

In 1988 Rotaru became a People’s Artist of the USSR, and in 2002 she was made a Hero of Ukraine. In 2021 she was among the first to receive the National Legend of Ukraine award, together with the poet [Yurii Rybchynskyi](/en/people/yurii-rybchynskyi/), the painter [Ivan Marchuk](/en/people/ivan-marchuk/) and the footballer [Oleh Blokhin](/en/people/oleh-blokhin/).

## Debates and assessments

Rotaru built much of her career on the stages of the USSR and later Russia, and her performances there after 2014 are judged in different ways. In 2014, after Russia occupied Crimea, she was photographed with a Ukrainian flag, and her concerts in Russia began to be cancelled. Yet she kept appearing at Russia’s televised Song of the Year festival: she stayed away in 2018 but set off for Moscow again in 2019. That trip set off a campaign against her in Russia, and her concert director defended her on the Russian propaganda talk show 60 Minutes.

After the full-scale invasion of 2022, Rotaru spoke out against the war on Instagram, and in August 2022 she urged people to support a fund that helps the wives and children of Ukrainian soldiers. Russia’s occupation authorities in Crimea have since demanded that her hotel in Yalta and her honorary citizenship of the city be taken from her; one Crimean official said she “takes an anti-Russian position.”

## Why it matters today

Chervona Ruta launched the career of one of the best-known singers ever to come out of Ukraine. Several generations know her songs, and the arguments about her performances in Russia go on.
