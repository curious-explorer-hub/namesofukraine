## One breath, no fins

Imagine taking one breath and swimming 88 metres down into the sea, then back to the surface. No air tank, no fins, no pulling on the rope: just your own arms and legs. That is Constant Weight Without Fins (CNF), the hardest of freediving's depth disciplines. In December 2025, at a competition in Dominica, Kateryna Sadurska made that trip in 3 minutes 30 seconds. It was her ninth world record in the discipline.

## From the pool to the open sea

Kateryna Sadurska was born on 19 July 1992 in Mykolaiv. She started in artistic swimming and swam for Ukraine's national team, winning bronze at the 2013 World Championships and gold at the 2014 and 2016 European Championships in team events. In 2016 she went to the Olympic Games in Rio de Janeiro, where the Ukrainian team finished fourth.

When her artistic swimming career ended, Sadurska took up freediving. "It's a mix of meditation and sport," she explained. She competes for Kharkiv.

## Nine records

Freediving has two international federations, AIDA and CMAS, and each keeps its own records. Sadurska has set them in both. Her first no-fins world record came in July 2023, at 74 metres, and she improved it twice more that month. Then came 78 metres at the World Championships in Honduras, 80 in Greece, and four records at competitions in Dominica: 82, 84, 86 and 88 metres. In two and a half years she added 14 metres to her first record. By early 2025 she had six world titles.

In August 2026, at the CMAS World Championships on the Honduran island of Roatán, Sadurska won her first gold in Constant Weight with fins, at 108 metres. Ukraine sent two freedivers to those championships: Sadurska and [Nataliia Zharkova](/en/people/nataliia-zharkova/).

## Why it matters today

Sadurska's home is Kharkiv, a city that has lived under Russian strikes since the full-scale invasion began. From there she sets off to competitions around the world, and comes back with new records. She names telling the world what is happening in Ukraine as one of her main reasons to compete. Her career shows that you can start over: an Olympian in one sport became a world record holder in another. Every one of her records is a few more metres that no woman had reached without fins before, with the Ukrainian flag above the water.
