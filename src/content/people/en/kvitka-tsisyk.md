## Whose voice is in the Oscar-winning song?

Did you know that the heroine of the 1977 American film You Light Up My Life sings with a Ukrainian voice? Kvitka Cisyk recorded the song for Joe Brooks’s film, but her name was left out of the credits. The version that topped the charts was a different recording, by Debby Boone, and the song itself won an Oscar and a Golden Globe.

## A violinist’s daughter, by way of Lviv

Kvitka (Kvitoslava-Orysia) Cisyk was born in 1953 in Queens, New York. Her family had lived in Lviv until 1944, and Ukrainian was spoken at home. Her father, the violinist Volodymyr Cisyk, taught her music from the age of four, and her older sister Maria became a well-known pianist. Kvitka went to a Saturday school of Ukrainian studies, spent her summers at Plast camps (Plast is the Ukrainian scouting movement), and later studied at New York’s High School of Music and Art and at the Mannes College of Music, took part in an opera seminar in Ghent, Belgium, and dreamed of opera. She was a coloratura soprano, and she could also sing in the folk “white voice”.

## The most wanted voice in advertising

In 1971 her father died suddenly, and Kvitka had to support the family. From the mid-1970s, under the name Kacey, she worked in the studio: she sang radio and TV commercials and sang backing vocals for Carly Simon, Linda Ronstadt, Roberta Flack and Michael Bolton. From the early 1980s she was one of the most popular jingle singers in the United States, and from 1981 until her death she was the voice of Ford’s advertising.

For Ukraine, she paid out of her own pocket, about 200,000 dollars, to record two albums of Ukrainian songs: Kvitka: Songs of Ukraine (1980) and Two Colors (1989). In the Soviet Union her songs were banned: her records reached Ukraine unofficially, and people copied the tapes and passed them on. In 1983 Kvitka and her mother made an unofficial visit to Lviv and other towns, her only trip to her parents’ homeland. She died of cancer in New York on 29 March 1998, six days before her 45th birthday.

## Why it matters today

Kvitka Cisyk could have remained just one of the most popular voices in American advertising. But this New Yorker, the daughter of a family from Lviv, paid to record two albums of Ukrainian songs herself and brought many listeners to Ukrainian song. She wanted to weave “joyful notes” into the fabric of her people’s life, and she showed that a culture can be cherished far from home. Anyone living abroad can carry Ukraine with them in the same way.
