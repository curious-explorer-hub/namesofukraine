## The general who studied diplomacy

Did you know that in December 2020 a serving general finished a master's degree in international relations at the Ostroh Academy? At the time Valerii Zaluzhnyi commanded the troops in northern Ukraine. Four years later he was an ambassador.

He was born on 8 July 1973 in Novohrad-Volynskyi (now Zviahel), in the Zhytomyr region. In 1997 he graduated with honours from a military institute in Odesa and worked his way up through every rung: platoon, company, battalion commander. In 2009 he took over the 51st Separate Mechanized Brigade, and in December 2019 he became commander of Operational Command North.

## Commander-in-Chief

On 27 July 2021 Zaluzhnyi was appointed Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of Ukraine, seven months before russia's full-scale invasion. He led the army through the first two years of the war. In March 2022 he was made a full general, and in May Time magazine put him on its list of the world's 100 most influential people.

In early November 2023 Zaluzhnyi gave an interview to the British magazine The Economist and published an essay, "Modern Positional Warfare and How to Win It". The war, he wrote, was gradually turning positional: the two sides had reached technological and tactical parity, and the front was thick with mines. A war like that suits russia, because it drags on. To break out of it, Ukraine needed air superiority, ways to breach deep minefields, better counter-battery fire, trained reserves and more electronic warfare.

## From the army to diplomacy

On 8 February 2024 Zaluzhnyi was dismissed as Commander-in-Chief. The same day a presidential decree made him a Hero of Ukraine "for outstanding personal merits in defending the state sovereignty and territorial integrity of Ukraine".

In May 2024 he left military service and was appointed Ukraine's Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary to the United Kingdom, taking up the post in London in July. In September 2026 he became the first Ukrainian diplomat to receive the Freedom of the City of London, an honour that goes back to the Middle Ages. As of October 2026 Zaluzhnyi is Ukraine's ambassador to the UK.

## Why it matters today

Zaluzhnyi took over the army a few months before the full-scale invasion and led it through the first and hardest years of the war. His essay made the case that courage alone doesn't win a modern war: to move forward you need technology, control of the air and new ways through minefields. His path from platoon commander to ambassador shows that you can serve your country in the trenches and at the negotiating table. And that it's worth learning at every stage, even with a general's stars on your shoulders.
