## Commander of the “flying BMP”

Mykhailo Drapatyi was born in 1982 in Kamianets-Podilskyi, in the Khmelnytskyi region. He graduated from the Kharkiv Institute of Tank Forces in 2004 and began his service with the 72nd Separate Mechanized Brigade.

In May 2014, when pro-Russian militants seized Mariupol, Drapatyi commanded the brigade's 2nd battalion. Footage of his armored vehicle ramming and flying over the militants' barricade at speed went around the world, earning him the nickname “commander of the flying BMP”. That summer his strike group broke out of the Izvaryne pocket near the Russian border, fighting its way over 160 km and bringing out about 260 soldiers and more than 30 vehicles.

## From Kherson to Kharkiv

After the full-scale invasion in 2022, Drapatyi took command of the “Kherson” operational grouping, which liberated the city of Kherson that autumn. In 2024, when Russia launched a new offensive on the Kharkiv region, he was put in charge of that front, and later of the Luhansk front too.

On 29 November 2024 Drapatyi became commander of Ukraine's Ground Forces, the country's largest branch of the military. Half a year later, on 1 June 2025, a missile strike on a training ground killed 12 soldiers. Drapatyi publicly admitted that as commander he had failed to ensure his orders on troop safety were carried out, and resigned. He was not pushed out of the service: two days later, on 3 June 2025, he became commander of Ukraine's Joint Forces, while Robert [“Madyar” Brovdi](/en/people/robert-brovdi/) took over the newly formed Unmanned Systems Forces.

## Commander-in-Chief

On 22 July 2026 President Volodymyr Zelenskyy appointed Mykhailo Drapatyi Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of Ukraine, replacing Oleksandr Syrskyi. Drapatyi thanked the president for his trust and called wartime service “absolute responsibility”.

## Debates and assessments

Soon after the appointment, Ukrainian and Russian media circulated an excerpt from a 2023 interview in which Drapatyi had called some Donbas residents “envious, lazy migrants and criminals” and criticized the lack of Ukrainianization of the region since 1991. Russian propaganda used the remarks to brand the new commander-in-chief as hostile to Donbas Ukrainians; Ukrainian outlets covered it as an old, controversial comment unrelated to his combat record or decisions in office.

## Why it matters today

Drapatyi is cited as part of a new generation of Ukrainian generals shaped by combat since 2014 rather than Soviet-era training. His path from battalion commander to Commander-in-Chief shows how the war has reshaped the leadership of Ukraine’s armed forces. When a missile strike on a training ground killed 12 soldiers, he publicly took responsibility as commander, a reminder that a commander answers for the safety of his troops, not only for results. He has called wartime service “absolute responsibility”.
