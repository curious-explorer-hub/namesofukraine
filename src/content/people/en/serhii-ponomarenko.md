## Six against twenty

Imagine six tanks moving on a village where more than twenty enemy tanks lie hidden. That is what happened in March 2022 near Topolske in the Kharkiv region. The Ukrainian company was led by Captain Serhii Ponomarenko, a civilian only months before.

## From the shop floor to “Zvirobii”

Serhii was born in 1983 in the village of Dvirkivshchyna in the Kyiv region. As a student at the agrarian university in Kyiv, he trained in the military department as a tank unit commander. But he chose civilian life: in 2014 he was managing a store for a large supermarket chain.

In August 2014 he volunteered for the army. With the 3rd Separate Tank Battalion “Zvirobii” he fought near Mariupol, Lysychansk and Popasna, and stood out in the battles for the Butivka-Donetska mine and Marinka. In 2015 he received the Order of Bohdan Khmelnytskyi. He later served in the 3rd Separate Tank Brigade, and in November 2021 he left the army and went into business.

## Back in the ranks

The civilian job lasted just six weeks. On 26 February 2022, two days after the full-scale invasion began, Ponomarenko returned to his brigade. On 12 March his tank company arrived near Barvinkove and joined the long battles for the Kharkiv region.

Near Topolske the company attacked together with paratroopers of the 25th Brigade. Scouts had reported only a small enemy force; in battle it turned out to be far larger. Nine Russian tanks were confirmed destroyed by sight. When one Ukrainian tank was hit, the commander's tank covered it with fire, the vehicle was pulled off the battlefield and its crew was taken to safety. On 2 April 2022 Captain Ponomarenko was made a Hero of Ukraine. He says that battle wasn't special: in every fight you give everything you've got.

Soon afterwards he became a deputy battalion commander. As of March 2025, Ponomarenko was deputy commander of the 9th Battalion of the 3rd Separate Iron Tank Brigade. He argues that veterans with combat experience should be the ones training new soldiers.

## Why it matters today

Twice Serhii Ponomarenko chose to go back to the army himself: in 2014, and two days after the full-scale war began. His company showed that a smaller force can stop a bigger one when the crews have the skill and the will to fight, and the commander doesn't leave his own behind. He says he counts the company's achievements, not his own. And he reminds everyone that the future Ukraine has to be built at home, with our own hands.
