## Who he was

Ivan Piddubnyi was born in 1871 into a peasant family in the village of Krasenivka in the Poltava region (now Cherkasy Oblast). His parents and his six younger brothers and sisters were also very strong. In 1893 Ivan became a loader in the port of Sevastopol and later moved to Feodosia. There he first entered the circus ring as an amateur, lifting kettlebells and belt wrestling. From 1898 he performed as a professional.

## His story

Piddubnyi toured Kyiv, Odesa, Katerynoslav (now Dnipro), other cities of the Russian Empire and abroad. In the early 20th century he was considered the world record holder in barbell exercises. He won many commercial international tournaments in classical (Greco-Roman) wrestling. Some of them, in 1904–1909, were billed as world championships, although they were not official. Posters called him the “Champion of Champions.” In Hamburg he also beat opponents in serious bouts without an audience, held under special rules of fair wrestling, the so-called “Hamburg score.”

In 1910–1913 Piddubnyi put money and work into a farm in his home region. In the mid-1920s he performed in the United States, where he quickly mastered freestyle wrestling, a style new to him, and won the title of best wrestler in it. Later he settled in the town of Yeysk (now Krasnodar Krai, Russia). In old age he lived in poverty and faced harassment from the authorities, in part because he openly stressed his Ukrainian identity. He lived through the Nazi occupation during the Second World War. He died in 1949.

## Why it matters today

Piddubnyi is one of the iconic figures in the history of physical culture. His path from port loader to star of the world’s arenas shows what strength, hard training and fair competition can achieve.

## Interesting facts

- When he was already over 50, he won prizes in best-physique contests in the USSR and the United States.
