## How a port loader became the “Champion of Champions”

Ivan Piddubnyi was born in 1871 into a peasant family in the village of Krasenivka in the Poltava region (now Cherkasy Oblast). Strength ran in the family: his parents and his six younger brothers and sisters were all very strong. In 1893 Ivan became a loader in the port of Sevastopol and later moved to Feodosia. There he first stepped into the circus ring as an amateur, lifting kettlebells and belt wrestling. From 1898 he performed as a professional.

## Known as unbeatable

Piddubnyi toured Kyiv, Odesa, Katerynoslav (now Dnipro) and cities abroad. In the early 20th century he was considered the world record holder in barbell exercises. He won many commercial international tournaments in classical wrestling; some of them, in 1904–1909, were billed as world championships, although they were not official. Posters called him the “Champion of Champions”. He also won by the “Hamburg score”, in serious bouts fought under rules of fair wrestling.

In the mid-1920s Piddubnyi performed in the United States. There he quickly mastered freestyle wrestling, a style new to him, and won the title of best wrestler in it. At 56 he also won a men’s beauty contest in the United States.

## Old age in Yeysk

Later Piddubnyi settled in the town of Yeysk (now Krasnodar Krai, Russia). In old age he lived in poverty and faced harassment from the authorities, in part because he openly stressed his Ukrainian identity. He lived through the Nazi occupation and died in 1949.

## Why it matters today

Piddubnyi is one of the iconic figures in the history of physical culture. His path from port loader to star of the world’s arenas shows what strength, training and fair competition can achieve.
