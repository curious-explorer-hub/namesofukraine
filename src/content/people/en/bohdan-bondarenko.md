## 2.42 metres over New York

How many people in history have high-jumped higher than 2.42 metres? Just two. On 14 June 2014, at a Diamond League meeting in New York, Bohdan Bondarenko cleared exactly that height. It set a Ukrainian record and equalled the European one. As of October 2026, only Cuba's Javier Sotomayor (2.45 m) and Qatar's Mutaz Essa Barshim (2.43 m) have jumped higher.

## A decathlete's son

Bohdan Bondarenko was born on 30 August 1989 in Kharkiv. His father was a decathlete who later became his son's coach. Bohdan took up athletics at 13. In 2008, in Bydgoszcz, Poland, he won the World Junior Championships, and in 2011 he won the Summer Universiade.

## The year he took off

In 2013 Bondarenko won the Diamond League, the season-long series of athletics' top meetings. In Lausanne he jumped 2.41 metres, the first man of the 21st century to do so. That summer, at the World Championships in Moscow, he cleared 2.41 again, the best mark in the world that season, took the title and made three attempts at Sotomayor's world record. European Athletics named him European Athlete of the Year.

## Zurich, Beijing, Rio

In 2014 Bondarenko became European champion in Zurich, and in 2015 he took silver at the World Championships in Beijing. At the 2016 Olympic Games in Rio de Janeiro he won bronze with 2.33 metres. It was the only medal Ukraine's track and field athletes won at those Games.

## Injury and comeback

At the 2017 World Championships a foot injury left him short of his best. Ankle surgery and a long rehabilitation followed. He returned to competition in the summer of 2019, but injuries ended his international career, and he turned mostly to events in Ukraine. As of October 2026 he has not left the high jump: in July 2026 he cleared 2.20 metres in Kropyvnytskyi.

## Why it matters today

Bondarenko flew higher than almost anyone in the history of his event, and he came up through athletics in Kharkiv, coached by his father. His jumps made the world talk about a Ukrainian as a man who could go after Sotomayor's record. His bronze in Rio showed that even in a hard year for the team, one athlete can carry Ukraine's flag onto the podium. And his comeback after surgery is a reminder that an athlete's strength lies not only in records but in getting up again.
