## A name in the rulebook

Open the international rules that women’s artistic gymnastics is judged by, as of October 2026. In the floor exercise section you’ll find the name Podkopayeva: it’s what the tucked double front somersault, and its version with a half twist, are called. Another skill with her name is among the vaults. This is how gymnastics honours whoever first performs a hard skill at a major competition.

## A girl from Donetsk

Lilia Podkopayeva was born on 15 August 1978 in Donetsk and took up gymnastics at five. She became a star just as Ukraine began competing in world sport as a team of its own. In 1994 she won world silver on the beam and the European floor title. And in 1995, in the Japanese city of Sabae, she became world all-around champion.

## An injury before Atlanta

Early in 1996, months before the Olympics, she was badly hurt when she fell from the beam in training. Yet she came back, and that May she won the European all-around title, plus gold on the uneven bars and floor.

## Two golds at 17

In the summer of 1996, at the Atlanta Olympics, 17-year-old Podkopayeva won the all-around, the biggest title in women’s gymnastics. Then she became Olympic floor champion and took silver on the beam. The last woman to win the world and Olympic all-around titles back to back before her was Lyudmila Turishcheva, more than twenty years earlier.

Podkopayeva didn’t plan to stop, but injuries kept her out for two seasons, and in 1998 she retired. She stayed in gymnastics as a judge and coach and set up her own tournament in Kyiv. She also became a UN Goodwill Ambassador on HIV/AIDS in Ukraine.

## Why it matters

Podkopayeva was one of the first world stars of independent Ukraine’s sport. In 2008 she was inducted into the International Gymnastics Hall of Fame, and her name is still in the rules used to judge gymnasts all over the world.
