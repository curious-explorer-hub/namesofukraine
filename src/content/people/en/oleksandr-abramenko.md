## A twelve-year staircase

27, 24, 6. Those were Oleksandr Abramenko's places at his first three Olympics, in 2006, 2010 and 2014. Then, on 18 February 2018, in PyeongChang, South Korea, at his fourth, he came first. The judges scored his aerials jump at 128.51 points. China's Jia Zongyang, who tried the same jump, fell less than half a point short of gold.

It was only the third Winter Olympic gold in Ukraine's history. And it was the first time a Ukrainian man had won a Winter Olympic medal of any colour: until then, only women had made it onto the podium.

## From football to the ramp

Oleksandr Abramenko was born on 4 May 1988 in Pervomaiskyi, Kharkiv Oblast, and competed for Mykolaiv. His father first took him to football, but the boy felt it "wasn't for me". When he was ten, he was offered freestyle skiing, and he said yes.

## A globe and an injury

In 2016 Abramenko won the Aerials World Cup and the Small Crystal Globe that goes with it. That same summer, training on water, he damaged the ligaments in his knee and missed the whole of 2017. PyeongChang was his comeback from a serious injury.

## Silver in Beijing

In 2019 Abramenko took silver at the World Championships. At the 2022 Olympics in Beijing, his fifth, he carried Ukraine's flag at the opening ceremony and went on to win silver. It was Ukraine's only medal of those Games.

## Coaching the national team

After he stopped competing, Abramenko joined the coaching staff of Ukraine's national freestyle team: as of May 2026 he had been there for three years. Freestyle skiing is still a young sport in Ukraine, he says, but he believes the current generation can reach the top step of the Olympic podium.

## Debates and assessments

After his 2018 win Abramenko hugged the bronze medallist, Russia's Ilya Burov, on the podium, and the photo caused a scandal in Ukraine. In Beijing in 2022 Burov, bronze medallist again, hugged Abramenko from behind as he stood with the Ukrainian flag. Back home, Abramenko said: "I didn't hug anyone this time. I just congratulated him in a sporting way, one athlete to another, that's all."

## Why it matters today

Abramenko was the first Ukrainian man to win a Winter Olympic medal, and he did it in freestyle skiing, a sport still young in Ukraine. His road from 27th place to gold shows that the top is rarely reached at the first attempt, but it can be reached by anyone who doesn't stop. His two Olympic medals, at two different Games, raised the blue and yellow flag among the world's best aerial skiers. Now he passes what he knows on to the next generation of Ukrainian freestylers.
