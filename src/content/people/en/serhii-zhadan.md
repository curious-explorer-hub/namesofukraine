## When they told him to kneel

Serhii Zhadan was born on 23 August 1974 in Starobilsk, in the Luhansk region. He graduated from Kharkiv Pedagogical University in 1996 and soon became one of the sharpest new voices in Ukrainian writing: ironic, blunt, and attentive to ordinary people living through unsettled times.

On 1 March 2014, during a pro-Russian rally outside the Kharkiv regional administration building, militants beat Zhadan and demanded he kneel. He refused. The moment became a kind of marker for the stance the Kharkiv writer would keep for years afterward.

## The city he writes about

Zhadan writes about Donbas and the Kharkiv region without varnish: miners, smugglers, railway workers, people caught up in war. His novels Voroshilovgrad (2010), Mesopotamia (2014) and The Orphanage (2017) won him Ukrainian and international prizes, including the Angelus Award (2015) and the Vasyl Stus Prize (2017), and were translated into dozens of languages. Alongside the books, he fronts his own band, Zhadan and the Dogs, setting his lyrics to ska.

2022 brought him two major international honours at once, the Peace Prize of the German Book Trade and the Hannah Arendt Prize. In 2025 he became the first Ukrainian writer to win the Austrian State Prize for European Literature.

## From volunteer to soldier

Since 2014 Zhadan has carried humanitarian aid to the front and founded a charitable foundation under his own name. After the full-scale invasion began in 2022 he stayed in a Kharkiv under constant bombardment, and in June 2024 he enlisted in the 13th National Guard Brigade "Khartiia" - the same unit he had spent years supporting from the stage and through charity tours.

## Why it matters today

Zhadan showed that a writer can stay a writer and still refuse to look away from his city's troubles. His war writing is now read in places that had barely heard of Kharkiv before.
