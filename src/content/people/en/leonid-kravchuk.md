## The ideologist from a Volhynian village

Leonid Kravchuk was born in 1934 into a peasant family in the village of Velykyi Zhytyn (now in the Rivne region). In 1958 he graduated from Kyiv University as an economist and went to Chernivtsi, where he taught and worked in Party propaganda. A career in the Communist Party of Ukraine followed: in 1988 he became head of its Central Committee’s ideology department, and in 1990 its second secretary.

## Between Moscow and Kyiv

In July 1990 Kravchuk was elected chairman of the Verkhovna Rada. When hardliners in Moscow tried to oust Gorbachev on 19 August 1991, General Varennikov flew to Kyiv and demanded a state of emergency. Kravchuk refused, saying only the Verkhovna Rada could do that. He was in no hurry to back Yeltsin openly either, and told people to get on with the harvest. Soon after the coup failed he announced he was leaving the Soviet Communist Party.

## The day a state was born

On 24 August 1991 Kravchuk chaired the session that adopted the Act of Declaration of Independence, written by [Levko Lukianenko](/en/people/levko-lukianenko/). By his own account, he held off the vote until he was sure of a large majority. In the end 346 deputies voted for it. On 1 December 1991, 90.3% of voters backed independence in a referendum, and Kravchuk was elected president; [Viacheslav Chornovil](/en/people/viacheslav-chornovil/) came second. A week later Kravchuk and the leaders of Russia and Belarus signed the agreement that put an end to the USSR.

## President of the first years

Kravchuk led the country through its hardest early years: the economy shrank, people grew poorer, and a standoff with parliament ended in early elections in 1994. He lost them to Leonid Kuchma. He went on to serve as a member of parliament, and in 2020 he headed Ukraine’s delegation to the talks on the war in Donbas. He died on 10 May 2022.

## Debates and assessments

Scholars write that as Party ideologist Kravchuk put obstacles in Rukh’s way, and in August 1991 democrats reproached him for waiting instead of clearly condemning the coup. He said he first had to establish the facts. The historian Volodymyr Viatrovych criticises the trilateral agreements Kravchuk signed in January 1994: the nuclear weapons went to Russia without financial compensation or reliable security guarantees. He is also faulted for feeding inflation with credits, weakening local self-government and holding back the growth of political parties. In 1998 he joined the Social Democratic Party of Ukraine (united), and in 2004 he backed Viktor Yanukovych. The overall verdict, though, is that his presidency is remembered as laying the foundation of Ukraine’s independent statehood.

## Why it matters today

Kravchuk was a man of the Soviet system who, at the decisive moment, sided with independence. His story shows how complicated the road from Soviet Ukraine to Ukraine was.
