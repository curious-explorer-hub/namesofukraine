## 146 days without a photo

How many photos are left after 146 days of fighting? For Andrii Kovalchuk, who led the defence of Luhansk airport, not one. In the spring and summer of 2014 Ukrainian paratroopers held the airfield, and no journalists were allowed in: the road from the main Ukrainian forces was under constant fire. "To be in photos you have to be neat and clean, and at the airport we were unwashed and unshaven," he recalled.

## From a village in Volyn

Andrii Kovalchuk was born on 28 April 1974 in the village of Shchytyn in Volyn. In 1997 he graduated from the Kharkiv tank command school. Later he served as a peacekeeper: in Kosovo in 2005–2006, and in 2011 as chief of staff of a helicopter unit with the UN mission in Liberia.

## Luhansk airport

In April 2014 Colonel Kovalchuk, chief of staff of the 80th Separate Airmobile Brigade, went east. His brigade freed Sloviansk, Mykolaivka and Shchastia. In the spring the paratroopers took Luhansk airport under guard, and on 13 July Kovalchuk himself led one of the detachments that fought their way through to the encircled airfield.

He was wounded in the shoulder that same day, but he did not go to hospital. "How could you leave them? They're like your own children by then," the general explained. In August he was made commander of the brigade, and the paratroopers held the airfield until the end of the summer.

In 2016 Kovalchuk was made a Hero of Ukraine and promoted to major general.

## The counteroffensive in the south

In August 2021 Kovalchuk took over Operational Command South. After the full-scale invasion began, he was given the task of leading the counteroffensive in the Kherson region. His aim was not only to free the territory but to cut off the Russian forces on the right bank of the Dnipro so that they could not get out.

The offensive was hard going. Kovalchuk insisted that the Russians would retreat; it was only a matter of time. But according to press reports, Kyiv decided the offensive was moving too slowly and handed command of the operation to Brigadier General Oleksandr Tarnavskyi; the change was not announced publicly. In November 2022 Ukrainian forces took back Kherson.

In April 2024 the general became head of the Military Academy in Odesa; as of June 2026 he was its head.

## Why it matters today

From the first weeks of the war in 2014, Kovalchuk showed that a commander stays with his people, even when wounded. His paratroopers spent a whole summer keeping a strategically important airfield out of enemy hands. Later he led the troops in the south, where Ukraine took back Kherson. At the academy he passes this experience on to the people who will become officers. His story is a reminder that responsibility for people counts for more than rank or medals.
