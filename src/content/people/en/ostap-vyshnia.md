## Who he was

Ostap Vyshnia was the pen name of Pavlo Hubenko. He was born on the Chechva farmstead near Hrun in the Poltava region (now Sumy Oblast). In 1907 he graduated from the Kyiv military medical assistants’ school and worked as a medical assistant at the South-Western Railway hospital in Kyiv. In 1919, in Kamianets-Podilskyi, where the government of the Ukrainian People’s Republic was then based, he began publishing in the Ukrainian press under the pen name P. Hrunskyi. In 1920 he was imprisoned by the Bolshevik security service; Vasyl Blakytnyi, editor of the newspaper Visti VUTsVK, helped get him released.

## His story

From 1921, as Ostap Vyshnia, he published in Kharkiv periodicals such as Visti VUTsVK, Selianska Pravda and Chervonyi Perets. His feuilletons and “smiles” about the village, language and culture, Ukrainization and Russian great-power chauvinism had enormous print runs. Together with Maik Yohansen and [Mykola Khvylovy](/en/people/mykola-khvylovyi/) he wrote the revue Hello, on Wave 477!, staged by the Berezil theater in 1929.

From the late 1920s official critics began to hound the writer. On 25 December 1933 he was groundlessly arrested on charges of nationalism and plotting terrorism and sentenced to ten years in labor camps. He served his sentence in the North, in Ukhta (Komi ASSR). Released in 1943, he lived in Kyiv from 1944 and returned to literature, completing his cycle Hunting Smiles. Some of his works from this period, including pamphlets against “Ukrainian bourgeois nationalists”, followed official Party guidelines. He was rehabilitated in 1955 and died in Kyiv in 1956.

## Why it matters today

Ostap Vyshnia is a classic of Ukrainian humor whose works came out in enormous print runs. His fate shows how the Soviet regime broke the lives of artists: one of the most popular writers of his day spent ten years in labor camps.

## Interesting facts

- He opens his humorous “My Autobiography” by saying he was born “on 1 November (Old Style) 1889”, and that for years his mother claimed he had been “pulled out of the well while the cow Oryshka was being watered”.
