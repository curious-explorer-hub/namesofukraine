## Videos from an armoured vehicle

In 2014 many Ukrainians first saw the war in the Donbas through the videos of a blogger called Valerii Ananiev. He filmed the paratroopers of the 25th Separate Airborne Brigade, where he himself served as the driver of an airborne fighting vehicle. Valerii fought near Kramatorsk, Sloviansk, Shakhtarsk and Dobropillia. In 2016 he was wounded, and that same year he left the service for health reasons.

## A novel from the war

In 2018 Valerii’s debut novel Tracks on the Road came out, about what he had been through in the Donbas. He wrote it over several years and signed it Valerii Ananiev. The first print run was 10,000 copies, and within the first month about 5,000 people had read the book.

## A brigade meant to be a model

After the full-scale invasion in 2022 Valerii Markus went back to the army. Together with the officer Ivan Shalamaha he was asked to form a battalion. It grew into an assault regiment and then into the 47th Separate Mechanized Brigade Magura, with Markus as its chief sergeant. In his words, the brigade was meant to be a model for the whole army. In July 2023 he asked to be moved to a lower post, writing that he categorically disagreed with decisions on how the brigade was used and developed. Another sergeant took his place, and Markus, according to the brigade, went on serving in other units.

## “Dream”

In December 2024, after a year and a half of silence, Markus posted a series of videos on YouTube. The first was called Dream, followed by Struggle, Compromise, Acceptance and Duty. The first video passed 700,000 views within a day. Markus told how Magura had been built and sharply criticised its commanders, above all Ivan Shalamaha, with whom he had once set up the battalion. In his view, the idea of the brigade was ruined by “human envy, immaturity and incompetence”.

## Debates and assessments

Markus’s videos sparked a heated debate. Some felt he had raised questions that matter to the whole army. The MP Inna Sovsun pointed to gaps in the training of new recruits, and the veteran Oleh Symoroz called it a systemic problem that commanders are appointed for loyalty rather than competence. At the same time Symoroz reproached Markus for turning individual commanders into “fake heroes” and for not taking responsibility for the brigade himself. The 47th Brigade’s press service criticised the videos: it asked why Markus had kept silent for a year and a half and said such appeals hurt morale, while admitting that incompetent officers are a real problem. The political scientist Yevhen Mahda saw the videos as a sign of elections to come and warned against the military becoming a screen for politicians. Markus denied that he was preparing for a political career.

## Why it matters today

Markus’s story shows how an army changes during a big war: soldiers themselves help build it, and arguments about commanders, training and responsibility happen in the open, in front of millions of viewers.
