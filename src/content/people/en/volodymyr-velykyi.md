## Who he was

Volodymyr was the son of Prince Sviatoslav and Malusha, the housekeeper of [Princess Olha](/en/people/kniahynia-olha/). The exact date and place of his birth are unknown; around 958 is usually given. He first ruled in Novgorod, and around 980, after defeating his brother Yaropolk in a war between the princes, he became Grand Prince of Kyiv.

## His story

Early in his reign, Volodymyr tried to unite the state around the pagan gods, but later chose Byzantine-rite Christianity. The Christianization of Rus’ is traditionally dated to 988; historians place these events in 987–990. Volodymyr was baptized himself and made Christianity the state religion. This opened Kyiv to writing, education, stone architecture and close ties with Byzantium and Europe.

Volodymyr expanded and strengthened the state, built forts to defend it against the nomadic Pechenegs, and minted the first coins of his own. The Church venerates him as a saint, Equal to the Apostles.

## Debates and assessments

Modern Russia counts Volodymyr as part of its own history and calls him “Vladimir.” For Ukraine, he is a ruler of the Kyivan state, whose center was in Kyiv, and his sign, the trident, is today the state coat of arms of Ukraine.

## Why it matters today

Volodymyr’s choice set the cultural path of Rus’ for centuries to come. Ukrainian Statehood Day is marked on July 15, the day on which Volodymyr the Great is commemorated. His portrait is on the 1 hryvnia banknote.
