## From teacher to academician

Myroslav Popovych was born in 1930 in Zhytomyr. In 1953 he graduated from the philosophy faculty of Kyiv University and went to work in Zolotyi Potik in the Ternopil region, where for three years he was the school principal and a history teacher. In 1956 he began graduate studies at the Institute of Philosophy in Kyiv, and the institute remained his home for the rest of his life.

## The logic of language and the worldview of the ancient Slavs

How do you work out what the words of science really mean? Popovych started as a logician, studying the language of science and semantics: how signs are tied to their meanings. In 1967 he earned his doctorate, and in 1969 he became head of the department for the logic of scientific knowledge. Later he turned to the history of ideas: he wrote about Gogol and Dostoevsky, and in 1985 he published The Worldview of the Ancient Slavs.

## A book about Skovoroda

In 1984 the book Hryhorii Skovoroda came out, written by Popovych together with the poet [Ivan Drach](/en/people/ivan-drach/) and the philosopher Serhii Krymskyi. Many years later Popovych became director of the [Hryhorii Skovoroda](/en/people/hryhorii-skovoroda/) Institute of Philosophy of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine, and he led it until his death.

## The history of culture, and freedom

In 2001 Popovych received the Shevchenko Prize for An Outline of the History of Ukrainian Culture. His book The Red Century (2005) is about the 20th century, and The Philosophy of Freedom came out in 2018. After 1989 he sat on the leadership of the People’s Movement of Ukraine (Rukh), and later joined the First of December Initiative Group, whose founders included [Liubomyr Huzar](/en/people/liubomyr-huzar/).

## Why it matters today

Myroslav Popovych died in Kyiv on 10 February 2018. A few days later the president posthumously awarded him the Order of Freedom for strengthening Ukraine’s independence, developing democracy and many years of scholarly and public work. His main themes, the history of Ukrainian culture and human freedom, still matter to Ukraine today.
