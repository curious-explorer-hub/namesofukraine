## From dishwasher to patron

Did you know that the man who gave more than 16 million dollars to Ukrainian scholarship started out in Canada washing dishes in a restaurant? Petro Yatsyk made his fortune in construction, but he didn’t spend it on himself: he lived very modestly.

## The boy who walked west

Yatsyk was born in 1921 into a poor peasant family in the village now called Verkhnie Synovydne, in the Lviv region. His father died early, and the farm fell to him as the eldest son. During the Second World War he was a liaison of the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA), and in 1944, as Soviet troops approached, he set off west on foot. In 1949 he moved to Canada, so he spent his whole adult life in the diaspora.

## A million dollars, one condition

Yatsyk’s construction firm built whole neighbourhoods in Toronto and across Ontario. He shared what he earned with scholarship: he supported the Ukrainian institute at Harvard, the Encyclopedia of Ukraine, and Ukrainian studies programmes in Toronto, London and New York. In 1989 he gave a million dollars for a centre for Ukrainian historical research in Edmonton, on one condition: an English translation of the History of Ukraine-Rus’ by [Mykhailo Hrushevskyi](/en/people/mykhailo-hrushevskyi/), whom he admired.

Yatsyk urged other wealthy Ukrainians to do the same and became the first president of the League of Ukrainian Patrons in Kyiv. With his support the International Ukrainian Language Competition was launched, and after his death in 2001 it was named after him.

## Why it matters today

Yatsyk showed that diaspora money can work for Ukrainian scholarship and the Ukrainian language. Millions of school and university students from more than 20 countries have taken part in the competition that bears his name.
