## World champion at 18

Can you become world champion at just 18? Ruslan Ponomariov did. In 2002 he won the World Championship of FIDE, the International Chess Federation, and became the youngest winner of the event in its history. The final was an all-Ukrainian affair: Ponomariov beat Vasyl Ivanchuk 4½–2½.

## A prodigy from Horlivka

Ruslan Ponomariov was born on 11 October 1983 in Horlivka, in the Donetsk region. In 1998, still a teenager, he became the youngest grandmaster in the world. Others later broke the record, but by then he was already among the world's chess elite.

## A crown split in two

Why "FIDE champion" and not simply "world champion"? From 1993 to 2006 the chess crown was split: FIDE ran its own championships, while a separate "classical" line of champions existed alongside, held from 2000 by Vladimir Kramnik. Ponomariov held the FIDE title from 2002 to 2004. FIDE championships in those years were knockout events, and the weight of such a title was much debated. Ponomariov himself has explained more than once that he simply played by the rules and won fair and square.

## Gold for Ukraine

Knockouts became his strength: Ponomariov reached the World Cup final twice more, in 2005 and 2009. With the Ukrainian team he won the Chess Olympiad twice, in 2004 in Calvià, Spain, and in 2010. In July 2011 his rating reached 2764, the highest of his career.

## Chess to help

In 2022, during Russia's war against Ukraine, Ponomariov coordinated the book From Ukraine with Love for Chess. The games in it were chosen and annotated by the Ukrainian players themselves, among them Vasyl Ivanchuk and the sisters Anna and Mariya Muzychuk. All proceeds go to Ukrainian charities.

## Why it matters today

A boy from Horlivka proved that you can reach the top of world chess before you turn twenty. In his final against Ivanchuk two Ukrainians fought for the FIDE title, and the world saw how strong Ukrainian chess is. The Olympiad golds of 2004 and 2010 showed that Ukraine can beat the strongest chess nations. And a book put together in wartime reminds anyone that even a favourite game can become a way to help your country.
