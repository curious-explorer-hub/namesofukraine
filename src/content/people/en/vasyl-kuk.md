## Did you know the last commander-in-chief of the UPA lived to see Ukraine become independent?

Vasyl Kuk was born on 11 January 1913 in the village of Krasne near Zolochiv, in what is now the Lviv region. Still at school, he joined the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists; Polish police arrested him twice in 1933 for it. He went underground in the 1930s, and during the war he organized the OUN's anti-Nazi resistance in the Dnipropetrovsk region. From 1943 he commanded UPA-South, one of the insurgent army's regional groupings.

## Taking over from Shukhevych

[Roman Shukhevych](/en/people/roman-shukhevych/), the UPA's commander-in-chief, was killed on 5 March 1950. Kuk took his place, leading both the insurgent army and the OUN's leadership in Ukraine, and in 1952 was given the UPA's highest rank, general-khorunzhyi. By then he commanded a small, exhausted underground that had already lost most of its fighters.

On 23 May 1954, KGB officers captured him alive. He spent the next six years in investigative prisons in Kyiv and Moscow, held without trial or sentence.

## Debates and assessments

Kuk was released under an amnesty in 1960. Soon afterward, an open letter appeared under his name, calling on the remaining underground to lay down arms and declaring the struggle hopeless. Historians still disagree about it: some read it as the result of years of KGB pressure on a man held in isolation without trial; others see a genuine change of mind after nearly two decades of fighting and defeat. Kuk himself never publicly explained how the letter came about. Know Your Own presents both readings without taking a side.

## How he is remembered

After his release, Kuk trained as a historian and worked at research institutions in Kyiv until 1972, studying among other things the music of the composer Artemii Vedel, until renewed KGB scrutiny cost him his position. When Ukraine became independent in 1991, Kuk - by then in his eighties - spent his remaining years preserving the memory of the independence underground, sharing his testimony with historians and journalists and helping prepare the Litopys UPA document series for publication. He died in Kyiv on 9 September 2007 and, as he had asked, was buried in his native Krasne.
