## A country cut in two

In 1667 Muscovy and the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth signed the Truce of Andrusovo and divided Ukraine: the Left Bank of the Dnipro went to the tsar, the Right Bank to the king. Petro Doroshenko, hetman of the Right Bank, refused to accept it. His main aim was to restore the Cossack state on both banks of the river.

## A Cossack from Chyhyryn

Doroshenko was born in 1627 in Chyhyryn, into a Cossack family. He carried out diplomatic missions for [Bohdan Khmelnytskyi](/en/people/bohdan-khmelnytskyi/), and after Khmelnytskyi’s death he backed Hetman [Ivan Vyhovskyi](/en/people/ivan-vyhovskyi/) and signed the Treaty of Hadiach. In 1665 the Cossack officers chose Doroshenko as hetman of Right-Bank Ukraine.

## Hetman of both banks

In 1666 he defeated a Polish army at Brailiv. In 1668 an uprising against Moscow broke out on the Left Bank, and on 8 June Doroshenko was proclaimed hetman of all Ukraine. The unity didn’t last: a Polish offensive forced him back to the Right Bank, and another hetman took over the Left. The Zaporozhian Cossacks worked against him too; their otaman [Ivan Sirko](/en/people/ivan-sirko/) stood in his way more than once.

Looking for a strong ally, the hetman turned to the Ottoman Empire. In 1669 a Cossack council in Korsun approved the alliance. Under its terms, the Turks and Tatars were not to meddle in the election of the hetman, nor make peace with Poland or Moscow without his consent. In 1672 Doroshenko beat the Poles at Batih, and then, together with the army of Sultan Mehmed IV, took the fortress of Kamianets.

## Giving up the mace

The wars laid waste to the Right Bank, and people stopped supporting the hetman. In 1676, besieged in Chyhyryn, Doroshenko surrendered his mace, the hetman’s symbol of office, to Ivan Samoilovych, hetman of the Left Bank. He was taken to Moscow and never returned home. Doroshenko died in 1698 in the village of Yaropolche.

## Debates and assessments

The Ottoman alliance is Doroshenko’s most disputed decision. Some scholars explain it as an effort to reunite the Ukrainian lands within an independent state. At the same time, as others note, the idea was unpopular in Ukraine. Ottoman garrisons on the Right Bank plundered the population and carried people off into captivity, and many fled to the Left Bank.

## Why he is remembered

Doroshenko lost, but he never gave up the goal he set at the start: one Ukraine on both banks of the Dnipro. In 2017 the 1st Operational Brigade of the National Guard of Ukraine was named after him.
