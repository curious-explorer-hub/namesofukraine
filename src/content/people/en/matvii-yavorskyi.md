## The Galician who held science "in his fist"

How did a Marxist historian who ran Soviet scholarship in Ukraine end up on the Solovets Islands among "Ukrainian nationalists"? Matvii Yavorskyi was born in 1885 into a peasant family in the Galician village of Korchmyn. He graduated in law from Lviv University, fought in the Austrian army in the First World War and then served in the Ukrainian Galician Army. He was among its officers who prepared the army's switch to the Bolshevik side, and in 1920 he joined the Bolshevik party.

## The first Marxist historian

In Kharkiv, then the capital of Soviet Ukraine, Yavorskyi became one of the heads of the office that oversaw the All-Ukrainian Academy of Sciences (VUAN). "He holds all of Ukrainian science in his fist. Poor science!" [Serhii Yefremov](/en/people/serhii-yefremov/) wrote wryly in his diary in 1925. Yavorskyi's books told Ukraine's past as a history of class struggle. The historian Dmytro Bahalii called him the first Marxist historian of Ukraine, though other scholars found his work dogmatic.

## Striking at Hrushevskyi's school

At the turn of 1928 and 1929, at a conference of Marxist historians in Moscow, Yavorskyi spoke against "anti-Marxist trends" in Ukrainian historical scholarship. His main target was the school of [Mykhailo Hrushevskyi](/en/people/mykhailo-hrushevskyi/), accused of a theory of a "classless" Ukrainian nation. Yet speakers in the debate argued that Yavorskyi's own concept was no different from Hrushevskyi's.

## From "Yavorshchyna" to Sandarmokh

Yavorskyi saw Ukraine's history as a process of its own, with the Ukrainian people at its core. In 1929 the Moscow historian Pavel Gorin called one of his books "a non-Bolshevik work", and a campaign against "Yavorshchyna" began. That same year Yavorskyi was elected a full member of the VUAN, and in the autumn he publicly admitted his "mistakes". It did not help: in 1930 he was expelled from the party, and a congress of the Communist Party (Bolsheviks) of Ukraine condemned his "theory" together with the deviations of Oleksandr Shumskyi, [Mykola Khvylovyi](/en/people/mykola-khvylovyi/) and Mykhailo Volobuiev.

In March 1931 Yavorskyi was arrested in Leningrad in a fabricated case and sentenced to six years in the camps. On Solovky, according to his fellow prisoner Semen Pidhainyi, the former Marxist became an implacable enemy of Bolshevism and of everything that came from Moscow. On 3 November 1937 he was shot at Sandarmokh. He was 51. Yavorskyi was rehabilitated in 1989.

## Debates and assessments

Some scholars see Yavorskyi above all as an agent of the party line: he ran scholarship from an administrator's desk and spread the party's label of Hrushevskyi's "theory of classlessness". He has been called a "local Pokrovsky", after the leading Soviet Marxist historian in Moscow. Others note that Yavorskyi tried to present Ukraine's history as national as well as Marxist, and that this is exactly why he was accused of nationalism. The picture is complicated further by the fact that in 1929 he wrote an almost wholly favourable report on Hrushevskyi's work for the elections to the USSR Academy of Sciences.

## Why it matters today

Yavorskyi's fate shows that even in Marxist textbooks Ukraine's history kept emerging as the history of a people of its own. He helped build a system that took away scholars' freedom, and that system destroyed him as soon as he put Ukraine at the centre of his scheme. His path from party historian to Solovky prisoner who turned against that system is a reminder that loyalty is no protection from an empire that cannot tolerate Ukraine's separate identity. And the debates around him teach us to speak honestly about both a person's wrongs and their tragedy.
