## The boy from Chapli

Valerian Pidmohylnyi was born in 1901 in the village of Chapli near Katerynoslav, today’s Dnipro. He went to secondary school in Katerynoslav. His first stories were printed in 1919 by Sich, a Katerynoslav journal, when he was 18.

## The hungry years

During the famine years of 1921–1923 Pidmohylnyi taught Ukrainian at the labour school in Vorzel near Kyiv. He wrote about the famine of those years in the stories “The Dog”, “The Problem of Bread” and “The Son”.

## The City

In Kyiv Pidmohylnyi worked for publishing houses and on the journal Zhyttia i revoliutsiia (Life and Revolution), and in 1924 he helped found the literary group Lanka (from 1926, MARS). In 1928 came his novel The City, about village meeting city in the years of the NEP, the New Economic Policy of the 1920s. Critics attacked his work harshly, The City included, and in 1930 he was dismissed from his editorial post.

## Balzac in Ukrainian

Pidmohylnyi translated Balzac, Diderot, Anatole France, Maupassant, Stendhal, Voltaire and Mérimée into Ukrainian, and edited a 15-volume Balzac and a 25-volume Anatole France. His translations had a marked influence on Ukrainian literature in the 1920s. His last novel, A Little Drama, was serialized in a journal in 1930 but came out as a separate book only in 1956, in Paris.

## Solovki and Sandarmokh

On 8 December 1934 Pidmohylnyi was arrested as a supposed member of a “group of nationalist writers with terrorist leanings”. He was imprisoned in the Solovetsky camp. A special troika of the Leningrad NKVD sentenced him to death. Pidmohylnyi, [Mykola Kulish](/en/people/mykola-kulish/), [Mykola Zerov](/en/people/mykola-zerov/) and [Les Kurbas](/en/people/les-kurbas/) were among the Ukrainian prisoners shot at that time in the Sandarmokh forest in Karelia; Pidmohylnyi was shot on 3 November 1937. He was rehabilitated in 1956.

## Why it matters today

In 1989 The City was published in Ukraine again. Pidmohylnyi’s works have been translated into English, German, Polish and Russian; A Little Drama came out in English in 1972 as A Little Touch of Drama.
