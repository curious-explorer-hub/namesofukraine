## Who he was

Mykola Zerov was born in 1890 in Zinkiv in the Poltava region. He studied at gymnasiums in Okhtyrka and Kyiv and in 1914 graduated from the Faculty of History and Philology of Kyiv University. He taught history and Latin at gymnasiums in Zlatopil and Kyiv and edited the bibliographic journal Knyhar. In the famine year of 1920 he moved to Baryshivka near Kyiv, where until 1923 he taught, translated a great deal and wrote his own poems. From 1923 he was a professor at the Kyiv Institute of People’s Education, and later he headed departments of the history of Ukrainian literature and of translation theory.

## His story

Zerov became the intellectual leader of the Kyiv Neoclassicists, poets who in the 1920s championed classical European values, craftsmanship and taste. He wrote polished sonnets and alexandrines, and his Anthology of Roman Poetry (1920) and the collection Kamena (1924) introduced Ukrainian readers to the finest Roman poetry. As a critic Zerov wrote about [Ivan Franko](/en/people/ivan-franko/), [Lesya Ukrainka](/en/people/lesya-ukrainka/), Panteleimon Kulish, Pavlo Tychyna and Maksym Rylskyi.

In December 1923 he met [Mykola Khvylovy](/en/people/mykola-khvylovyi/), and they soon became allies against “proletarian hackwork”. During the Literary Discussion of 1925–1928, a public debate about the path of Soviet Ukrainian literature, Zerov spoke at a debate on 24 May 1925, supported Khvylovy’s call to learn from cultured Europe and added the slogan “Ad fontes!” (“To the sources!”). Both men were later held guilty for this alliance.

In 1934 Zerov was dismissed from his university post and left for Moscow. On the night of 27–28 April 1935 he was arrested near Moscow and sent to Kyiv for investigation. Under pressure from the interrogators he confessed to membership of a fictitious “counter-revolutionary nationalist organization”, and in 1936 he was sentenced to 10 years in the camps. He served his term on the Solovetsky Islands, where he kept up his literary plans. On 9 October 1937 a special NKVD troika sentenced him to death. Zerov and [Les Kurbas](/en/people/les-kurbas/) were among the Ukrainian prisoners shot at the Sandarmokh forest in Karelia in the Solovetsky executions timed for the 20th anniversary of the Bolshevik coup; Zerov was shot on 3 November 1937. He was rehabilitated in 1958.

## Why it matters today

Zerov and the Neoclassicists showed that Ukrainian poetry could be part of the great European tradition, from Rome to Ronsard’s France. His fate is one of the most tragic pages of the Executed Renaissance, the generation of Ukrainian writers and artists destroyed in the 1930s. His works, long kept silent in the USSR, began returning to readers in the 1960s; a two-volume edition appeared in 1990.
