## Who he was

Vadym Hetman was born in 1935 in the village of Snityn in the Poltava region, into a family of village teachers. In 1956 he graduated from the Kyiv Institute of Finance and Economics and spent almost 20 years in banking, finance and planning bodies in the Zaporizhzhia region. From 1975 he was first deputy head of the State Price Committee of the Ukrainian SSR, and in 1987 he took over Agroprombank, which in 1990 became the joint-stock bank Ukraina.

## His story

In 1990 Hetman was elected to the Verkhovna Rada, Ukraine’s parliament. He co-wrote the laws On the Economic Independence of the Ukrainian SSR, On Prices and Pricing, and On Banks and Banking. From March to December 1992 he chaired the board of the National Bank of Ukraine. In that time the bank began building a stabilization fund for the national currency, set up a single settlement and cash centre, and moved payments with the other post-Soviet states onto correspondent accounts. His name is linked with the making of the hryvnia, whose first paper notes carry his signature, with Ukraine joining the IMF, the World Bank and the EBRD, and with the founding of the Ukrainian Interbank Currency Exchange. In December 1992 Hetman resigned because he disagreed with the official line on the terms of leaving the “rouble zone”.

In 1994 he was elected to parliament a second time. He was deputy chair of the committee on finance and banking, co-chaired the parliamentary group “Independents” and took part in adopting the Constitution of Ukraine in 1996. That year he also became chairman of the Exchange Committee of the Ukrainian Interbank Currency Exchange.

On 22 April 1998 Vadym Hetman was shot dead in the lift of the Kyiv apartment building where he lived. In 2003 the Luhansk Regional Court of Appeal convicted members of a criminal gang behind a series of killings, including those of Hetman and of MP Yevhen Shcherban. Two of them, among them Serhii Kuliev, were sentenced to life. The verdict did not name who ordered the killing. The prosecutor in the trial, and later the Prosecutor General’s Office, named former prime minister Pavlo Lazarenko as the organizer, saying that in their view Hetman stood in his way as a political rival; the case against Lazarenko was split off into separate proceedings. Lazarenko rejects the accusations and calls the case against him fabricated. In 2005 Hetman was posthumously made a Hero of Ukraine.

## Why it matters today

Hetman was one of the builders of independent Ukraine’s banking and monetary system: the National Bank, its own currency and an interbank exchange. The Kyiv National Economic University and a street in Kyiv bear his name, and memorial plaques hang on the house where he lived and on the building of the Ukrainian Interbank Currency Exchange.
