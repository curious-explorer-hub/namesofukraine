## Batons instead of weapons

What did Russia's National Guard bring with it to "restore order" in Kharkiv? When soldiers of the 92nd Brigade destroyed one of its columns near Mala Rohan and Tsyrkuny, they found no super-weapons, just rubber batons, flare pistols and rubber bullets for breaking up protests. That was when brigade commander Pavlo Fedosenko, call sign "Maestro", understood: the enemy had come expecting a parade.

## From Kryvbas to brigade commander

Pavlo was born in 1974 in Kryvyi Rih. In 1995 he graduated from the tank school in Kharkiv. When the war began in 2014, he joined the Kryvbas territorial defence battalion together with other men from his city. He fought near Ilovaisk and Debaltseve and on the Svitlodarsk bulge, was wounded three times and rose to deputy commander of the 54th Brigade. In May 2020 he took command of the 92nd Mechanized Brigade, named after Ivan Sirko and based near Kharkiv.

## Kharkiv held

Fedosenko and his intelligence officers kept a constant watch on the border, and on 22–23 February 2022 he moved his battalions up to it to dig in. On 24 February Commander-in-Chief [Valerii Zaluzhnyi](/en/people/valerii-zaluzhnyi/) came on the line and gave the order: open fire. The brigade fought in mobile groups: two or three armoured personnel carriers with anti-tank missiles, Javelins among them, hitting the columns from every side. The enemy had planned to enter Kharkiv within a day, but got stuck on the ring road. On 2 March 2022 Lieutenant Colonel Fedosenko was made a Hero of Ukraine.

## On to Kupiansk

In September 2022 Fedosenko's brigade advanced alongside air assault brigades on the main line of attack in the Kharkiv counteroffensive. The task was to move as fast as possible and give the enemy no time to dig in. Only when the brigade reached Shevchenkove and Kupiansk did its commander say the battle for Kharkiv had been won. He led the brigade until June 2024, then moved up to a higher post in the Armed Forces.

## Why it matters today

In the first days of the full-scale war, Kharkiv was supposed to fall within a day, and it held. Fedosenko showed what preparation decides: battalions in position ahead of time, and a commander who knows his city. An officer who started the war in a volunteer battalion grew into the brigade commander who helped free the Kharkiv region. And the task he put first was keeping his people alive.
