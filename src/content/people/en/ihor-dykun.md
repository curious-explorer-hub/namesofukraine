## The student who took a leave of absence

"An ordinary family, no one in the military": that is how Ihor Dykun describes where he comes from. He was born on 4 June 1994 in Borshchiv, a town in the Ternopil region of western Ukraine. At school his favourite subject was history, above all the history of the Cossacks. In 2014, when russia started the war in eastern Ukraine, Ihor was studying at the Lviv State University of Internal Affairs. He took a leave of absence and went to the front.

## From soldier to officer

Ihor fought with the 24th Separate Mechanized Brigade named after King Danylo. He was wounded near Yampil in the Donetsk region and received the Order For Courage, 3rd class, for those battles. He decided to become an officer in 2018, when Valerii Hudz came to the brigade: Ihor took him as a model and tried to be in front of his soldiers in battle, not behind them. After earning a law degree, in 2020 he entered the National Army Academy in Lviv and graduated with honours as a junior lieutenant.

## The line at Irpin

In March 2022 Ihor's unit was holding a defensive line in Irpin. Everyone knew the line could not be given up: this was a suburb of Kyiv. On 24 March, after heavy shelling, russian troops attacked, and their armoured vehicles tried to get round the position from the flank. Junior Lieutenant Dykun went out to meet them with a small group and destroyed the enemy vehicles with a grenade launcher. He was wounded in the shoulder and the leg but kept directing fire until the enemy pulled back, and only then let himself be evacuated. On 26 March 2022 he was made a Hero of Ukraine.

## From platoon to brigade

In March 2023, while fighting near Bakhmut, Ihor formed and led the reconnaissance battalion of the 5th Separate Assault Brigade. He later became the brigade's deputy commander. In February 2026 Major Dykun took command of the 32nd Separate Mechanized Brigade, known as the "Steel" brigade. "I don't promise it will be easy. War is hard, dirty work. But I promise that every combat mission will be thought through, and every life will be valued," he wrote to his new comrades.

## Why it matters today

Ihor Dykun went to war as a student, from a family with no soldiers in it, and rose to command a brigade. At Irpin he and his comrades stopped the enemy from going round their positions on the approaches to Kyiv. His path shows that commanders are made, through study, experience and the example of those who led before them. He once learned from Valerii Hudz to be in front of his soldiers in battle; now he promises his own soldiers that every one of their lives will be valued.
