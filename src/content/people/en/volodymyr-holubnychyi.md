## A second and a half

That was the gap between Volodymyr Holubnychyi and his rival at the finish of the Olympic 20 km walk in Mexico City in 1968. A home walker, a Mexican, closed in until there was almost nothing between them, but Holubnychyi held on and won his second Olympic gold.

## A skier who took up walking

Holubnychyi was born in 1936 in Sumy and grew up in a poor family. He started out as a skier. His coach, Vasyl Poliakov, added race walking to his summer training, and it was in walking that the young man became champion of Ukraine; he soon switched to athletics. In 1955, at 19, he set his first world record over 20 km.

## Five Olympics

Holubnychyi walked for the Soviet national team, and at home for Spartak Sumy. At his first Olympics, in Rome in 1960, he didn't expect to win. But the team's experienced walkers were disqualified for breaking the rules, and from around the tenth kilometre Holubnychyi pulled away and crossed the line first.

In Tokyo in 1964 he felt ill during the race and even blacked out for a moment, yet still took bronze. Then came gold in Mexico City, silver in Munich in 1972 and the European title in 1974. He went to his fifth Olympics, Montreal 1976, at the age of 40 and finished seventh.

## Medals that aren't for sale

After he retired, Holubnychyi coached athletes in his home town of Sumy. In 1991 he won the world veterans' title over 5 km, and in 2012 he was inducted into the IAAF Hall of Fame. He was once offered a three-room flat in Sumy and a new car for his full set of Olympic medals. He said no, even though the 1990s were hard. Holubnychyi died in Sumy in 2021, aged 85.

## Why it matters today

Holubnychyi proved you can reach the top of world sport without leaving your home town: for more than twenty years he walked for a club in Sumy, and from there to Olympic podiums. His career is a lesson in endurance: win at your first Olympics, come back after a rough Tokyo, and step up to the start line again at 40. He wouldn't sell his medals even in hard times, and he passed on what he knew to athletes in Sumy. Anyone who wonders how far they can go can think of the walker from Sumy who reached four Olympic medals one step at a time.
