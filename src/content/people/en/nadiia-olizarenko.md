## A record older than independent Ukraine

Ukraine became independent in 1991. Nadiia Olizarenko's Olympic 800-metre record of 1 minute 53.43 seconds, set in Moscow in 1980, is older than that, and no one has beaten it (as of October 2026). At the time it was also the world record, and as late as 2013 it was still the second-fastest time in history. Soviet-era results list her as Nadezhda Olizarenko.

## From Bryansk to Odesa

Nadiia Mushta was born in 1953 in Bryansk, russia, and took up athletics in 1967. She ran for the army sports club in Odesa (SKA). She made the Soviet national team in 1977, and the next year, at the European Championships in Prague, won two silver medals: in the 800 metres and the 4×400 metres relay. In 1979 she won the Universiade in Mexico City.

## Summer 1980

Shortly before the Olympics, Olizarenko broke the 800-metre world record for the first time. At the Games she won the event and lowered the record again, and three days later she added a bronze in the 1500 metres.

## A second attempt

After the Olympics she retired, but in 1984 she came back, hoping to reach a second Games. That year the USSR boycotted the Los Angeles Olympics. Even so, on 5 August 1984 in Moscow she and her Soviet teammates set a 4×800 metres relay world record of 7 minutes 50.17 seconds, which still stands (as of October 2026). At the Friendship Games, held instead, she took bronze. She kept going: in 1986 in Stuttgart she became European 800-metre champion, and in 1988 she finally made her second Olympics, in Seoul, where she reached the semi-finals. In 1986 she also graduated from the Odesa Pedagogical Institute.

## The Nadiia club

After her running career Nadiia Olizarenko coached athletes at her Odesa club, SKA, and in 2001 she opened her own athletics club in Odesa, called Nadiia ("hope"). In 2002 she was awarded the Order of Princess Olga. She died in Odesa in 2017.

## Why it matters today

Nadiia Olizarenko was born in russia, but Odesa became her sporting home: she won Olympic gold running for an Odesa club, coached there and founded the Nadiia club there. Her Olympic 800-metre record has outlasted more than forty years of Games. She showed that you can come back after a break and become the best in Europe again, as long as you don't give up. Any young runner in Ukraine who dreams of the Olympics can remember that an Odesa club has already produced an Olympic champion whose record has outlived generations.
