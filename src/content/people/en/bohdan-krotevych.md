## “What is Ukraine to you?”

Bohdan Krotevych was asked this in captivity, in Moscow’s Lefortovo detention centre. He said it was personal and would not answer. He gave his answer at home, after he was freed: “Ukraine is the dearest thing I have in my life.”

## A Crimean who never planned on the army

Bohdan Krotevych was born in 1993 in Izmail in the Odesa region, but he calls Simferopol his home town: that is where he grew up. As a boy he drew, went to the Simferopol art school and learned English and German. After school he entered the Academy of Water Transport in Kyiv to train as a merchant navy navigator, and in 2013 he joined the Euromaidan protests.

## From private to chief of staff

In 2014, after russia occupied Crimea, Krotevych volunteered for Azov as a private. He finished his degree by correspondence while serving. He fought for Shyrokyne and defended Marinka, Krasnohorivka and the Svitlodarsk arc in the Donetsk region. In 2021, under the call sign Tavr, he became Azov’s chief of staff.

## Mariupol and captivity

When the full-scale invasion began in 2022, Krotevych was chief of staff and first deputy to Azov’s commander [Denys Prokopenko](/en/people/denys-prokopenko/), and with him and [Sviatoslav Palamar](/en/people/sviatoslav-palamar/) he helped direct the defence of encircled Mariupol. In March he said: “We have to win this war. We can, if the Ukrainian people believe in their own strength on their own land, which must not be given up to the enemy without a fight.” The defenders held the Azovstal steelworks for almost three months, until they were ordered out. In May Krotevych was taken prisoner. He spent four months in solitary confinement in Moscow’s Lefortovo prison, completely cut off, and on 21 September 2022 he was freed in an exchange.

## Rebuilding Azov

Back home, Krotevych set about restoring Azov’s fighting strength and helped grow it into the 12th Special Purpose Brigade. In summer 2023 he coordinated the brigade’s fighting in the Zaporizhzhia direction. In June 2024 Krotevych asked the State Bureau of Investigation to look into the actions of General Yurii Sodol, commander of the Khortytsia group of forces, and the Kyiv Court of Appeal later ordered the bureau to open a criminal case. In February 2025, after more than ten years in uniform, Lieutenant Colonel Krotevych left the service.

## Why it matters today

Bohdan Krotevych lost his home in Crimea in 2014 and went on to defend all of Ukraine. A student who meant to sail ships became one of the people who directed the defence of Mariupol, and after captivity he rebuilt his unit. Asked “What is Ukraine to you?”, he gave his captors no answer, and at home he said it simply: the dearest thing he has. His path shows that an army is held together by people for whom Ukraine is not an idea but home.
