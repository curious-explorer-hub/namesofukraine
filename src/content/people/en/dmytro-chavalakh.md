## The “posthumous” award that wasn’t

Can you be made a Hero of Ukraine “posthumously” and hear about it alive? On 2 March 2022, when the President was announcing new Heroes of Ukraine, Dmytro Chavalakh’s relatives misheard “posthumously” next to his name and spent the whole evening mourning him. But the decree lists Senior Lieutenant Chavalakh among the living. He learned of the award in a hospital in Kherson, when a nurse ran into his ward and played the President’s address for him on her phone.

## A volunteer at nineteen

Dmytro Chavalakh was born on 19 August 1994 in the village of Sokolivka in the Khmelnytskyi region of western Ukraine. After a military college in Kamianets-Podilskyi, he began serving as a junior sergeant in a pontoon-bridge regiment in the Odesa region in March 2014. That year he asked to be sent to the war in the east, but his battalion commander said no: “You’ve only just turned 19, and it’s a war out there…” So Dmytro transferred to a new engineering battalion in Nizhyn, and by November he was in the Luhansk region.

## From sergeant to company commander

After the east, Chavalakh decided to become an infantry officer, and in 2019 he graduated from the National Army Academy in Lviv. The young lieutenant chose the 11th “Kyivska Rus” Battalion of the 59th Motorised Infantry Brigade, and in 2020 he took command of a company. In 2021, at the Ukrainian-American Rapid Trident exercise, the company practised attacking behind armoured vehicles. Some of the soldiers wondered why: motorised infantry holds defences, doesn't it?

## Breaking out across the Antonivskyi Bridge

On 24 February 2022 the battalion was at the Oleshky Sands in the Kherson region. From Crimea russian troops pushed north, and russian paratroopers landed on the Antonivskyi Bridge, which links Kherson with the left bank of the Dnipro. The only way out of the encirclement was to break through across the bridge: exactly the attack behind armour they had practised at the exercise. A tank company went first, commanded by Chavalakh’s namesake and fellow cadet, Dmytro Dozirchyi. Covered by the tanks, the infantry fought their way along a kilometre and a half of bridge for about three hours. Chavalakh’s company crossed to the far side and was moving up to cover the rest of the brigade’s breakout when a sniper badly wounded him. The brigade got out of the encirclement.

## Almost three months under occupation

Dmytro was taken to hospital in time, and a few days later russian troops entered the city. Medical staff hid the wounded soldiers. After about a month, when the occupiers began searching the wards, the singer and volunteer Liubov Chernetska found him a hiding place: for almost two months a man from Kherson she knew hid Dmytro in his flat, at the risk of his own life. In all that time Dmytro went outside only once, when he secretly moved from the hospital to the flat. In May 2022 he was brought out to Ukrainian-held territory. The code word for the operation was “going fishing”, and a journey of an hour and a half to two by the main road took a day and a half to get round the russian checkpoints.

## A history teacher

His wounds meant he was discharged from the army. In 2023 he completed a master’s degree in history in Kamianets-Podilskyi. As of early 2024 he was teaching history at a school.

## Why it matters today

At nineteen, Dmytro Chavalakh was already asking to go to the front, and he rose from a pontoon sergeant to a company commander. On the first day of the full-scale war his company helped his brigade break out of encirclement. His survival is also the story of doctors and ordinary people in Kherson who took risks for a wounded soldier. He passes on what he knows to children and to anyone who wants to be able to defend Ukraine. His story shows that you can keep serving your country even after the war has taken away your ability to fight.
