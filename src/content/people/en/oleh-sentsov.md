## 145 days

That is how long Oleh Sentsov’s hunger strike lasted in a Russian penal colony in the Yamal region. On 14 May 2018 he declared it indefinite, with one condition: free all Ukrainian political prisoners in Russia. He lost 20 kilograms and ended the strike in early October, when he was facing force-feeding. That same month the European Parliament awarded him the Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought.

## A director taken from Crimea

Sentsov (often spelled Oleg Sentsov, the Russian form) was born in 1976 in Simferopol, studied at the Kyiv National Economic University, and in 2008 co-founded the film company Krai Kinema. His first feature, Gamer, was screened at the Rotterdam film festival in 2012.

In May 2014, shortly after Russia annexed Crimea, Russian security forces detained him in Simferopol. He was charged with terrorism, and on 25 August 2015 a Russian court sentenced him to 20 years in a penal colony. The human rights centre Memorial recognised Sentsov as a political prisoner, and in 2016 he received Ukraine’s Shevchenko National Prize.

## From a cell to the front

On 7 September 2019 Sentsov was freed in an exchange between Ukraine and Russia, and that November he received the Sakharov Prize in person in Strasbourg. He went back to filmmaking and made the drama Rhino (2021). After the full-scale invasion in 2022 he joined the territorial defence, fought for Kyiv and later served in the 47th Separate Mechanized Brigade as a junior lieutenant commanding an assault company. In 2023 he suffered a concussion. In September 2025, Interfax-Ukraine reported, Sentsov became a battalion commander (the latest information as of October 2026).

## Why it matters today

Sentsov’s case drew the world’s attention to Ukrainians persecuted by Russia for resisting the occupation of Crimea. His path from debut director to political prisoner, Sakharov Prize laureate and soldier has become, for many, an example of how an artist answers a war.
