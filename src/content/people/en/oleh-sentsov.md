## Who he is

Oleh Sentsov (often spelled Oleg Sentsov, the Russian form) was born in 1976 in Simferopol, the main city of Crimea. In 1993–1998 he studied at the Kyiv National Economic University and then took film courses in Moscow. He opened a computer club in Simferopol, and in 2008 he co-founded the film company Krai Kinema. His first feature film, Gamer (2011), was screened at the International Film Festival Rotterdam in 2012. In 2016 Sentsov received the Shevchenko National Prize, Ukraine’s highest award for culture.

## His story

In May 2014, shortly after Russia annexed Crimea, officers of Russia’s FSB security service detained Sentsov in Simferopol together with other Crimeans and took him to Russia. He was accused of setting up a “terrorist group” and plotting attacks; on 25 August 2015 a Russian court sentenced him to 20 years in a penal colony. Human rights groups called the trial unfair, and Sentsov became a symbol of the Ukrainian political prisoners held by Russia.

On 14 May 2018, a month before the FIFA World Cup in Russia, Sentsov, then serving his sentence in a penal colony in the Yamal region of Russia’s far north, declared an indefinite hunger strike, demanding the release of all Ukrainian political prisoners in Russia. He refused food for 145 days, lost 20 kilograms and ended the strike in early October 2018, when he was facing force-feeding. On 25 October 2018 the European Parliament awarded him the Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought.

On 7 September 2019 Sentsov was freed in a prisoner exchange between Ukraine and Russia, in which 35 Ukrainians came home. In November that year he received the Sakharov Prize in person in Strasbourg. After his return he went back to filmmaking and made, among other films, the drama Rhino. After Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022 he joined Kyiv’s Territorial Defense and later fought in the 47th Separate Mechanized Brigade of the Armed Forces of Ukraine: in 2023 he was commissioned as a junior lieutenant, commanded an assault company, and suffered a concussion and later a minor wound. In September 2025, Interfax-Ukraine reported, Sentsov became a battalion commander (the latest information as of October 2026).

## Why it matters today

Sentsov’s case drew the world’s attention to Ukrainians persecuted by Russia for resisting the occupation of Crimea. His path, from debut director to political prisoner, Sakharov Prize laureate and soldier, has become for many an example of how an artist responds to war.
