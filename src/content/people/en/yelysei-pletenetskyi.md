## A press, scholars and paper

What does it take to print a book? A press, people who can write and set the text, and paper. In the early 17th century the archimandrite of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, Yelysei Pletenetskyi, saw to all three himself. That is how Kyiv got a press that kept working for centuries.

## A student from Galicia

Pletenetskyi's lay name was Oleksandr. He was born around 1550; some sources say in Lviv, into the family of a church cantor, others in the village of Pletenychi near Zolochiv. He probably studied at the school of Lviv's Dormition Church and at the Ostroh Academy. In 1595, with the backing of Prince [Kostiantyn-Vasyl Ostrozkyi](/en/people/kostiantyn-vasyl-ostrozkyi/), he became head of the Leshchyn Monastery in Pinsk. He rejected the union with Rome, and in 1596 he took part in the Orthodox council in Berestia (Brest).

## Master of the Lavra

In 1599 he was elected archimandrite of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. He won stauropegial status for the monastery: the Lavra answered directly to the Patriarch of Constantinople rather than to the local metropolitan. When the king handed Lavra estates to the Uniates, Pletenetskyi took them back with the help of the Cossacks of Hetman [Petro Sahaidachnyi](/en/people/petro-sahaidachnyi/). He restored the Lavra's Dormition Church and founded a hospital for the poor and, nearby, a convent.

## A press and a paper mill

Pletenetskyi bought the equipment of the Balaban family's press in Striatyn, Galicia, and brought it to the Lavra. In 1616 its first known book came out: a Horologion, a book of hours used to teach children. The Antholohion (1619) and commentaries by John Chrysostom followed. The archimandrite wrote prefaces to many of the books himself. To keep the press in paper, he founded a paper mill in Radomyshl. From Lviv and Ostroh he invited scholars and writers to the Lavra: [Pamvo Berynda](/en/people/pamvo-berynda/), Zakhariia Kopystenskyi, Lavrentii Zyzanii, Yov Boretskyi. This circle worked with the Kyiv Brotherhood school.

## What Mohyla carried on

Pletenetskyi died in Kyiv in 1624 and was buried in the Lavra's Dormition Church. Three years later [Petro Mohyla](/en/people/petro-mohyla/) became archimandrite. He built on both the press and the circle of scholars that his predecessor had started.

## Why it matters today

Pletenetskyi understood that faith and language are defended not only with words but with books, schools and paper. He gathered at the Lavra everything Kyiv needed to publish its own books, and turned the monastery into a centre of learning. The scholars of his circle wrote the dictionaries and translations from which Ukrainian book culture grew, and Petro Mohyla took the work further. Anyone who opens a book published in Ukraine today is carrying on what he began.
