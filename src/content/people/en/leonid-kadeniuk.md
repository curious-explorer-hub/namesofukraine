## Who he was

Leonid Kadeniuk was born in 1951 in the village of Klishkivtsi in the Khotyn area (Chernivtsi Oblast), into a family of village teachers. His dream of space began at the age of ten, when news of Yuri Gagarin's flight went around the world. In 1971 he graduated from the Chernihiv Higher Military Aviation School for Pilots and spent several years there as a flight instructor.

## His story

In 1976 Kadeniuk was selected for the cosmonaut group being trained for the Soviet reusable spacecraft Buran. He qualified as a test pilot and test cosmonaut, worked at the State Research Institute of the Soviet Air Force, took part in testing fighter aircraft, and in all flew more than 50 types and variants of aircraft. In 1989 he graduated from the Moscow Aviation Institute, and until 1992 he trained as a commander of the Buran and Soyuz TM spacecraft.

In 1995 Kadeniuk joined the astronaut group of the National Space Agency of Ukraine and trained at NASA. From 19 November to 5 December 1997 he flew as a payload specialist on the Space Shuttle Columbia (mission STS-87) as part of an international crew. On board, Kadeniuk carried out a joint Ukrainian-American biological experiment with three plant species: scientists studied how weightlessness affects photosynthesis, fertilization and embryo development.

After the flight Kadeniuk served in the Armed Forces of Ukraine (a major general from 1998), was an adviser to the President on aviation and space, and in 2002–2006 was a member of parliament. In 1999 he was made a Hero of Ukraine, and in 2006 he defended his candidate's dissertation in technical sciences. Leonid Kadeniuk died suddenly in Kyiv on 31 January 2018, during a morning run. The same year, Chernivtsi International Airport was named after him.

## Why it matters today

Kadeniuk's flight showed that independent Ukraine could be an equal partner in international space research. For generations he has been proof that a childhood dream of space can come true.
