## Three days at Zamość

In late August 1920 Semyon Budyonny’s First Cavalry Army was racing towards Warsaw. In its way stood the Polish town of Zamość. It was held by the Ukrainian 6th Sich Rifle Division and Polish units, under the command of Colonel Marko Bezruchko of the UNR Army. The garrison beat off the assault for almost three days, and on 31 August the siege was lifted.

Budyonny never managed to help the Reds at Warsaw, which historians count as one of the reasons for the Polish victory in the Battle of Warsaw. For the defence of Zamość, Bezruchko and his deputy Vsevolod Zmiienko were promoted to general-khorunzhyi (major general).

## From a teachers’ seminary to the General Staff

Marko Bezruchko was born in 1883 in Velykyi Tokmak in southern Ukraine (now Tokmak in the Zaporizhzhia region). He studied at a teachers’ seminary in Pereiaslav, then graduated from the infantry cadet school in Odesa and, in 1914, from the Imperial Military Academy in Saint Petersburg. In the First World War he was a staff officer and a lieutenant colonel.

In 1917 Bezruchko helped Ukrainize Russian army units at the front. At the end of 1918 he went over to the Directory and took charge of the operations department of the UNR Army General Staff. From April 1919 he was chief of staff of the Sich Riflemen Corps, commanded by [Yevhen Konovalets](/en/people/yevhen-konovalets/).

## The Sixth Sich Division

In 1920 Bezruchko formed and took command of the 6th Sich Rifle Division. After [Symon Petliura](/en/people/symon-petliura/)’s alliance with Józef Piłsudski, Ukrainian and Polish troops marched on Kyiv together, and on 7 May 1920 Bezruchko’s division entered the city with the Polish army. Hard fighting with the Reds followed, then the defence of Zamość, and in September–November 1920 Bezruchko commanded the Central Group of the UNR Army, then led by [Mykhailo Omelianovych-Pavlenko](/en/people/mykhailo-omelianovych-pavlenko/).

## A general in Warsaw

After the UNR’s defeat Bezruchko stayed in Poland. In 1920–1924 he was defence minister and deputy minister of the UNR government in exile; in 1931–1935 he headed the Ukrainian Military History Society in Warsaw, and he wrote a book about the Sich Riflemen in the struggle for statehood. According to the Encyclopedia of the History of Ukraine, at the start of the Second World War he actively supported the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists. Marko Bezruchko died in Warsaw on 10 February 1944 and is buried in the Orthodox cemetery in Wola, next to Zmiienko.

## Why he is remembered

In 2020 Warsaw city council named a square after him, and Ukraine’s embassy thanked the Poles for recognizing the role of their Ukrainian allies in the war with the Bolsheviks in 1920. A roundabout in Wrocław bears his name, and in Ukraine, since 2022, the 110th Separate Mechanized Brigade of the Armed Forces has been named after General Marko Bezruchko.
