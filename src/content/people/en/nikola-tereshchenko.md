## The boy who traded grain

Nikola (Mykola) Tereshchenko was born in 1819 in Hlukhiv in the Chernihiv region (now the Sumy region), into a family of Cossack and townspeople stock. His only schooling was a three-year district school. As a teenager he was already trading grain, and later he brought salt and fish from Crimea. During the Crimean War of 1853–1856 he supplied the army on state contracts, which greatly increased his capital.

## A sugar empire

After serfdom was abolished in 1861, Tereshchenko, his father Artemii and his brother Fedir bought up large estates, sugar mills and distilleries. In 1870 the brothers founded the Tereshchenko Brothers’ Sugar and Refinery Company, at the time the largest in the sugar industry of the Russian Empire. The same year the family was granted hereditary nobility. Nikola personally owned three more sugar mills, flour mills, brickworks and about 60,000 desiatins of land.

## Money given back to the cities

In Hlukhiv Tereshchenko was the senior burgomaster, and from 1860 the mayor. The family gave the town one and a half million roubles: it got gymnasiums for boys and girls, hospitals, an orphanage and a trade school. In 1875 Tereshchenko moved to Kyiv. There his money built the 5th Gymnasium, a city school, a commercial school for girls, a hospital for labourers and a night shelter for 500 people. He gave large sums to the Polytechnic Institute and to the City Museum of Antiquities and Arts, whose building was designed by [Vladyslav Horodetskyi](/en/people/vladyslav-horodetskyi/). In all, Tereshchenko gave away more than 5 million roubles in his lifetime. He was an honorary citizen of both Kyiv and Hlukhiv.

## Why it matters today

Nikola Tereshchenko died in Kyiv in 1903. Much of what he built is still in use: the old hospital for labourers is the Okhmatdyt children’s hospital, the city school building houses the Karpenko-Karyi Kyiv University of Theatre, Film and Television, and the museum building is the National Art Museum of Ukraine. His former mansion is home to the National Museum of [Taras Shevchenko](/en/people/taras-shevchenko/). His paintings, together with those of his brother and his son, became the core of the Kyiv Museum of Russian Art. And the collection of his daughter Varvara and her husband Bohdan Khanenko became the basis of today’s Bohdan and Varvara Khanenko Museum of Arts.
