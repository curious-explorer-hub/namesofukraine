## How did a hopak win in London?

In 1935 London hosted an International Folk Dance Festival, and first prize went to a Ukrainian piece, the Triple Hopak. It was staged by Vasyl Verkhovynets and L. Zhukov, to music by Verkhovynets himself. Behind that success lay decades of work by the man who wrote the first textbooks of Ukrainian folk dance.

## From the stage to scholarship

Verkhovynets (his real surname was Kostiv) was born in 1880 in the village of Staryi Mizun in Galicia, now in the Ivano-Frankivsk region. He trained at the teachers’ seminary in Sambir, then sang, acted and led the choir at the Ruthenian People’s Theater in Lviv. From 1906 to 1915 he was an actor, choirmaster, choreographer and conductor at Mykola Sadovskyi’s theater, and in 1908 he graduated from [Mykola Lysenko](/en/people/mykola-lysenko/)’s music and drama school in Kyiv. In 1915–1917 he conducted and staged dances for the Society of Ukrainian Artists, whose company included [Mariia Zankovetska](/en/people/mariia-zankovetska/) and Panas Saksahanskyi.

In 1912 Verkhovynets recorded an entire wedding rite in the village of Shpychyntsi: 140 wedding songs and 70 melodies. He wrote the first Ukrainian textbooks of folk dance, Ukrainian Folk Dances (1913) and Theory of the Ukrainian Folk Dance (1919), which went through four more editions.

## Songs for children and for the stage

In the 1920s and early 1930s Verkhovynets worked in Poltava, teaching and heading the art department of the Institute of People’s Education. For children he compiled Vesnianochka (1925): 210 folk games and 226 songs and melodies. He set poems by [Lesya Ukrainka](/en/people/lesya-ukrainka/), [Ivan Franko](/en/people/ivan-franko/) and [Maksym Rylskyi](/en/people/maksym-rylskyi/) to music. In 1931, with V. Lytvynenko, he staged Mykhailo Verykivskyi’s Pan Kanovskyi at the Kharkiv Opera and Ballet Theater, the first Ukrainian national classical ballet.

## Arrest

Verkhovynets was arrested and accused of belonging to an underground “nationalist” organization. He was shot in Kyiv on 11 April 1938. He was later rehabilitated posthumously.

## Why it matters today

Verkhovynets turned folk dance, passed from person to person, into knowledge that could be learned from a book and brought to the stage. His textbooks and field records gave Ukrainian choreography a foundation of its own, and Vesnianochka taught children to play and sing in Ukrainian. The hopak that took first prize in London showed that Ukrainian dance could hold its own on the world stage. Everyone who teaches or dances Ukrainian folk dance builds on what he collected and described.
