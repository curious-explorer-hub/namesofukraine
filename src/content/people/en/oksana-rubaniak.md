## Where the title came from

Why would a young machine gunner call her poetry book “Towards Death”? Oksana Rubaniak has told the story: one night she and a comrade, Oleh, were driving ammunition towards Bakhmut through a thunderstorm, and she told him it felt as if they were driving towards death. That became the title. Oleh was later killed.

Ksena wrote the poems at the front. In dugouts and trenches she kept the lines in her head, and whenever she reached a quieter place she wrote them down. The book came out in a print run of 1,000 copies. She signed one of the very first copies in a trench, and promised all the money from sales to equipment for her unit.

## From a Hutsul village to a machine-gun platoon

Oksana was born in 2003 in a Hutsul village in the Ivano-Frankivsk region, in the Carpathians. She trained as a primary school teacher, worked in the youth and sports department of Ivano-Frankivsk city council, and in 2021 joined a university military training course.

On 24 February 2022, the first day of the full-scale invasion, 19-year-old Oksana joined Ivano-Frankivsk’s volunteer formation, and later the Armed Forces. With her comrades she ended up in the 72nd Separate Mechanized Brigade and became a machine gunner, call sign “Ksena”. For a year and a half she was the only woman in her platoon. At first her comrades doubted she could handle a machine gun: she weighs under 50 kilograms.

## Wounded, then a new command

Ksena was wounded near Vuhledar in the Donetsk region, and after treatment she returned to service. She later took command of an unmanned aerial systems platoon, recruiting and training its people herself. As of October 2026 she holds the rank of captain.

## Why it matters

“Towards Death” has been called a trench diary in verse, and Oksana dedicated her next collection, “The Road of Life”, to fallen comrades. A young soldier from the Carpathians, she tells the story of the war in the first person and keeps the memory of those who stood beside her.
