## From Kirov to Kryvyi Rih

Borys Baranov was born in 1940 in the village of Sozinovy, now in Russia’s Kirov Oblast. In 1966 he began working in Ukraine, at the combined heat and power plant of the steelworks in Kryvyi Rih. He spent ten years there and earned an engineering degree by correspondence.

## Shift supervisor

In 1976 Baranov moved to the Chornobyl Nuclear Power Plant, where for many years he was a shift supervisor. He was working there when Unit 4 exploded on 26 April 1986.

## Water under the reactor

There was still water under the wrecked reactor. If the molten nuclear fuel had reached it, a second, even more powerful explosion could have followed. Baranov, with the engineers Oleksii Ananenko and Valerii Bespalov, went down into the flooded space beneath the unit and opened the valves by hand to drain the water. A legend later grew around them: three divers swimming in the dark who soon died. In fact, research suggests the water was only about knee-deep, and all three survived. Baranov worked at the plant for another 19 years.

## Remembered

Borys Baranov died in Kyiv on 6 April 2005. In 2018 he was posthumously awarded the Order for Courage, 3rd class, and on 27 June 2019 the president made him a Hero of Ukraine (posthumously), together with Oleksii Ananenko and Valerii Bespalov, for heroism during the clean-up of the Chornobyl disaster.

## Why it matters today

The Chornobyl disaster could have been even worse. Borys Baranov’s story is a reminder that it was held back not only by firefighters and soldiers, but also by plant workers who knew their equipment well and went where they were needed.
