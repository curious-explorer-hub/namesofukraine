## Graves in the forest

What do young artists do when they learn that people shot by the state lie in secret graves right next to Kyiv? In 1962–1963 the director Les Taniuk, together with [Alla Horska](/en/people/alla-horska/) and [Vasyl Symonenko](/en/people/vasyl-symonenko/), tracked down the places where the NKVD, the Soviet secret police, had buried the people it shot: at Bykivnia and at the Lukianivka and Vasylkiv cemeteries. They reported what they had found to the Kyiv city council.

## The club where the Sixtiers met

Les Taniuk was born in 1938 in the village of Zhukyn near Kyiv. He studied directing in Kyiv under Marian Krushelnytskyi, a pupil of [Les Kurbas](/en/people/les-kurbas/), and became a champion of Kurbas’s theatre. In 1959–1963, while still a student, Taniuk was president of the Club of Creative Youth. [Ivan Svitlychnyi](/en/people/ivan-svitlychnyi/), Alla Horska, [Vasyl Stus](/en/people/vasyl-stus/), [Viacheslav Chornovil](/en/people/viacheslav-chornovil/) and others gathered around it. Taniuk also founded the New Kyiv Theatre and staged Mykola Kulish’s plays in Odesa and Kharkiv.

## Driven off the stage

Censors cut his productions, and officials accused him of “abstract art” and “formalism”. In 1965 Taniuk left for Moscow. For almost twenty years he directed there, more than fifty productions in all, and wrote a book about his teacher Krushelnytskyi.

In 1986 he was invited back to lead the Kyiv Youth Theatre. There, with [Ivan Drach](/en/people/ivan-drach/), he translated and staged the sharp play The Dictatorship of Conscience, and put on Kurbas, Shakespeare, Franko and Stus. But in 1988 Taniuk was dismissed, and barred from directing.

## A diary of the century

So he turned to public life. He joined the Memorial society, which brought back the memory of the victims of repression, and led it from 1992. In 1990 Taniuk was elected to Ukraine’s parliament, where he chaired the commission on culture and spiritual revival. He served five terms as a member of parliament.

For decades Les Taniuk wrote in his diary every day, even at the cost of sleep. He died in Kyiv on 18 March 2016. The poet Yurii Rybchynskyi called his notes a diary of the 20th century, written by someone who took part in events rather than watching them.
