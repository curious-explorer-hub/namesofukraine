## Who he was

Little is known about Ivan Bohun’s origins. He probably came from the Ukrainian petty nobility; the year and place of his birth are unknown (about 1618 is the year most often given). From the early 1640s he took part in the struggle of the Don and Zaporozhian Cossacks against Turkish and Tatar raids. The Treaty of Zboriv of 1649 lists him among the officers of the regimental company of the Chyhyryn regiment, and in 1650 Bohun was appointed colonel of Kalnyk (Vinnytsia).

## His story

Bohun was one of the most outstanding commanders of the national war of liberation led by Bohdan Khmelnytskyi. In 1651–1653 he defeated Polish forces at Vinnytsia and Monastyryshche, defended Zhvanets and took part in the campaign into Moldavia. He won his greatest fame at the Battle of Berestechko in 1651: when the Crimean khan Islam Giray III betrayed the Cossacks and took Khmelnytskyi captive, and the army found itself encircled, the Cossacks elected Bohun acting hetman. Thanks to his decisiveness and ingenuity, most of the army broke out and retreated to Bila Tserkva. In 1654–1657 he fought successfully near Bratslav, Uman and Okhmativ and repelled Tatar raids.

Even before the Pereiaslav Council of 1654, Bohun warned against an alliance with the Muscovite tsar, and he later refused to swear allegiance to him. After Khmelnytskyi’s death he at first supported Hetman Ivan Vyhovskyi, but he opposed Vyhovskyi’s pro-Polish policy and in 1659 became one of the organizers of an uprising against him in Right-Bank Ukraine, together with Ivan Sirko. In 1662 the Polish authorities imprisoned Bohun in the fortress of Marienburg (today Malbork in Poland). The following year King John II Casimir released him, counting on his military talent for a campaign into Left-Bank Ukraine, and gave him the Pavoloch regiment. But in February 1664 Bohun was suspected of secret contacts with the Left-Bank Cossacks and the Muscovites and was executed near Novhorod-Siverskyi.

## Why it matters today

Bohun is an example of a commander who defended Ukraine’s self-rule against both Poland and Moscow. His deeds are celebrated in folk tradition, including the historical duma “Ivan Bohun.”
