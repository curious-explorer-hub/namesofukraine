## A forward with an engineering degree

Valeriy Lobanovskyi was born in 1939 in Kyiv and played as a forward for Dynamo Kyiv. In 1961 Dynamo became the first Soviet champion from outside Moscow, and Lobanovskyi was part of that team. Alongside football he studied engineering, and that training later reshaped how he coached.

## Football by numbers

As Dynamo's coach, Lobanovskyi and the scientist Anatolii Zelentsov put the team's preparation on a scientific footing. They measured players' physical performance, analyzed match statistics and built training around precise calculations.

The trophy cabinet shows the result. Dynamo won the Cup Winners' Cup twice, in 1975 and 1986, and its players [Oleh Blokhin](/en/people/oleh-blokhin/) and Ihor Belanov won the Ballon d'Or. In the 1990s Lobanovskyi came back to the club and raised a new generation, including [Andriy Shevchenko](/en/people/andrii-shevchenko/) and Serhii Rebrov. In 1999 Dynamo reached the Champions League semi-final.

In 2002 he suffered a stroke during a match in Zaporizhzhia and died a few days later. He was posthumously made a Hero of Ukraine.

## What he left behind

Lobanovskyi showed that a Ukrainian club could beat Europe's best through system, hard work and science. Kyiv's Dynamo Stadium bears his name.
