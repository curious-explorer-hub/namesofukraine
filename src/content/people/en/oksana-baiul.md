## The missing anthem

Picture it: the Olympic champion is waiting for her medal, and nothing happens. In February 1994 in Lillehammer, Norway, the organizers had no recording of Ukraine's national anthem, and the ceremony had to be held up. The head of Ukraine's figure skating delegation brought a recording over from the Olympic village. That is how 16-year-old Oksana Baiul won independent Ukraine's first Olympic gold.

## From Dnipro to Odesa

Oksana Baiul was born in 1977 in Dnipro (then Dnipropetrovsk) and started skating in 1981, when she was still tiny. At the Soviet championships she never finished higher than tenth. In 1992 she moved to Odesa, where Halyna Zmiievska later became her coach.

## World champion at 15

In 1993 in Prague, Baiul skated a flawless free program, beat France's Surya Bonaly and America's Nancy Kerrigan, and became world champion. She was 15.

## One tenth of a point

In practice the day before the free program, Baiul collided with the German skater Tanja Szewczenko. She badly wrenched her back and gashed her shin, a cut that needed three stitches. Before she skated she was given painkilling injections, and she went out on the ice. Near the end of her program she changed it on the fly, added a combination of jumps she hadn't planned in advance, and beat America's Nancy Kerrigan by one tenth of a point. Five of the nine judges voted for Baiul.

After the Games she moved to the United States, and for eight years she was a lead skater in ice shows across the US, Canada and France.

## Why it matters today

Independent Ukraine's Olympic story began with Baiul. As of 2020 she was still Ukraine's only Olympic champion in figure skating. Anyone who doubts themselves can remember how a 16-year-old from Dnipro didn't give up.
