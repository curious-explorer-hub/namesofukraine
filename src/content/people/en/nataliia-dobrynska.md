## Thirteenth, then first

Who would have bet on an athlete with no international combined-events win to her name? Going into the heptathlon at the 2008 Beijing Olympics, Nataliia Dobrynska was ranked only 13th. The reigning champion, Sweden's Carolina Klüft, had chosen to focus on the long jump, so the gold was up for grabs. Dobrynska believed in herself: "Coming into the Games, I thought I could win." Over two days she set personal bests in five of the seven events and scored 6,733 points, 346 more than ever before.

## From a stadium in Vinnytsia

Nataliia Dobrynska was born on 29 May 1982 in Vinnytsia. Her father first took her to a track when she was eight. At 17 she won her first national junior heptathlon title. She competed for the Kolos club in Vinnytsia. Her first international medal, a silver at the World Indoor Championships, came in Budapest in 2004.

## A record in Istanbul

Injuries dogged her after Beijing, but in 2010 she took silver at both the World Indoor Championships and the European Championships in Barcelona. At the 2012 World Indoor Championships in Istanbul she scored 5,013 points in the pentathlon. She became the first woman to break the 5,000-point barrier and beat a world record that had stood for 20 years. Her record lasted 11 years: only in March 2023, in the same arena, did two rivals go past it.

## The sport cannot stop

Dobrynska retired in 2013. In 2023 she was elected to the World Athletics Council, taking the seat of [Serhii Bubka](/en/people/serhii-bubka/). During the war she has spoken of how the World Athletics Kids' Athletics programme reached many of Ukraine's small cities: "The sport cannot stop. Otherwise, we may lose the next generation of athletes." In May 2026 she held her own event in Vinnytsia, Dobrynska Next, where more than 100 children and young athletes from eight regions competed in combined events.

## Why it matters today

Dobrynska showed that an Olympic title can be won even when nobody is betting on you: belief and your best performance on the right day were enough. For eleven years her Istanbul record was the mark the world's best all-rounders aimed at. After her career she became Ukraine's voice in world athletics. And in wartime she fights to keep Ukrainian children coming to the track, because they are where the next champions will come from.
