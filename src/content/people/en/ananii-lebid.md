## Kotsiubynskyi’s manuscripts

After [Mykhailo Kotsiubynskyi](/en/people/mykhailo-kotsiubynskyi/) died, his wife gave the writer’s manuscripts and archive to a museum in Chernihiv in 1920. The first to sort through them was Ananii Lebid. He checked the texts and prepared the letters and drafts for print. He also tracked down and published the story “Andrii Soloveiko”, which never came out in Kotsiubynskyi’s lifetime.

## A boy from Chernihiv

Ananii Lebid was born in 1898 in the village of Vertiivka in the Chernihiv region and grew up in Chernihiv, where Kotsiubynskyi lived at the time. He knew the writer personally. After the gymnasium he studied in Petrograd, and in 1922 he graduated from the Chernihiv Institute of People’s Education. At Chernihiv museums he studied the archives of Panteleimon Kulish, Ivan Nechui-Levytskyi and Kotsiubynskyi.

## In the Neoclassicist circle

In 1924 Lebid moved to Kyiv. He taught Ukrainian literature and worked at the Academy of Sciences. [Serhii Yefremov](/en/people/serhii-yefremov/) and [Ahatanhel Krymskyi](/en/people/ahatanhel-krymskyi/) recommended him for graduate study. Lebid belonged to the circle of the Kyiv Neoclassicists. With [Maksym Rylskyi](/en/people/maksym-rylskyi/) he compiled the reader Over 25 Years (1926), which brings together Lesya Ukrainka, Kotsiubynskyi, Stefanyk, Tychyna and Khvylovyi. With [Mykola Zerov](/en/people/mykola-zerov/) and [Pavlo Fylypovych](/en/people/pavlo-fylypovych/) he ran a student circle on the culture of the Ukrainian word. In 1929 his book M. Kotsiubynskyi: Life and Work came out.

## Two arrests

In 1929 Lebid was arrested in the fabricated case of the “Union for the Liberation of Ukraine”. Nothing was proved, and after seven months in prison he got a suspended sentence. But the 13-volume edition of Kotsiubynskyi’s works he was preparing stopped at the fifth volume. On 20 April 1935 he was arrested again, in the “Neoclassicists case”, together with Zerov, Fylypovych and [Marko Voronyi](/en/people/marko-voronyi/). Lebid refused to admit belonging to an imaginary organization led by Zerov, both under interrogation and in court. Even so, in February 1936 he was sentenced to 10 years in the camps and sent to the Solovetsky Islands. On 25 November 1937 his sentence was changed to death. Ananii Lebid was shot with other Solovki prisoners on 8 December 1937; sources differ on where. He was rehabilitated in 1958.

## Why it matters today

Ananii Lebid kept Kotsiubynskyi for us: he was the first to put the writer’s manuscripts in order, checked the texts and gave readers an unknown story. The reader he compiled with Rylskyi gathered a quarter century of Ukrainian writing into one book. He would not admit to fabricated charges, even when that could not save him. Anyone who looks after the archives and manuscripts of Ukrainian writers today carries on his work.
