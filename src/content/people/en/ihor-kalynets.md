## The book they took off the shelves

In 1966 a young poet from Lviv published his first collection in Kyiv, Kupala’s Fire. Soon it was withdrawn from sale in western Ukraine, and the author was summoned to the regional Communist Party committee to explain his “suspicious” lines. His next books came out only abroad: in Brussels, Munich and New York.

## The Sixtiers’ voice in Lviv

Ihor Kalynets was born in 1939 in Khodoriv, in the Lviv region. He graduated from Lviv University and worked at the regional archive. In 1962 [Ivan Dziuba](/en/people/ivan-dziuba/) and [Ivan Drach](/en/people/ivan-drach/) came to Lviv, and that meeting began a lasting friendship. [Ivan Svitlychnyi](/en/people/ivan-svitlychnyi/) recorded Kalynets reading his poems on tape. On trips to Kyiv the circle grew to include [Alla Horska](/en/people/alla-horska/) and [Yevhen Sverstiuk](/en/people/yevhen-sverstiuk/). And when Ukrainian intellectuals were put on trial in Lviv, [Viacheslav Chornovil](/en/people/viacheslav-chornovil/) and [Lina Kostenko](/en/people/lina-kostenko/) came to the trials.

From 1965 the KGB kept watch on Kalynets and his wife, the poet Iryna Kalynets.

## Six years in the camps, three in exile

Iryna was arrested in January 1972, and he was arrested on 11 August. The court gave the poet 6 years in strict-regime camps and 3 years of exile, mainly for poems printed in the West. He served his term in the political camps of the Perm region and joined the prisoners’ hunger strikes and appeals. In 1975, while still in the camp, he was made a member of PEN. The Kalynetses served their exile together, in Transbaikalia.

## The impresario of a former poet

In 1981 Kalynets came back to Lviv and stopped writing poems. By then he had put together seventeen collections, nine of them before his arrest. From then on he called himself “the impresario of the former Ihor Kalynets”. In 1987 he helped publish the unofficial journal Yevshan-zillia, and later he wrote fairy tales for children. In 1992 he received the Shevchenko Prize.

Ihor Kalynets died in Lviv on 28 June 2025, aged 85, and was buried in the field of honour at Lychakiv Cemetery. His poems, which for decades circulated in the USSR only in samvydav and in editions printed abroad, finally came out as a complete collection in 1991.
