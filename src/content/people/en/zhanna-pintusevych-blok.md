## A victory lap that started too soon

At the 1997 World Championships in Athens, Zhanna Pintusevich crossed the 100 m line almost level with America's Marion Jones. Sure she had won, she set off on a victory lap. Then the scoreboard showed Jones ahead by two hundredths of a second, and the Ukrainian sank to the track. She won gold in the 200 m at the same championships, but, her husband said, that 100 m defeat haunted her for years.

## From Nizhyn to the world's tracks

Zhanna Tarnopolska was born on 6 July 1972 in Nizhyn, in the Chernihiv region. In 1991 she won the European junior title in both the 100 m and the 200 m, and the next year she became European indoor champion in the 60 m. She won three silver medals at European Championships, and in 1999, in Maebashi, Japan, she became world indoor champion in the 200 m.

## The 100 m she waited four years for

In November 2000 her husband, the American sports agent Mark Block, became her coach, and they set out to rebuild her start and acceleration. On 6 August 2001, at the World Championships in Edmonton, Canada, Pintusevich-Block beat Jones twice in one day, in the semi-final and the final; Jones had won 42 finals in a row before that. She won the final in 10.82 seconds, three hundredths ahead of the American. This time there was no early victory lap: she dropped to the track and waited for the official result.

## Debates and assessments

In March 2011 a US anti-doping arbitration panel banned Mark Block for 10 years, finding him guilty of trafficking drugs from the BALCO laboratory and of giving banned substances to his wife. In December 2011 the Ukrainian Athletics Federation banned Zhanna Block for two years for breaking anti-doping rules in 2002–2003. By then she had not competed since 2006. In June 2012 the sport's world governing body confirmed the decision, and all her results from 30 November 2001 were annulled, including her 2003 world indoor gold in the 60 m. Her world titles of 1997 and 2001, won before that date, were not affected.

## Why it matters today

Pintusevich-Block showed that a defeat at the finish line can become a goal: after Athens she worked for four years until she won the 100 m. A two-time world champion, the girl from Nizhyn put Ukrainian sprinting on a level with the best runners on the planet. Her Edmonton race tells anyone who has lost by a few hundredths that a second chance is worth preparing for, and it can come.
