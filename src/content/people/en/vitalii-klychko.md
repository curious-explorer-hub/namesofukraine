## The champion who gave up his belt

Vitali Klitschko was the reigning WBC heavyweight boxing world champion in December 2013. And he gave up the title himself, to concentrate on politics. The World Boxing Council named him its “champion emeritus”.

## Kickboxing first

Vitali Klitschko (Vitalii Klychko in Ukrainian transliteration) was born in 1971 in the village of Belovodskoye in Kyrgyzstan, then part of the Soviet Union, into a military family. His father, a military pilot, also served in Czechoslovakia, and in 1985 the family moved to Ukraine. Vitali graduated from the Pereiaslav-Khmelnytskyi Pedagogical Institute (1995) and earned a PhD-level degree (candidate of sciences) in physical education and sport. His younger brother is [Wladimir Klitschko](/en/people/volodymyr-klychko/), also a world boxing champion.

Klitschko first made his name in kickboxing, winning world titles many times as an amateur and as a professional. In boxing he was Ukrainian champion several times and won the first World Military Games (1995), and in 1996 he turned professional. In 1999 he won the WBO heavyweight world title, and in 2004 the WBC title. In 2005 injuries made him announce his retirement, but in 2008 he returned to the ring and won the WBC belt again. In all he had 47 professional fights and won 45 of them, 41 by knockout. In 2004 he was made a Hero of Ukraine. With his brother he founded the promotion company K2 Promotions and the Klitschko Brothers Foundation, a charity.

## From the ring to City Hall

Klitschko won a seat on Kyiv City Council as early as 2006, and in 2010 he became leader of the UDAR party (the name means “punch”). In 2012–2014 he was a member of the Verkhovna Rada, Ukraine’s parliament. In 2014 he was elected mayor of Kyiv, and he was re-elected in 2015 and 2020. As of October 2026 he leads a city living under constant Russian attacks.

## Why it matters today

Together with his brother [Wladimir](/en/people/volodymyr-klychko/), Vitali Klitschko made Ukrainian boxers known all over the world. After injuries and years away from the ring he came back and won the world title again, then gave up the belt himself for a new calling: politics. As of October 2026 he leads Kyiv, a city living under constant Russian attacks. His story shows that you can make it back to the top, and that a new goal can be worth giving up what you have won.
