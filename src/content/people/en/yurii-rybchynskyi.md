## Geese that have been flying for half a century

Did you know that the song “Oi letily dyki husy” (Wild Geese Were Flying) has a lyricist and a composer? Yurii Rybchynskyi wrote its words in 1969, and Ihor Poklad wrote the music. Its best-known singers are [Nina Matviienko](/en/people/nina-matviienko/) and [Ruslana](/en/people/ruslana-lyzhychko/), who won the Slavianskyi Bazaar festival with it. The poet himself has said he cried several times listening to “Wild Geese.”

## A poet from Kyiv

Yurii Rybchynskyi was born in 1945 in Kyiv. He began writing poems at school and graduated from the philology faculty of Taras Shevchenko Kyiv State University. He has been writing song lyrics since 1965. One of his first hits was “Glaza na peske” (Eyes on the Sand), sung by Tamara Miansarova.

## Words for many voices

Rybchynskyi is a lyricist: his poems become songs together with composers such as Ihor Poklad, Oleksandr Zlotnyk, Eduard Khanok and Mykola Mozghovyi. With Mozghovyi he wrote “Mynaie den” (The Day Goes By). Among those who sang his songs was [Sofiia Rotaru](/en/people/sofiia-rotaru/). He is also a playwright.

In 2020 Rybchynskyi was made a Hero of Ukraine with the Order of the State, for enriching Ukrainian culture, shaping national identity and many years of creative work. In 2026 the Hryhorii Veryovka Choir closed his jubilee year with the concert “Legends of Ukraine. Golden Hits” at the Palace “Ukraine” in Kyiv.

## Why it matters today

Several generations have sung songs to Rybchynskyi’s words, and many people know them by heart without knowing who wrote the lyrics. “Wild Geese,” written more than half a century ago, is still heard both on the pop stage and from choirs.
