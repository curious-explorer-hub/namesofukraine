## Gold where she had left

Did you know that the first Olympic champion in women's wrestling could have been Greek? Iryna Merleni spent a while preparing to compete for Greece, but she came home. And in 2004, in Athens of all places, she won for Ukraine the first Olympic gold ever awarded in women's wrestling.

## The girl who kept getting thrown

Iryna Melnyk was born on 8 February 1982 in Kamianets-Podilskyi. She tried gymnastics, chess and the violin, and took up wrestling at 15: her father had wrestled and coached. At her first session a girl who had trained for just three months threw the newcomer onto the mat again and again. Back then people often said wrestling was "not a sport for women". Iryna didn't give up: at 16 she won the Cadet World Championships in Manchester, England, and in 2000 and 2001 she was senior world champion.

## How Melnyk became Merleni

Women's wrestling wasn't an Olympic sport yet, and there was hardly any money for it: Iryna often travelled to competitions on her parents' or sponsors' money. So after 2001 she agreed to compete for Greece. There she was offered an invented surname, Merleni: the Ukrainian Melnyk, as she tells it, didn't suit the Greeks. She never wrestled for Greece at a World or European Championships, and after a year and a half she returned to Ukraine's team. She kept the new name.

## The first gold in history

Three months after her return, in 2003, Merleni became world champion for the third time, in New York, where the first Olympic places were at stake. At Athens 2004 women wrestled for Olympic medals for the first time, in four weight classes. Merleni competed at 48 kg and won her first three bouts 31-0 in under five minutes in all. In the final against Japan's Chiharu Icho, a reigning world champion, the score ended 2-2, and the judges gave the win to the Ukrainian.

Merleni also won two European titles, and at the 2008 Beijing Games, now as Merleni-Mykulchyn, she took bronze.

## Why it matters today

Women's wrestling entered Olympic history with a Ukrainian name: its first gold belongs to Ukraine. Merleni chose to compete for her own country even when it could barely support women's wrestling, and proved that you can reach the top from home. She came into a sport many people didn't think was for women and made it a source of pride for Ukraine. For any girl who is told something is "not for her", her story is proof that you can fight for your place and win it.
