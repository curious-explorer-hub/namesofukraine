## A physicist from the theology faculty

Ivan Puliui was born in 1845 in the small town of Hrymailiv in the Ternopil region; this part of Galicia then belonged to the Austrian Empire. In 1864 he finished the gymnasium in Ternopil with distinction and entered the theology faculty of the University of Vienna. But he never became a priest: after theology he studied physics and mathematics in Vienna, and in 1877 he defended a doctorate in Strasbourg on friction in gases.

## A lamp with a cold glow

Puliui studied electrical discharges in thin gases. In 1882 he invented a phosphorescent lamp that is seen as a forerunner of the X-ray tube. His work on “cold light”, which we would now call neon light, was a highlight of the 1884 exhibition in Steyr, Austria. Earlier, in 1878, his device for measuring the mechanical equivalent of heat won an award at the World’s Fair in Paris.

When Wilhelm Röntgen announced the discovery of new rays, Puliui already had both the tubes and the experience. Early in 1896 he published two papers in Vienna on how these rays arise and how they act on photographic plates.

## Professor in Prague

From 1884, for more than 30 years, Puliui was a professor at the German Polytechnic Institute in Prague. He was elected its rector, and in 1902 he became the first dean of Europe’s first faculty of electrical engineering. He built an alternating-current power station in Prague and served as an expert on electrical engineering in Bohemia. In 1916 he was offered the post of Austria-Hungary’s minister of education, but turned it down because of serious illness.

## The Bible in Ukrainian

Puliui never forgot where he came from. In Vienna he was one of the founders, and for a time the head, of the Ukrainian student society Sich. With Panteleimon Kulish he translated the Gospels (1871) and the whole New Testament (1880) into Ukrainian, and with Ivan Nechui-Levytskyi the Old Testament. These translations went into the Ukrainian Bible published by the British Bible Society in 1903. Puliui also translated the first prayer book in everyday Ukrainian (Vienna, 1871), campaigned for a Ukrainian university in Lviv, and in Prague set up a fund for poor students from Ukraine. He died in Prague on 31 January 1918.

## Why it matters today

Puliui was one of the first researchers of X-rays, which no hospital today can do without. His translation helped Ukrainians read the Bible in their own language. Since 1994 the technical university in Ternopil has borne his name.
