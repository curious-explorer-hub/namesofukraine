## The boy from the cathedral choir

Mykhailo Verbytskyi was born in 1815 in the village of Yavornyk Ruskyi near Przemyśl (now in Poland), into the family of a Greek Catholic priest. His father died early, and the boy was raised by a distant relative, Bishop Ivan Snihurskyi of Przemyśl. Mykhailo studied at the singing school of Przemyśl cathedral and sang in its choir. His music is counted in the “Przemyśl school” of composers, who were shaped by the choral concertos of [Dmytro Bortnianskyi](/en/people/dmytro-bortnianskyi/).

## A priest with a guitar

In Lviv, where he studied at the seminary, Verbytskyi took up the guitar, taught it himself and put together a collection of pieces for guitarists. He is remembered as a virtuoso who made guitar music fashionable in Galicia. He was ordained in 1850, and from 1856 he served as the parish priest of Mlyny, a village near Przemyśl.

## A song from a magazine

In 1863 the Lviv magazine Meta printed the poem Shche ne vmerla Ukraina (“Ukraine Has Not Yet Perished”). It carried no signature, so people took it for Shevchenko’s. In fact the words were by [Pavlo Chubynskyi](/en/people/pavlo-chubynskyi/) of Kyiv. In the spring of 1863, in Mlyny, Verbytskyi set the poem to music: first as a song for voice and guitar, later for choir and orchestra. It was first sung in public on 10 March 1865, at a Shevchenko evening in Przemyśl.

## Music for the stage and the church

Verbytskyi wrote music for plays, including The Soldier-Sorcerer after [Ivan Kotliarevskyi](/en/people/ivan-kotliarevskyi/), and from 1864 for the Ruthenian People’s Theatre in Lviv. He set [Taras Shevchenko](/en/people/taras-shevchenko/)’s Zapovit (“Testament”) for choir and wrote music for the Divine Liturgy and other church hymns. He died in Mlyny in 1870 and is buried near the village church.

## Why it matters today

A song a village priest wrote for the guitar became the music of Ukraine’s state anthem. In the spring of 1863, in Mlyny, Mykhailo Verbytskyi set [Pavlo Chubynskyi](/en/people/pavlo-chubynskyi/)’s poem Shche ne vmerla Ukraina to music, first for voice and guitar, later for choir and orchestra, and on 15 January 1992 that melody became the anthem of an independent state. He wrote for the stage and for the church, set [Taras Shevchenko](/en/people/taras-shevchenko/)’s Zapovit for choir and composed Galicia’s anthem too, so he wrote the tunes of two anthems. Every time Ukrainians sing their anthem, it is his music they hear, and at his grave in Mlyny they traditionally sing it.
