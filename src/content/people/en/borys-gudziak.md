## A boy from Syracuse

Borys Gudziak was born in 1960 in Syracuse, New York, to immigrants from Ukraine. He studied biology and philosophy at Syracuse University, and in 1980 went to study in Rome. There he was also a student of the Ukrainian Catholic University founded by Patriarch [Yosyf Slipyi](/en/people/yosyf-slipyi/), and grew up under his care.

## From Harvard to Lviv

Next came Harvard, where in 1992 Gudziak earned his doctorate in Slavic and Byzantine cultural and church history. That same year he moved to Lviv and founded the Institute of Church History. He led the Image of Strength project, which recorded the memories of people who kept their faith in the underground Greek Catholic Church under Soviet rule (1946–1989).

## Reviving the academy

The Theological Academy in Lviv was founded in 1928 by Metropolitan [Andrei Sheptytskyi](/en/people/andrei-sheptytskyi/), and the Soviet authorities closed it. From 1993 Gudziak chaired the commission to revive it, then became vice-rector and, in 2000, rector. In 2002 the academy became the Ukrainian Catholic University (UCU); Gudziak was its rector, and in 2013 he became its president. He was ordained a priest in 1998.

## A bishop in Paris and Philadelphia

Gudziak was consecrated a bishop in 2012. From 2013 to 2019 he led the Paris eparchy for Ukrainian Greek Catholics in France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Luxembourg and Switzerland, and in 2014 he became head of the UGCC’s Department of External Church Relations. He took an active part in the Revolution of Dignity of 2013–2014. In 2019 he was appointed Metropolitan Archbishop of Philadelphia for Ukrainian Catholics in the USA.

## Why it matters today

The Ukrainian Catholic University, which grew out of the academy of Sheptytskyi and Slipyi, is one of Gudziak’s main achievements. He is an honorary citizen of Lviv, and as of October 2026 he leads the UGCC’s Archeparchy of Philadelphia.
