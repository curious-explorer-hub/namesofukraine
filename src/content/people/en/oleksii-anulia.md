## The last day of 2022

On 31 December 2022 Russia handed 140 prisoners back to Ukraine. One of them was Oleksii Anulia from Chernihiv Oblast, who had spent ten months in captivity. Ever since, he has worked to bring home those who are still there.

## A kickboxer with a machine gun

Before the war Oleksii was a kickboxer. When the full-scale invasion began, he joined the 21st Separate Rifle Battalion as a machine gunner. In late February and early March 2022 the battalion fought for the village of Lukashivka near Chernihiv.

## Captivity

In March 2022, when Russian troops took the village, Oleksii was captured. He was held first in Chernihiv Oblast and then taken to Russia. He was freed in the New Year’s Eve exchange.

## A voice for those still held

After his treatment, Oleksii took on a new cause. He campaigns for the release of other prisoners, supports families of the missing and testifies to the war crimes of the Russian army. In July 2024 he went to Geneva for a session of the UN Human Rights Council and told a room full of diplomats about his captivity.

## A book and a prize

In 2025 Oleksii published “Jingle bellz”, a book in which he reflects on captivity, the psychological pressure, and how both prisoners and their captors behave.

On 10 December 2025 the Center for Civil Liberties gave out the Ihor Kozlovskyi Prize for the first time. It is named after a philosopher and scholar of religion who spent 700 days held captive in occupied territory. The first winner was Oleksii Anulia. He was away on an advocacy trip and spoke to the guests by video. At the same ceremony, paramedic [Yuliia “Taira” Paievska](/en/people/yuliia-paievska/) received an award from PEN Ukraine.

## Why it matters

Ukrainians are still held captive by Russia. Oleksii Anulia’s story is a reminder that each of them must be spoken about until they are brought home.
