## One role, a first time twice

Mariia Adasovska was born in 1854 in the village of Zanky in the Chernihiv region, into a noble family, and she had a fine mezzo-soprano voice. As early as 1874–1875 she acted with an amateur group in Nizhyn, and that is where she first played Natalka in [Ivan Kotliarevskyi](/en/people/ivan-kotliarevskyi/)’s Natalka Poltavka. In 1875 she married an officer (until 1888 her surname was Khlystova), lived with him in the fortresses of Bender and Sveaborg near Helsinki, and took singing lessons there.

On 27 October 1882 in Yelysavethrad (now Kropyvnytskyi) she made her professional debut with Marko Kropyvnytskyi’s troupe. The role was the same, Natalka. The name was new, a stage name: Zankovetska.

## Forty years on stage

From then on she played in the best Ukrainian troupes, those of Kropyvnytskyi, Mykhailo Starytskyi, Mykola Sadovskyi, Panas Saksahanskyi and Ivan Karpenko-Karyi. She created more than 30 roles, among them Halia in Taras Shevchenko’s Nazar Stodolia and Kharytyna in Karpenko-Karyi’s The Hired Girl. She toured with the troupes to Saint Petersburg, Moscow and other cities of the empire, and her contemporaries ranked her alongside Sarah Bernhardt and Eleonora Duse.

In 1907, together with Sadovskyi, she founded the first permanent Ukrainian theater in Kyiv, and in 1918 she led her own troupe in Nizhyn. She last appeared on stage in December 1922, at a celebration of her 40 years in the theater. In 1923 she was named People’s Artist of the Ukrainian SSR. She died in Kyiv in 1934.

## Why it matters today

Zankovetska showed that Ukrainian theater could have an actress of world rank: her contemporaries ranked her alongside Sarah Bernhardt and Eleonora Duse. People urged her to move to the Russian stage, Anton Chekhov among them, but she stayed with the Ukrainian one and even shamed him for asking. For 40 years she brought the Ukrainian classics to the stage, from [Kotliarevskyi](/en/people/ivan-kotliarevskyi/) to Karpenko-Karyi. And in 1907, with Mykola Sadovskyi, she founded the first permanent Ukrainian theater in Kyiv, giving the Ukrainian stage a home of its own. Today the Ukrainian drama theater in Lviv bears her name.
