## From a tyre shop to the front

Until 2014 Mykhailo Vershynin, a native of Donetsk, was a businessman with a tyre-fitting workshop. When russia attacked the Donbas, he realised he couldn't live under an enemy flag. He volunteered for the Ukrainian Volunteer Corps of Right Sector, took the call sign “Kit” (Ukrainian for “tomcat”) and fought near Donetsk airport, then in Sector M around Mariupol.

## Why the police, not the army

Vershynin thought about signing an army contract. But a comrade from the front, who by then was running the country's patrol police, told him: “You can't change the country from the trenches.” In spring 2016 Mykhailo became deputy chief of Mariupol's patrol police, later its chief, and after that head of the patrol police of the whole Donetsk region.

## Mariupol, 2022

From the first days of the full-scale invasion, Vershynin stood up to defend the encircled city. His officers did things nobody expects of patrol officers. “My officers evacuated women from the maternity hospital wrecked by an airstrike,” he recalled. After the shelling, free patrol crews gave first aid and drove the wounded to hospital.

Together with dozens of other police officers he defended the Azovstal steelworks until the defenders were ordered to lay down their arms. He was among the last to leave the plant and was taken prisoner in May 2022. On 17 May 2022, police captain Mykhailo Vershynin was awarded the Order of Bohdan Khmelnytskyi, 3rd class.

## Coming home

On 21 September 2022, after four months in captivity, Vershynin was freed in an exchange that brought 215 Ukrainian defenders home.

## Debates and assessments

In 2020, after four Donetsk-region patrol officers were detained on suspicion of dealing drugs and extorting bribes, Vershynin was suspended, and later a court removed him from his post in a criminal case. He was charged with acquiring and keeping ammunition without a permit: investigators from the State Bureau of Investigation had found a grenade and more than a thousand rounds in his office. Vershynin said he had grounds to see it as persecution of people “who don't fit into the system”. Mariupol residents rallied under the hashtag #НашВершинін (“Our Vershynin”), and 752 people signed an open letter asking for him to be reinstated. When the full-scale invasion began, he was once again in charge of the patrol police of Mariupol and the Donetsk region.

## Why it matters today

Mykhailo Vershynin showed that you can change a country outside the trenches too: a Donetsk businessman who had lost his home built, in a frontline city, a police force that its residents took to the streets to defend. When the big war came, that police force saved people from the shelling, and its chief stayed with the defenders of Azovstal to the end. His story is a reminder to everyone: serving people means being there when it is hardest.
