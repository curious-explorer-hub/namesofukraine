## Why “Horus”?

Horus is the ancient Egyptian god of the sky and war. That is the call sign Kyiv-born Lev Pashko chose when he volunteered for the Azov regiment in 2017, right after turning 18. Five years later he was a lieutenant and company commander.

## 86 days in Mariupol

The full-scale invasion found Pashko on the Azov Sea coast near Mariupol. On 26 March 2022 he personally destroyed an armoured personnel carrier and two enemy tanks - a battle later cited in his award decree. Days later he received the Order of Bohdan Khmelnytskyi, 3rd class, for personal courage, and on 17 April 2022, at 22, the president signed decree No. 251/2022 making him a Hero of Ukraine with the Gold Star order.

The defence of Mariupol lasted 86 straight days. Its defenders' last refuge was the Azovstal steel plant. Pashko was badly wounded in the leg during an attempted breakout. When the plant fell, he was taken prisoner, and spent four months in Russian captivity without proper medical care.

## Coming home

On 21 September 2022 Lev Pashko was freed in what was then the largest prisoner exchange of the war - more than two hundred Ukrainian service members came home with him, among them other Azovstal defenders, including [Denys Prokopenko](/en/people/denys-prokopenko/) and [Nazarii Hryntsevych](/en/people/nazarii-hryntsevych/). He spent a long time in treatment and rehabilitation afterward.

“Horus” remains one of the faces of the price Mariupol's defenders paid to hold the city against a far larger force.
