## How Pavel Zorev became Pavlo Fylypovych

Pavlo Fylypovych was born in 1891 in the village of Kaitanivka (now in the Cherkasy region), into a priest’s large family. He graduated from the Pavlo Galagan College in Kyiv in 1910 with a gold medal, and from the faculty of history and philology at Kyiv University in 1915. In 1910 he also began to publish poems, but in Russian and under the pen name “Pavel Zorev”. From 1917 on he wrote in Ukrainian.

## A poet of the “fivefold cluster”

Fylypovych was one of the Kyiv Neoclassicists, the poets around [Mykola Zerov](/en/people/mykola-zerov/) who prized classical form and the European tradition. In his sonnet The Swans (1928) [Mykhailo Drai-Khmara](/en/people/mykhailo-drai-khmara/) hailed them as a “fivefold cluster of unconquered singers”, and Fylypovych was one of the five. He published two collections, Earth and Wind (1922) and Space (1925), known for their depth of thought and polished form. He translated French poets: Baudelaire, Verlaine, Béranger. In the Literary Discussion of 1925–1928 he boldly stood up for the Neoclassicists’ views.

## A professor who read Shevchenko anew

As a scholar Fylypovych studied Taras Shevchenko, the Ukrainian classics and the ties between Ukrainian and Russian literature. His works include Shevchenko and the Decembrists (1926) and Pushkin in Ukrainian Literature (1927). He was a professor at the Kyiv Institute of People’s Education, taught modern Ukrainian literature and ran a seminar on its history together with Zerov.

## Solovki and Sandarmokh

On 5 September 1935 Fylypovych was arrested on a fabricated charge of belonging to a “counter-revolutionary nationalist organization”. He was tried together with Zerov and sentenced to 10 years of imprisonment, which he served on the Solovetsky Islands. From there he and other prisoners were taken to the Sandarmokh forest in Karelia and shot on 3 November 1937. Zerov, [Mykola Kulish](/en/people/mykola-kulish/) and [Les Kurbas](/en/people/les-kurbas/) were killed there the same day. Fylypovych was rehabilitated in 1958.

## Why it matters today

Fylypovych showed that a scholar and a poet can be the same person: after decades of bans, his poems and his essays on Shevchenko have come back to readers. Collections of his poetry came out in Munich in 1957 and in Kyiv in 1989, and his critical essays in Kyiv in 1991.
