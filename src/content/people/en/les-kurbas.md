## Who he was

Les (Oleksandr-Zenon) Kurbas was born in Galicia into a family of actors who toured with the theater of the Ruska Besida society. Most encyclopedias give his birthplace as Sambir; the Encyclopedia of the History of Ukraine, citing his Polish birth certificate, names Przemyśl. He spent his childhood with his grandfather, a priest, in the village of Staryi Skalat in the Ternopil region. Kurbas studied philosophy at the universities of Vienna and Lviv, acted in the Hutsul Theater and the Ruska Besida theater, and in 1915 founded the Ternopilski Teatralni Vechory troupe in Ternopil.

## His story

In 1916 Kurbas worked in Mykola Sadovskyi’s theater in Kyiv, and in 1917 he created the Molodyi Teatr (Young Theater). There he staged Sophocles’ Oedipus Rex in Ukrainian for the first time and opened Ukrainian theater to European modernism, from antiquity to expressionism. In 1922 Kurbas founded the Berezil artistic association, with workshops and a directors’ laboratory; in 1926 the theater moved to Kharkiv, then the capital of Soviet Ukraine. Kurbas also made several films and trained a generation of actors and directors, among them Amvrosii Buchma, Yosyp Hirniak and Nataliia Uzhvii.

Berezil’s peak came with productions of plays by Mykola Kulish, designed by Vadym Meller: The People’s Malakhii (1928), Myna Mazailo (1929) and Maklena Grasa (1933). The authorities launched a campaign of harassment against the artists. In the autumn of 1933 Kurbas was removed as head of the theater and stripped of his title of People’s Artist, and on 26 December 1933 he was arrested in Moscow in the fabricated “Ukrainian Military Organization” case. He was sentenced to 5 years in labor camps. On the White Sea–Baltic Canal and on the Solovetsky Islands he went on staging plays in camp theaters. In 1937 a special NKVD troika sentenced Kurbas to death; on 3 November 1937 he was shot at Sandarmokh in Karelia. He was rehabilitated in 1957.

## Why it matters today

Kurbas proved that Ukrainian theater could be intellectual and innovative, the equal of the best European stages. His fate became one of the symbols of the Executed Renaissance. His anniversaries are marked by decision of UNESCO, and theaters, a State Prize and the National Center of Theater Arts bear his name.
