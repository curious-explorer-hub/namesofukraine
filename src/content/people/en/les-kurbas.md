## How do you make Ukrainian theater European?

Les Kurbas knew the answer: go and see the best, then bring it home. He was born in 1887 in Galicia into a family of actors (in Sambir, according to most sources; according to his Polish birth certificate, in Przemyśl), and he spent his childhood with his grandfather, a priest, in the village of Staryi Skalat in the Ternopil region. He studied philosophy at the universities of Vienna and Lviv, acted in the Hutsul Theater and the Ruska Besida theater, and in 1914 travelled to Warsaw, Prague, Berlin and Munich to see their theaters. In 1915 he founded the Ternopilski Teatralni Vechory troupe in Ternopil.

## From the Young Theater to the Berezil

Kurbas went to Kyiv to work in Mykola Sadovskyi’s theater in 1916, and in 1917 he created the Molodyi Teatr (Young Theater). There he staged Sophocles’ Oedipus Rex in Ukrainian for the first time and opened Ukrainian theater to European modernism. In 1922 he founded the Berezil artistic association with five workshops; in 1926 the theater moved to Kharkiv, then the capital of Soviet Ukraine. Kurbas made several films and trained a generation of actors and directors, among them Amvrosii Buchma, [Yosyp Hirniak](/en/people/yosyp-hirniak/) and Nataliia Uzhvii.

Berezil’s peak came with the productions Kurbas created together with the playwright Mykola Kulish and the designer Vadym Meller: The People’s Malakhii (1928), Myna Mazailo (1929) and Maklena Grasa (1933).

## Plays even in the camp

The authorities launched a campaign of harassment against the artists. In the autumn of 1933 Kurbas was removed as head of the theater and stripped of his title of People’s Artist, and on 26 December he was arrested in Moscow in the fabricated “Ukrainian Military Organization” case. He was sentenced to 5 years in labor camps. On the White Sea–Baltic Canal and on the Solovetsky Islands he went on staging plays in camp theaters. In 1937 a special NKVD troika sentenced Kurbas to death; on 3 November 1937 he was shot at Sandarmokh in Karelia. He was rehabilitated in 1957.

## Why it matters today

Kurbas proved that Ukrainian theater could be intellectual and innovative, the equal of the best European stages. He didn’t copy others: he took the best of Europe and made it speak Ukrainian, and a whole generation of actors and directors grew out of his workshops. Even in the camp he kept staging plays, because for him art was work you don’t walk away from. Anyone who boldly renews Ukrainian art carries on his work.
