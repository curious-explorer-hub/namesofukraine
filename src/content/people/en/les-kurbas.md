## How do you make Ukrainian theater European?

Les Kurbas knew the answer: go and see the best, then bring it home. He was born in 1887 in Galicia into a family of actors (in Sambir, according to most sources; according to his Polish birth certificate, in Przemyśl), and he spent his childhood with his grandfather, a priest, in the village of Staryi Skalat in the Ternopil region. He studied philosophy at the universities of Vienna and Lviv; in 1910 he was expelled from Lviv University for taking part in a student demonstration, and went back to Vienna to study. He acted in the Hutsul Theater and the Ruska Besida theater, and in 1914 travelled to Warsaw, Prague, Berlin and Munich to see their theaters. In 1915 he founded the Ternopilski Teatralni Vechory (Ternopil Theater Evenings), regarded as the first permanent professional Ukrainian theater.

## From the Young Theater to the Berezil

Mykola Sadovskyi saw one of his productions in Ternopil and invited him to his theater in Kyiv, where Kurbas worked in 1916; in 1917 he created the Molodyi Teatr (Young Theater). [Ostap Vyshnia](/en/people/ostap-vyshnia/) later wrote: “Had Sadovskyi known what would come of that invitation, he would have leapt on his horse like Taras Bulba, drawn his sabre and cut Kurbas in two down to the saddle. Because Les Kurbas broke the whole history of our theater.” There he staged Sophocles’ Oedipus Rex in Ukrainian for the first time and opened Ukrainian theater to European modernism. In 1922 he founded the Berezil artistic association with five workshops. At its height the Berezil had nearly 400 actors and staff, a directors’ lab, a theater museum and ten specialized committees. In Jimmie Higgins he became the first in Ukraine to use film on stage, and in 1925 he brought home from Paris a gold medal of a theater exhibition for the Berezil’s stage models. In 1926 the theater moved to Kharkiv, then the capital of Soviet Ukraine. Kurbas made several films, but all of them, like most of his archive, were destroyed. He trained a generation of actors and directors, among them Amvrosii Buchma, [Yosyp Hirniak](/en/people/yosyp-hirniak/) and Nataliia Uzhvii.

Berezil’s peak came with the productions Kurbas created together with the playwright Mykola Kulish and the designer Vadym Meller: The People’s Malakhii (1928), Myna Mazailo (1929) and Maklena Grasa (1933). Maklena Grasa was the first to raise the theme of the 1933 Holodomor.

## Plays even in the camp

The authorities launched a campaign of harassment against the artists. In the autumn of 1933 Kurbas was removed as head of the theater and stripped of his title of People’s Artist. He went to Moscow to stage King Lear at the State Jewish Theater with Solomon Mikhoels, but on 26 December he was arrested there in the fabricated “Ukrainian Military Organization” case. He was sentenced to 5 years in labor camps. On the White Sea–Baltic Canal and on the Solovetsky Islands he went on staging plays in camp theaters. With the playwright [Myroslav Irchan](/en/people/myroslav-irchan/) and the Czech composer Urbanik, Kurbas created a camp operetta, A Dream on Vian-Guba, which was a real success. He himself played an old clown who is not allowed to play the piano and plays anyway. In 1937 a special NKVD troika sentenced Kurbas to death; on 3 November 1937 he was shot at Sandarmokh in Karelia. Years later his widow was handed a death certificate claiming he had died of a “brain hemorrhage” on 15 November 1942. He was rehabilitated in 1957.

## Why it matters today

Kurbas proved that Ukrainian theater could be intellectual and innovative, the equal of the best European stages. He didn’t copy others: he took the best of Europe and made it speak Ukrainian, and a whole generation of actors and directors grew out of his workshops. Even in the camp he kept staging plays, because for him art was work you don’t walk away from. Anyone who boldly renews Ukrainian art carries on his work.
