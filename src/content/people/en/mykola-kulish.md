## The orphan from Chaplynka

Mykola Kulish was born in 1892 in the village of Chaplynka in southern Ukraine (now in the Kherson region). His mother died early, and he spent most of his childhood in orphanages and charity homes, working as a farm hand from a young age. As a gymnasium student in Oleshky he was already writing satirical poems and plays.

## From the trenches to the stage

In the First World War Kulish went through the ensign school in Odesa and fought as an infantry officer in Galicia, Volhynia and Belarus; in 1917 he was wounded. After the revolution he headed the council in Oleshky. From 1922 he worked in education in the Odesa region, and in the autumn of 1925 he moved to Kharkiv.

## A play with a new ending

Fame came with his very first play, 97 (1924). But audiences didn’t see it as he wrote it: the director Hnat Yura swapped the tragic ending for an optimistic one. The most important productions of Kulish’s plays were made by [Les Kurbas](/en/people/les-kurbas/) at the Berezil theatre: The People’s Malakhii, Myna Mazailo and Maklena Grasa. Myna Mazailo is a comedy about how people took Ukrainization. It was the only one the authorities let run for long: The People’s Malakhii was banned after a few performances, and Maklena Grasa a few weeks after its dress rehearsal. In all, Kulish wrote about 15 plays.

## VAPLITE and Khvylovy

In 1924 Kulish joined the writers’ union Hart. In Kharkiv [Mykola Khvylovy](/en/people/mykola-khvylovyi/) had a great impact on his writing and views. From November 1926 to 1928 Kulish was president of the literary organization VAPLITE.

## Solovki and Sandarmokh

In June 1934 Kulish’s plays were condemned as nationalist, and he was expelled from the Party as a “counter-revolutionary”. In December 1934 he was arrested. In the fabricated case of a “Borotbist terrorist centre” he was sentenced to 10 years of imprisonment, which he served on the Solovetsky Islands. On 9 October 1937 a special NKVD troika sentenced him to death. Kulish, [Mykola Zerov](/en/people/mykola-zerov/), Les Kurbas and [Valerian Pidmohylnyi](/en/people/valerian-pidmohylnyi/) were among the Ukrainian prisoners shot at that time in the Sandarmokh forest in Karelia; Kulish was shot on 3 November 1937. His manuscripts were confiscated and most of them destroyed; the play Such Ones was lost for good. He was rehabilitated in 1956.

## Why it matters today

Kulish wrote about language, Ukrainians’ hopes and Soviet reality, and his plays are read and staged again. [Les Taniuk](/en/people/les-taniuk/) staged them in Odesa and Kharkiv, and in 1990 a two-volume edition of Kulish’s works, edited by Taniuk, came out in Kyiv.
