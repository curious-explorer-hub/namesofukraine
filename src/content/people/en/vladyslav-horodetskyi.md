## A nobleman from Podillia

Vladyslav Horodetskyi was born in 1863 in the village of Sholudky in Podillia (now the Vinnytsia region), into a Polish noble family. His full Polish name was Leszek Dezydery Władysław Horodecki. He studied at the Academy of Arts in St Petersburg and graduated in 1890. He lived and worked in Ukraine for most of his life, above all in Kyiv.

## The Kyiv he built

At the end of the 19th century Kyiv was growing fast, and Horodetskyi became one of its most prominent architects. He designed the City Museum of Antiquities and Arts in a Neo-Greek style (1897–1900); the patron [Nikola Tereshchenko](/en/people/nikola-tereshchenko/) gave large sums towards the building. Today it is the National Art Museum of Ukraine. For the Karaite community Horodetskyi built a kenesa in a Moorish style (now the House of Actors), and for Catholics the Neo-Gothic Church of St Nicholas. He also worked far beyond Kyiv: in Cherkasy, Uman, Simferopol and Yevpatoriia. On top of that, he designed jewellery, women’s dresses, theatre costumes and sets.

## The House with Chimaeras

His strangest building stands on a steep slope at 10 Bankova Street. From Bankova it has three storeys; from the other side, six. The façades are covered with sculptures of elephants, rhinos, lions, antelopes and eagles, and giant frogs sit on the roof. The Art Nouveau house was built in 1901–1903, and its sculpted decoration was made by Elio Sala.

## Hunter and traveller

Horodetskyi went on long hunting expeditions to the South Caucasus, Central Asia and the Altai, and gave his trophies to the Kyiv Hunting Museum, which he had helped to found. His farthest trip took him to Africa, to Kenya, where he hunted at the foot of Kilimanjaro.

## Warsaw and Tehran

In 1920 Horodetskyi emigrated to Poland. There he worked at the Ministry of Public Works and restored the Vyshnevetskyi Palace in Vyshnivets, in today’s Ternopil region. From 1928 he was chief architect of the syndicate building railways in Persia. In Tehran he designed the Shah’s palace, a railway station, a theatre and a hotel. Horodetskyi died in Tehran on 3 January 1930.

## Why it matters today

Horodetskyi’s buildings still shape the centre of Kyiv: the National Art Museum works in his museum building, and people still come to the House with Chimaeras to look at its stone animals. In 2004 a monument to him was unveiled in Kyiv, and a street there bears his name.
