## Who he was

Anatolii Solovianenko was born in 1932 in Stalino (now Donetsk) into a coal miner’s family. As a child he loved the singing of the tenor Ivan Kozlovsky. He graduated from the mining faculty of the Donetsk Polytechnic Institute (1954), taught descriptive geometry and technical drawing there in 1954–1962, and at the same time took private singing lessons from O. Korobeichenko, a soloist of the Donetsk opera.

## His story

In 1962 Solovianenko sang in Kyiv at a showcase concert of amateur performers from the Donetsk region, and that same year he was taken on by the Kyiv Theatre of Opera and Ballet. Winning an all-Union competition for young performers earned him a traineeship at La Scala in Milan, where in 1963–1965 he studied with maestro G. Barra. In 1964 he sang on the La Scala stage in a concert by the visiting Bolshoi Theatre company and took third place at a singing contest in Naples. At the opening of Expo 67 in Montreal, alongside arias by Tchaikovsky, Verdi and Puccini, he sang the Ukrainian songs “Povii, Vitre, na Vkrainu” (Blow, Wind, to Ukraine) and “Dyvlius Ya na Nebo” (I Look at the Sky).

In 1977–1978 Solovianenko was a soloist of the Metropolitan Opera in New York, where he sang Turiddu in Mascagni’s Cavalleria rusticana, the Duke in Verdi’s Rigoletto and the Italian Singer in Strauss’s Der Rosenkavalier. On the Kyiv stage he sang the Duke, Alfredo, Rodolfo, Cavaradossi, Lensky, Andrii in Hulak-Artemovskyi’s Zaporozhian Cossack beyond the Danube and Petro in [Mykola Lysenko](/en/people/mykola-lysenko/)’s Natalka Poltavka. He gave thousands of concerts of opera arias, romances and Ukrainian folk songs and recorded 18 LPs. In 1978 he received his diploma from the Kyiv Conservatory, and in 1992, after a conflict with the theatre’s management of the day, he left the company and concentrated on concerts.

Anatolii Solovianenko died on 29 July 1999 in the settlement of Kozyn near Kyiv and is buried there. In 2008 he was posthumously made a Hero of Ukraine.

## Why it matters today

Already in Soviet times Solovianenko sang at La Scala and the Metropolitan Opera, and all his life he championed Ukrainian song and romance. In 1999 the Donetsk Theatre of Opera and Ballet was named after him; a museum in his memory opened in Kozyn in 2001, and the minor planet 6755 bears his name.
