## Seventeen and gold

Can you be the best in the world at 17? On 20 September 2018 in Baku, 17-year-old Daria Bilodid stepped onto the mat at the World Championships in the under-48 kg class. In the semi-final she beat the Olympic champion, Argentina's Paula Pareto, and in the final the reigning world champion, Japan's Funa Tonaki. That made Bilodid the youngest world champion in judo history and the first Ukrainian woman to win the title. After the final she ran off the mat in tears and into the arms of her mother, who was coaching her from the side of the mat.

## Judo in the family

Daria Bilodid was born on 10 October 2000 in Kyiv. Her coaches were her own parents: she is the daughter and pupil of Hennadii Bilodid and of her mother. In 2017, at 16, she won the European Championships in Warsaw. In the 2018 season, before Baku, she had already won five events on the International Judo Federation's World Tour.

## A second title and an Olympic bronze

In 2019 in Tokyo Bilodid became world champion again, and the International Judo Federation named her the best female judoka of the 2019–2020 season. At the Tokyo Olympics in 2021 she won a bronze medal.

## A new weight

Later Bilodid moved up to the under-57 kg class, and in 2024 in Zagreb she became European champion for the second time. At the Paris Olympics she lost in the round of 16 and shared ninth place. After Paris she took a break from competition and tried football: in April 2025 she made her debut for Kharkiv's Metalist 1925. In August 2026 she returned to training with Ukraine's national judo team.

## Why it matters today

Bilodid proved that a Ukrainian can be the best in the world at 17 and beat people who had been at the top for years. She was the first Ukrainian woman to raise Ukraine's flag over a world judo championship, and she showed girls that this sport is for them too. Her path began with her parents as coaches, a reminder that champions are made by daily work and the support of people close to them. For anyone just stepping onto the mat, her story is proof that age is no barrier to great wins.
