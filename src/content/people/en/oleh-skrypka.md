## A Ukrainian found in France

Can you feel Ukrainian far away from Ukraine? Oleh Skrypka says that is exactly what happened to him: he first felt Ukrainian in France. In 1990 his band Vopli Vidopliassova toured France and Switzerland and was written up in Le Monde, and from 1991 to 1996 the musicians lived and worked in France.

## From the Polytechnic to the rock club

Oleh Skrypka was born in 1964 in Tajikistan, to a family of Ukrainian settlers: his father was a doctor and his mother a kindergarten teacher. He spent his childhood in Russia. In 1986 he graduated from the radio engineering faculty of the Kyiv Polytechnic Institute, and that same year the band Vopli Vidopliassova was formed in Kyiv. Skrypka sang and played button accordion, guitar and trumpet. In 1987 the band made its debut at the Kyiv Rock Club, won the Rock Parade festival with the song “Tantsi” (Dances) and was voted band of the year by listeners.

## “Vesna” and the return

In 1996 the musicians came back to Ukraine. In 1997 the video for “Vesna” (Spring) came out; the song became a smash hit and marked the band’s return to the Ukrainian stage. That same year came the show “Ve.Ve. and the Army for Eternal Spring!” with the Song and Dance Ensemble of the Armed Forces of Ukraine. The albums Kraina Mrii (1997), Liubov (1998), Mamai (2001) and Faino (2002) followed.

## The Kraina Mrii festival

In 2004 Skrypka organized the first Kraina Mrii ethnic festival, named like the band’s 1997 album. The festival works with folk traditions and invites visitors not just to listen but to join in. Skrypka also started the Rock-Sich rock festival. In 2005 he turned down the title of Honoured Artist of Ukraine, awarded to him for Independence Day, in protest against honours being handed out too freely.

## Why it matters today

Skrypka is called a legend of Ukrainian ethno-rock: in his songs and at Kraina Mrii, folk tradition sounds modern. After the full-scale invasion began, his family stayed in Kyiv. In 2024 he marked his 60th birthday with a jubilee concert in Kyiv with the NAONI folk orchestra, collecting charity donations “for Victory.”
