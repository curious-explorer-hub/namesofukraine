## From Liberia to Mariupol

Did you know that long before the full-scale war, Yurii Tymus flew a Mi-8 helicopter on peacekeeping missions in Liberia and Congo? That experience mattered in spring 2022, when helicopters were breaking through to encircled Mariupol.

## A boy from near Brody

Yurii Tymus was born on 2 May 1987 in the village of Ditkivtsi near Brody in Lviv region. In 2008 he graduated from the Air Force University in Kharkiv and went on to serve with the 16th Brody Army Aviation Brigade. He became a Mi-8 helicopter commander, a first-class pilot and an instructor, and from 2014 he flew combat missions in eastern Ukraine.

## The sky over Kyiv

From 24 February 2022 Tymus defended Kyiv. In February and March he flew 28 combat sorties, striking Russian armour heading for the capital. On 10 March 2022 he was awarded the Order For Courage, 3rd class.

## The run to Mariupol

By late March Mariupol had been encircled for weeks. Army Aviation helicopters broke through to the city, carrying volunteers, weapons, medicine and food in and flying the wounded out. On 31 March Yurii Tymus flew the fifth of seven such runs. On the way back, with badly wounded soldiers on board, his helicopter was hit by a Russian missile near the village of Rybatske. The 34-year-old commander was killed together with navigator Denys Badika and flight engineer Ivan Vakhovskyi. For more than two years Yurii was listed as missing; in 2024 he was buried in Brody, and on 30 September 2024 he was made a Hero of Ukraine, the country’s highest title, posthumously.

## Why it matters today

When Mariupol was under siege, helicopters linked its defenders with the rest of Ukraine, and Yurii Tymus knew what he was taking on each time he lifted off. He flew so that the defenders would get weapons and medicine, and the wounded a chance to live. His service, from peacekeeping missions to the skies over Kyiv and Mariupol, shows that Ukrainian pilots put themselves at risk for those who cannot be left behind.
