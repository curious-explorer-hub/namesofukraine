## From Verbivka to Paris

What do peasant embroiderers from a village in central Ukraine have in common with the cubists of Paris? Alexandra Exter. She was a friend of Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque, and for years she also kept going back to the village of Verbivka, home to an embroidery workshop. Exter carried the bright colours of Ukrainian folk art into cubism, which until then had been almost monochrome.

## A Kyivan from Białystok

Alexandra Grigorovich was born in 1882 in Białystok, now in Poland. Her wealthy family soon moved to Kyiv, and that is where she grew up. At the Kyiv Art School she studied under Mykola Pymonenko alongside Oleksandr Bohomazov and [Alexander Archipenko](/en/people/oleksandr-arkhypenko/). She married her cousin, the Kyiv lawyer Mykola Exter, and took his name. In the late 1900s she studied in Paris and got to know Picasso and the poet Guillaume Apollinaire. The Exters' home in Kyiv became a salon for artists, poets and actors.

## An avant-garde with folk roots

Exter did more than admire folk art: she helped revive it. In 1915–1916 women in the villages of Verbivka (now in Cherkasy region) and Skoptsi (now in Kyiv region) embroidered and wove to designs by avant-garde artists, among them [Kazimir Malevich](/en/people/kazymyr-malevych/).

## The stage as a painting

In 1916 Exter became chief designer of Alexander Tairov's Chamber Theatre in Moscow. Her sets and costumes for Thamira Khytharedes, Salome and Romeo and Juliet made her name far beyond Moscow.

## The studio that trained a generation

In 1918 Exter opened a studio in Kyiv. Her students studied Picasso, Matisse and Cézanne alongside Ukrainian folk art. Among them were Anatol Petrytskyi, Pavel Tchelitchew, Oleksandr Khvostenko-Khvostov and Vadym Meller, who later became chief designer of [Les Kurbas](/en/people/les-kurbas/)'s Berezil theatre. That same year Exter was one of the organisers of the First All-Ukrainian Congress of Cultural Figures under Hetman [Pavlo Skoropadskyi](/en/people/pavlo-skoropadskyi/). In 1919–1920 she taught in Odesa, then moved to Moscow.

## A one-way ticket

In 1924 Exter designed the costumes for the film Aelita and went to Venice for the Biennale. She never came home: she settled outside Paris and taught at Fernand Léger's academy. Alexandra Exter died in Fontenay-aux-Roses on 17 March 1949, aged 67. She left her works and archive to a fellow Kyivan, the artist Simon Lissim, so much of her legacy ended up in the United States.

## Why it matters today

Exter showed that the avant-garde can grow out of folk art: she carried the colours of Verbivka embroidery into cubism and onto the stages of Europe. Her Kyiv studio trained the artists who built a new Ukrainian theatre, including the stage design of the Berezil. For a long time she was filed among russian artists, and reclaiming her name is part of Ukraine reclaiming its own culture. Anyone looking for their own voice in art can learn from her: be modern without cutting your roots.
