## “Friend, get back in the ranks, fast”

Did you know that Mykyta Nadtochii was put in charge of Azov by a call on Telegram? He was still in rehabilitation after a serious wound when Azov’s commander [Denys Prokopenko](/en/people/denys-prokopenko/) rang him. Talks were under way on getting the defenders out of Mariupol, and Prokopenko said: “Friend, get back in the ranks, fast. Your task will be to take temporary command of the regiment while I’m away.”

## A student from Cherkasy

Mykyta Nadtochii was born in Cherkasy. He joined Azov in September 2014, while still a student at Cherkasy National University, where he studied at the institute of international relations, history and philosophy. He went on to graduate.

## The 2nd battalion in Mariupol

When the full-scale invasion began in 2022, Nadtochii commanded Azov’s 2nd battalion in Mariupol and was one of those who planned the city’s defence. In March he was badly wounded by shrapnel and became one of the first soldiers flown out of the encircled city by helicopter.

## A commander without commanders

While Azov’s main force was encircled and then held captive, Nadtochii, as acting commander, put the unit back together: with fighters who had been flown out of Mariupol and with new volunteers. “The task was simple: get back in the ranks fast. Take everything into your own hands,” he recalled. Later Azov’s chief of staff [Bohdan Krotevych](/en/people/bohdan-krotevych/) came home from captivity and set about rebuilding the unit. Nadtochii stayed in Azov’s leadership: from July 2023 he was deputy commander of the 12th Brigade, and from April 2025 deputy commander of the National Guard’s 1st Azov Corps. “You need people who are simply ready to fight… People decide,” Nadtochii believes.

## Why it matters today

When Azov’s commanders were taken prisoner, the unit could have disappeared. Mykyta Nadtochii, still recovering from his wound, did not let that happen: he gathered people and made them a fighting unit again. His story shows that an army is held up not only by those on the front line but also by those who can rebuild it after the heaviest losses.
