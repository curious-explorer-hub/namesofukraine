## A voice from Hlukhiv

Did you know that the “Ukrainian Mozart” had the same teacher as the real one? But it all began in Hlukhiv, then the hetmans’ capital, where Maksym Berezovskyi was born to a Cossack family in 1745. He studied at the Hlukhiv singing school and at the Kyiv Academy, where he began to compose, and he sang in the choir and orchestra of Hetman [Kyrylo Rozumovskyi](/en/people/kyrylo-rozumovskyi/). Even so, the basic facts of his life are still in doubt, and no portrait of Berezovskyi has ever been found.

## A singer at the imperial court

In 1758 the young man became a singer in the court choir at Oranienbaum near Saint Petersburg, and in 1759–1760 he performed as a soloist with the Italian opera company. At the same time he studied composition with the Italian maestro Francesco Zoppis.

## The Bologna academician

Later Berezovskyi went to study in Italy. In Bologna he was taught by Padre Giovanni Battista Martini, who also taught the young Mozart. In 1771 Berezovskyi earned the title of academician-composer and became a member of the Bologna Philharmonic Academy. In 1773 his opera Demofonte was staged in Livorno, the first opera written by a Ukrainian composer.

## The return

In the mid-1770s Berezovskyi returned to Saint Petersburg, but his talent was not valued there. He died in 1777, aged 31. The circumstances of his death are not known for certain; according to a widespread version, he took his own life. Most of his works have been lost.

## Why it matters today

Berezovskyi was the first representative of the early Classical style in Ukrainian music and one of the creators of the Ukrainian choral style in sacred music. His best-known concerto, Do Not Forsake Me in My Old Age, is rooted in the Kyivan church chant tradition and folk song. His Symphony in C major was found in the early 2000s, and in 2020 two more symphonies “came back” from Paris. In 2005 a memorial plaque in his honour was put up on the front of the Bologna academy.
