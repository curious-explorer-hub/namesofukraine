## Who he was

Ilya Mechnikov was born on the Panasivka estate (now the village of Ivanivka in the Kupiansk district of Kharkiv Oblast), then in the Kharkiv gubernia of the Russian Empire. In 1864 he graduated from Kharkiv University as an external student, then trained in Germany and Italy, where he studied the embryology of invertebrates. He defended his doctoral dissertation in zoology at Saint Petersburg University (1868).

## His story

From 1870 to 1882 Mechnikov was professor of zoology and comparative anatomy at Novorossiya University in Odesa (now the Odesa I. I. Mechnikov National University). In 1882 he described phagocytosis, the ability of the body’s cells to engulf and digest microbes, and on that basis developed the phagocytic theory of immunity. In 1886, together with Mykola Hamaliia and Yakiv Bardakh, he founded in Odesa the first bacteriological station in the Russian Empire, and the second in the world, to fight infectious diseases.

In 1887, after conflicts with the local medical establishment, Mechnikov left Odesa, and from 1888, at Louis Pasteur’s invitation, he worked in Paris. At the Pasteur Institute he headed a laboratory for the rest of his life and from 1905 was deputy director. In 1908 he shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Paul Ehrlich. He died in Paris in 1916.

## Why it matters today

Mechnikov’s phagocytic theory is one of the foundations of modern immunology. Odesa University and a research institute in Odesa that grew out of his bacteriological station bear his name, and a monument to him was unveiled in Kharkiv in 2005.
