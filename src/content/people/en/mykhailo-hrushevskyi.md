## Whose history is it?

For a long time the history of the Ukrainian lands was told as part of Russia’s. In his ten-volume History of Ukraine-Rus’, Mykhailo Hrushevskyi showed something else: Ukrainian history runs unbroken from Kyivan Rus’ to modern times, separate from Russia’s. That idea became the foundation of Ukrainian historical scholarship.

## A professor in Lviv

Hrushevskyi was born in 1866 in the town of Kholm (now Chełm in Poland) into the family of an educator and studied at Kyiv University. In 1894 he took over a chair at Lviv University that became, in effect, the first chair of Ukrainian history. Under his leadership the Shevchenko Scientific Society grew into a true academy of sciences.

## From autonomy to independence

In 1917 Hrushevskyi was elected head of the Ukrainian Central Rada (council). Under his leadership the Rada first proclaimed autonomy, and then, on 22 January 1918, by its Fourth Universal (proclamation), the independence of the Ukrainian People’s Republic.

In 1919 he emigrated, and in 1924 he returned to Soviet Ukraine. In 1931 he was forced to move to Moscow and arrested; after his release he lived under surveillance. In 1934 Hrushevskyi died suddenly in Kislovodsk after a minor operation, and the circumstances of his death still arouse suspicion.

## Why it matters today

Hrushevskyi gave Ukrainians a scholarly history of their own people and led the Rada that proclaimed the Ukrainian People’s Republic. The Verkhovna Rada, Ukraine’s parliament, stands on a street named after him.
