## The Bayraktar money that became a satellite

In June 2022 Serhii Prytula’s foundation and the blogger Ihor Lachenkov launched a drive for three Bayraktar TB2 strike drones. In three days Ukrainians donated more than 600 million hryvnias. Then came the surprise: the Turkish maker, Baykar, refused the money and gave Ukraine the three drones for free. The money didn’t vanish. The foundation bought Ukrainian intelligence a satellite and access to ICEYE’s database of satellite images. By early September 2022 the Bayraktars were in Ukraine.

## From Zbarazh to the airwaves

Serhii Prytula was born in 1981 in Zbarazh, in the Ternopil region. He finished school with a gold medal, went to the Ternopil Academy of National Economy, and in 1999 went on air at a Ternopil radio station as “Siryi”. In 2005 he moved to Kyiv. Ukrainians know him as the host and actor of the Variaty comedy show, and from 2017 he hosted the national selection for Eurovision.

## A volunteer since 2014

When the war began in 2014, Prytula became a volunteer: he collected money and supplies for soldiers and travelled to areas near the front. By early 2020 he had given the army about 40 million hryvnias, his own and donated. In 2019 he was awarded the Order of Merit, 3rd class.

## A try at politics

In 2019 Prytula joined the Holos (Voice) party and ran for parliament on its list, but wasn’t elected. In August 2020 he announced he would run for mayor of Kyiv for Holos. On 7 June 2021 he left the party, citing disagreements with its leadership.

## The people’s fundraisers of the full-scale war

After 24 February 2022 Prytula took charge of his charitable foundation again; it raises money for weapons, equipment and humanitarian aid. The Bayraktar drive was one of its best known. Another came in October 2022: after a massive Russian missile strike on Ukrainian cities, Prytula and [Serhii Sternenko](/en/people/serhii-sternenko/) launched a fundraiser for “revenge”. The goal was 100 million hryvnias; in a day people gave 352 million. The money bought 142 strike drones, some of them able to fly hundreds of kilometres. In August 2022 Prytula was awarded the Order of Merit, 2nd class, as a volunteer and the founder of the foundation.

## Why it matters today

Serhii Prytula’s story shows that a country’s defence rests not only on soldiers but on millions of people who donate every day. His fundraisers turned small contributions into drones, satellite images and equipment for the front. They prove that trust in volunteers can bring a whole country together around one goal in a matter of days. Anyone who donates today is part of that defence.
