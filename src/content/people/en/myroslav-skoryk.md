## A twist, the Shadows and the Melody

Did you know that the first Ukrainian twist was written by the same composer whose Melody orchestras in Poland, the United States and elsewhere played in the spring of 2022 to stand with Ukraine? His name was Myroslav Skoryk.

## The out-of-tune piano

Skoryk was born in Lviv in 1938. His grandmother was the sister of the opera singer [Solomiia Krushelnytska](/en/people/solomiia-krushelnytska/). When he was about six, the boy noticed that the piano in her home was out of tune. The singer realised he had perfect pitch and told his parents to send him to music school. In 1947 or 1948 (sources differ) the Soviet authorities deported the family to Anzhero-Sudzhensk in Siberia. Myroslav got back to Lviv only after Stalin died.

## Jazz for Soviet pop

In 1963, as a young lecturer at the Lviv Conservatory, Skoryk founded the ensemble Veseli skrypky (Merry Fiddles) and wrote songs to jazz, tango, blues and bossa nova rhythms. His Ne topchit konvalii (Don't Trample the Lilies of the Valley) is called the first Ukrainian twist, and Namaliui meni nich (Paint Me a Night) and other songs became hits.

## Hutsul Shadows

In 1964, aged 26, Skoryk wrote the score for [Serhii Paradzhanov](/en/people/serhii-paradzhanov/)'s film Shadows of Forgotten Ancestors. It grew into his Hutsul Triptych. He later moved to Kyiv and taught composition at the conservatory for more than twenty years. He completed and orchestrated On Mermaid Easter, the opera that [Mykola Leontovych](/en/people/mykola-leontovych/) did not live to finish because he was murdered.

## The Melody in A minor

In the early 1980s the director Volodymyr Denysenko asked Skoryk to score his film Vysokyi pereval (High Pass). That was where the Melody in A minor was first heard; it became the composer's best-known work.

## Music that remembers

After 1991 Skoryk wrote the symphonic poem 1933 in memory of the Holodomor victims, the opera Moses after [Ivan Franko](/en/people/ivan-franko/)'s poem, which Pope John Paul II blessed, and the Penitential Psalm in memory of the Heavenly Hundred killed on the Maidan. He died on 1 June 2020, aged 81, and is buried at Lychakiv Cemetery in Lviv. In March 2022, after russia's full-scale invasion, the Society of Polish Philharmonics called on orchestras around the world to play his Melody in solidarity with Ukraine.

## Why it matters today

Skoryk proved that Ukrainian music can be folk and modern at once: Hutsul tunes, jazz and the symphony spoke one language in his hands. He brought his predecessors' music back to the stage and trained a generation of Ukrainian composers. His works keep the memory of the Holodomor and the Maidan alive. And the Melody, born in a Soviet film, became Ukraine's voice in the world's concert halls in 2022.
