## Who he was

Volodymyr Boiko was born in 1938 in Mariupol. After school, in 1955, he joined the Illich steel plant (the city was then called Zhdanov) as a pipe fitter and, after a few years in other jobs and in the army, stayed with it all his life. In 1970 he graduated from the Zhdanov Metallurgical Institute. At the plant he rose from worker to head of the plate rolling shop, and in 1987 he became deputy director for production.

## His story

In 1990 Boiko became the plant’s general director, and from 1997, when it became a joint-stock company, he chaired its board. Under his leadership the 1700 plate mill and the cold rolling shop were rebuilt, and a continuous casting machine was put into service in the converter shop. In 1999 he received the State Prize of Ukraine in Science and Technology, and in 2003 the title Hero of Ukraine.

Boiko was an unpaid adviser to President Leonid Kravchuk (1993–1994) and a member of the Donetsk regional council. In 2002 he was elected to parliament for the For a United Ukraine! bloc. In parliament he changed factions several times, among them the Regions of Ukraine faction; in 2006 he was elected on the Socialist Party list, and in 2012 on the Party of Regions list. He died in Mariupol on 10 June 2015.

## Debates and assessments

The plant’s path from state to private ownership has been disputed. In 2000 parliament passed a special law on privatizing the state’s block of shares in the plant, and the stake was bought by Illich-Steel, a company set up by the workforce. In 2007 the Constitutional Court began to consider whether this law was constitutional. Mykhailo Pozhyvanov, a former mayor of Mariupol and a member of parliament, called the law a “lobbying conspiracy” and said Illich-Steel had unlawfully taken over state property, buying the stake for practically its face value. Boiko replied that the privatization was lawful and that reversing it would mean cancelling all privatization in Ukraine.

In 2010 a conflict broke out over who owned the plant. Representatives of Cypriot companies said that a year earlier a Russian financial and industrial group had bought all the shares of Illich-Steel, which owned over 90 percent of the plant. Boiko denied the sale and said the plant’s owners were its 37,000 workers. The plant’s press office said the deal had been secretly drawn up by “an unscrupulous broker”, while Mykhailo Brodskyi, head of the State Committee for Entrepreneurship, called the sale documents genuine. That same year the shareholders approved merging the plant into Rinat Akhmetov’s Metinvest holding. The deal closed in 2011: the plant joined Metinvest, Boiko’s company received 5 percent of the holding, which experts valued at 1–1.2 billion dollars, and Boiko himself stayed on as the plant’s general director.

## Why it matters today

Boiko’s story shows how large Soviet plants turned into private companies in independent Ukraine. In 2008, in the TV project Great Ukrainians, where the ranking was decided by viewers’ votes, he came 50th.
