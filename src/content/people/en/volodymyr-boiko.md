## From pipe fitter to director

In 1955 Volodymyr Boiko, a young man from Mariupol (then called Zhdanov), joined the Illich steelworks as a pipe fitter. Thirty-five years later, in 1990, he became general director of the plant, and from 1997, when it became a joint-stock company, he chaired its board.

His whole career lies between those dates. After a few years in other jobs and military service he came back to the plant, graduated from the Zhdanov Metallurgical Institute in 1970 and worked his way up to head of the sheet-rolling shop and deputy director for production. Under his leadership the plant rebuilt its 1700 sheet-rolling mill and cold-rolling shop and brought a continuous casting machine into service. In 1999 he received the State Prize of Ukraine in Science and Technology, and in 2003 the title Hero of Ukraine.

## A director in politics

Boiko was an unofficial adviser to President Leonid Kravchuk and a member of the Donetsk regional council. In 2002 he was elected to the Verkhovna Rada, and twice more after that: in 2006 on the Socialist Party list and in 2012 on the Party of Regions list. He died in Mariupol on 10 June 2015.

## Debates and assessments

The plant’s road into private hands was disputed. In 2000 parliament passed a special law on privatizing the state’s stake in the plant: it was to be sold to a company founded by the plant’s workers, at no less than nominal value. In 2010 representatives of Cypriot companies claimed they had bought a controlling stake on behalf of an unnamed Russian group; Boiko denied it. That same year the plant announced a merger with Rinat Akhmetov’s Metinvest holding. The deal was completed in 2011: Boiko’s company received 5% of the holding, which experts valued at 1–1.2 billion dollars, and he kept his post as general director.

## Why it matters today

Boiko’s story shows how big Soviet factories turned into private companies in independent Ukraine, and how much controversy that caused.
