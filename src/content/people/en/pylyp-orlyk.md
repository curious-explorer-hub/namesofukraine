## Mazepa’s right hand

Pylyp Orlyk was born in 1672 in the village of Kosuta in the Vilnius region (now Belarus) into a noble family. He studied at the Jesuit academy in Vilnius and in 1694 graduated from the Kyiv-Mohyla Collegium. He worked in the chancellery of the Kyiv Metropolitanate, then in the General Military Chancellery of the Hetmanate, and rose to general chancellor, the closest aide of Hetman [Ivan Mazepa](/en/people/ivan-mazepa/). He also wrote poetry, including panegyrics in Mazepa’s honor.

## A hetman who limited his own power

After the defeat at Poltava in 1709, Orlyk retreated with Mazepa to Ottoman territory, to Bender. After Mazepa’s death, on 16 April 1710, the Cossack officers and the Zaporozhians elected him hetman. On the same day he signed the Pacts and Constitutions of the Rights and Liberties of the Zaporozhian Host, of which he was the chief author. The document limited the hetman’s power, provided for regular General Councils of officers and delegates from the regiments and Zaporizhzhia, and secured the rights of the Cossacks.

## Thirty years abroad

Orlyk never managed to win Ukraine back: his campaigns into Right-Bank Ukraine with the Tatars and Zaporozhians ended in failure. Decades of exile followed: in 1714 he moved to Sweden, in 1720 to Silesia, and in 1721 to the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. From 1722 to 1734 he lived in Salonika under Ottoman supervision, then in the Budjak and Moldavia. All this time he corresponded with European courts, trying to build a coalition against Russia. He died in 1742 in Iași; his burial place is unknown.

## Why it matters today

Orlyk was one of the first Ukrainian émigré politicians who for decades represented the cause of Ukrainian statehood in Europe. His Constitution, although it never took effect, is an early Ukrainian vision of power limited by law and accountable to a council.
