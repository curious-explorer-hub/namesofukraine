## Deeper than any woman

How deep can a person dive on one breath? In May 2026, in Sharm el-Sheikh, Egypt, Nataliia Zharkova went down to 112 metres in Constant Weight with Bifins (CWTB): down and back up on two fins alone, with no air tank. It was a new women's world record. She dedicated the dive to the students she had trained with: "This is one of the most beautiful parts of teaching: growing while teaching others."

## From a pool in Kharkiv

Nataliia Zharkova was born on 24 January 1989 in Kharkiv. She started swimming at seven but left the pool at 17. After a two-year break she tried freediving, and ten years later she was a world champion. Along the way she earned a master's degree in architecture at the Kharkiv National University of Construction and Architecture. In her home city she opened a freediving school.

In 2018, at the CMAS World Championships in Kaş, Turkey, Zharkova set two world records.

## A record on Roatán

Freediving has two international federations, AIDA and CMAS, and each keeps its own records. In 2025, at the CMAS World Championships in Greece, Zharkova became world champion in Free Immersion (FIM), where the diver pulls herself down and up a rope by hand, and shared the bifins gold with Italy's Alessia Zecchini.

On 20 August 2026, at the CMAS World Championships on the Honduran island of Roatán, she won Free Immersion again, at 107 metres. It was a new CMAS world record: the previous one, 104 metres, had been held by Zecchini since 2024. Second place went to another Ukrainian, [Kateryna Sadurska](/en/people/kateryna-sadurska/).

## Why it matters today

Zharkova went from an ordinary swimming pool in Kharkiv to world records at depths only a handful of people ever reach. Her records in both international federations put Ukraine on the map in a sport where the world's best divers compete, and at the World Championships two Ukrainian women now stand side by side on the podium. She opened her school at home to pass on what she knows. For anyone with a big goal, her story is proof that the way there is years of patient work, step by step.
