## Six metres

No pole vaulter in the world had cleared 6 metres until Serhii Bubka did it in Paris on 13 July 1985. He hadn't even planned to compete there: he was passing through Paris on his way to a meet in Nice and decided to jump only that morning. He won the competition at 5.70 m and, instead of his usual 5.90, went straight to 6 metres. He missed his first two attempts and cleared the bar on his last. The French compared the jump to Gagarin's flight into space. That same year Bubka was named the best athlete in the world.

He called 13 his lucky number: four of his world records, the 6 metres among them, fell on the 13th, and in Donetsk he lived on the 13th floor for many years. In all he set world records 35 times: 17 outdoors (best 6.14 m, Sestriere, 1994; that record won him a Ferrari worth about 120,000 dollars) and 18 indoors (6.15 m, Donetsk, 1993). Both records stood for more than 20 years. From 1990 Donetsk also hosted the Pole Vault Stars international meet, which Bubka helped to found; it was not held from 2015. His name went into the Guinness Book of Records for the most world records in athletics.

## Six gold medals

Bubka was born in 1963 in Luhansk. He took up the pole vault in 1973 in his home town under coach Vitalii Petrov, and from 1979 he trained in Donetsk. In 1983, aged 19, he won the first-ever World Athletics Championships as an outsider, and then five more in a row, through 1997. No other athlete has won six world titles in the same individual event.

The Olympics were less kind. In 1988 in Seoul Bubka became Olympic champion, but in 1984 the USSR boycotted the Games, in 1992 his tactics let him down, and in 1996 and 2000 injuries got in the way. In 2001 he became the first athlete to be made a Hero of Ukraine.

## After the sport

Bubka was elected a member of the International Olympic Committee in 1999 and re-elected for another eight years in 2024. From 2005 to 2022 he headed the National Olympic Committee of Ukraine, and from 2007 to 2023 he was a vice-president of the International Association of Athletics Federations (now World Athletics). In 2002–2006 he was a member of parliament (elected on the list of the For a United Ukraine! bloc, later in the Party of Regions faction), and in 2010–2014 an unpaid adviser to President Viktor Yanukovych.

## Debates and assessments

In 2023 journalists at Bihus.Info reported that Serhii Bubka co-owned companies registered in russia, among them Monblan, which sells fuel in the occupied part of the Donetsk region. The SBU opened a criminal case on aiding the aggressor state. His brother Vasyl, Monblan’s general director, became a suspect in the case over trade with the occupiers; these reports do not say that Serhii Bubka himself was served a notice of suspicion. Serhii Bubka said he has nothing to do with any business in the occupied territories. On 26 February 2026 the skeleton racer [Vladyslav Heraskevych](/en/people/vladyslav-heraskevych/) called in the Verkhovna Rada for Bubka to be stripped of the title Hero of Ukraine. In April 2026 the Minister of Youth and Sports, Matvii Bidnyi, said he saw no legal grounds for this: such a decision would be possible if the facts in the reports were proven and assessed by law enforcement.

## Why it matters today

The boy who started vaulting in Luhansk and trained in Donetsk set the bar for the whole world for years. His 6 metres became a new horizon for pole vaulters, and his six world titles in a row, which no other athlete has matched, a yardstick for a long career at the top. Any young athlete in Ukraine can see in him proof that you can raise the world’s bar yourself.
