## Who he is

Sergey (Serhii) Bubka was born in Luhansk, in eastern Ukraine, in 1963. He took up the pole vault in his home town in 1973 under coach Vitalii Petrov and from 1979 trained in Donetsk. In 1983, aged 19, he won a surprise victory at the very first World Athletics Championships.

## His story

In Paris in 1985 Bubka became the first man in the world to clear 6 metres. In all he set world records 35 times: 17 outdoors (his best, 6.14 m, in Sestriere in 1994) and 18 indoors (6.15 m, in Donetsk in 1993). Both records stood for more than 20 years. Bubka won all six World Championships from 1983 to 1997; no other athlete has won six world titles in the same individual event. In 1988 in Seoul he became Olympic champion; at other Olympics luck was against him: in 1984 the USSR boycotted the Games, in 1992 he failed to clear a height, and in 1996 and 2000 he was held back by injuries. In 2001 he was made a Hero of Ukraine.

After his career Bubka became a sports administrator: in 1999 he was elected a member of the International Olympic Committee (in 2024 he was re-elected for another eight years), in 2005–2022 he headed the National Olympic Committee of Ukraine, and in 2007–2023 he was a vice president of the International Association of Athletics Federations (now World Athletics). In 2002–2006 he was a member of Ukraine's parliament (elected on the list of the For a United Ukraine! bloc, later in the Party of Regions faction), and in 2010–2014 an unpaid adviser to President Viktor Yanukovych.

## Debates and assessments

In 2023 the investigative journalists of Bihus.Info reported that Mont Blanc, a company registered in Russia and named as co-owned by Sergey Bubka and his brother Vasyl, supplied fuel to the occupation authorities in Russian-occupied parts of Ukraine. Ukraine's Security Service (SBU) opened a criminal investigation, and in November 2023 the Prosecutor General's Office served Vasyl Bubka in absentia with a notice of suspicion of collaboration and assisting a terrorist organization; these reports do not say that Sergey Bubka himself was served with a notice of suspicion. Sergey Bubka said he had not been to the occupied territories since 2014 and had nothing to do with any business there, and called the reports a campaign against his reputation. In February 2026 the skeleton racer Vladyslav Heraskevych called for Bubka to be stripped of the title Hero of Ukraine; in April 2026 Sports Minister Matvii Bidnyi said he saw no legal grounds for that: in his view, such a step would be possible if the facts in the reports were proven and assessed by law enforcement.

## Why it matters today

Bubka is a symbol of sporting longevity and one of the best-known figures of Ukrainian sport in the world: he is in the Guinness World Records for the most world records in athletics, and his 6 metres became a new horizon for pole vaulters.
