## Who was Yulian Shpol?

Yulian Shpol was the name on the books of one of the best-known writers in 1920s Kharkiv. His real name was Mykhailo Yalovyi. He was born in 1895 in the village of Dar-Nadezhda, now in the Kharkiv region. He studied medicine at Kyiv University, and after the 1917 revolution he threw himself into politics: first as a Socialist Revolutionary, then as a Borotbist, and as head of the county zemstvo (local council) around Kostiantynohrad.

## Futurist, poet, novelist

In 1921 Yalovyi, Mykhailo Semenko and Vasyl Aleshko founded the Combat Group of Futurist Poets. Then came his poetry collection Verkhy (The Peaks, 1923), the comedy Katia’s Love, or Building Propaganda (1928) and the novel Golden Fox Cubs (1929).

## VAPLITE and Khvylovy

Yalovyi was a close friend and ally of [Mykola Khvylovy](/en/people/mykola-khvylovyi/). In 1925 they were among the founders of VAPLITE, the Free Academy of Proletarian Literature, and Yalovyi became its first president. In the Literary Discussion of 1925–1928 he was one of the sharpest polemicists. In 1927 he took part in the All-Ukrainian Orthographic Conference in Kharkiv, which adopted a single standard Ukrainian spelling.

## An arrest that sent a signal

In May 1933 Yalovyi was arrested. Among other things, he was accused of being a Polish spy and a terrorist. His arrest marked the start of a purge of the Ukrainian intelligentsia led by the Communist Party official Pavel Postyshev. Soon after his friend’s arrest, Mykola Khvylovy took his own life. In the autumn of 1933 Yalovyi was sentenced to 10 years’ imprisonment, which he served on the Solovetsky Islands. On 9 October 1937 he was sentenced again, this time to death, and on 3 November 1937 he was shot. Sources differ on the place: most name the Sandarmokh forest in Karelia, while the Encyclopedia of the History of Ukraine names the town of Lodeynoye Pole. He was rehabilitated in 1957.

## Why it matters today

Yalovyi’s arrest was one of the first blows against the generation later called the Executed Renaissance. A volume of his selected works came out in 2007, and in 2025 the National Bank issued a commemorative coin bearing his pen name, Yulian Shpol.
