## A volunteer from Solomianka

Andrii Havryliuk was born on 3 February 1980 in Kyiv. He went to Specialized School No. 71 in the Solomianka district, then to Kyiv Vocational School No. 3, and in 1998-2000 did his conscript service at the Poltava Military Institute of Communications.

When russia's aggression began, Andrii didn't wait to be called up: on 23 August 2014 he volunteered. He was assigned to the 90th Separate Airborne Battalion of the 81st Separate Airborne Brigade, to the fire-support company, the same one in which [Ivan Zubkov](/en/people/ivan-zubkov/) served. Andrii became a senior gunner of a platoon and took the call sign "Bur."

## The new terminal

From November 2014, "Bur" fought at Pisky and Vodiane, and then at Donetsk airport. There he and a comrade fired an AGS-17 automatic grenade launcher. In all, he spent a little over two and a half months at the front.

In January 2015, Andrii held the new terminal of the airport together with a machine gunner from his battalion, [Ihor Branovytskyi](/en/people/ihor-branovytskyi/). When the new terminal was destroyed, the militants took its surviving defenders prisoner. On 21 January 2015, Andrii Havryliuk was killed by the militants. He was 34. He was buried at Berkovets Cemetery in Kyiv.

## Why it matters today

Andrii Havryliuk was not a career soldier: he had done his conscript service and lived a civilian life, but in 2014 he chose on his own to defend his country. He showed that courage is a decision a person makes without being ordered to. By presidential decree he was posthumously awarded the Order For Courage, 3rd class, and exactly one year after his death a memorial plaque was unveiled at the school where he studied, so that its pupils know who left their school to defend Ukraine.
