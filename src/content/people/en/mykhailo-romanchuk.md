## A medal for all Ukrainians

Budapest, June 2022. Mykhailo Romanchuk stands on the World Championships podium with bronze in the 800 metres: he taps his heart, bows his head, wipes away tears. On the other side of the podium stands the silver medallist, Germany's Florian Wellbrock, his long-time rival. Only months earlier, when russia launched its full-scale war, it was Wellbrock who had invited the Ukrainian to join him. Romanchuk dedicated his medal to all Ukrainians and the army.

## From a pool in Rivne

Mykhailo Romanchuk was born on 7 August 1996 in Rivne. He started swimming there at six, and for the next 18 years he trained with one coach, Petro Nahornyi. In 2014 he won the 400 metres at the Youth Olympic Games, and in 2018, in Glasgow, he became European champion over two distances at once: 400 and 800 metres.

## A record in Tokyo

At the Tokyo Olympics in 2021, Romanchuk set an Olympic record of 7:41.28 in the 800-metre heats. In the final he took bronze, less than half a second behind the American Robert Finke and the Italian Gregorio Paltrinieri. In the 1500 metres he won silver.

## Swimming instead of a gun

After the invasion his coach, Petro Nahornyi, went into military service and persuaded his student not to give up the sport. "I couldn't do anything with a gun," Romanchuk said of his choice. In March 2022 he joined Wellbrock's training group in Magdeburg, Germany. At the World Championships in Budapest, besides the 800-metre bronze, he won another, in the 5 km open water race. It was Ukraine's first world championship medal in open water swimming.

## A flag in Paris

At the Paris 2024 Olympics, Romanchuk and [Elina Svitolina](/en/people/elina-svitolina/) carried Ukraine's flag at the opening ceremony. He raced only once there, finishing 17th in the 800-metre heats, and illness kept him out of the 1500. At the end of 2025 he was elected vice-president of the Ukrainian Swimming Federation, and in January 2026 he announced his retirement, saying he would coach young swimmers.

## Why it matters today

Romanchuk showed that a boy from an ordinary pool in Rivne can race the world's best distance swimmers as an equal and take an Olympic record from them. When the war took away his coach and his normal life, he did not quit, and he made every race a way of telling the world about Ukraine. His friendship with a German rival is a reminder that support for Ukraine is made of concrete acts by real people. Now he is passing what he knows on to children.
