## 15.50 metres

How long can a world record last? On 10 August 1995, at the World Championships in Gothenburg, Sweden, Inessa Kravets jumped 15.50 metres with her third attempt in the final. It brought her the gold and a new triple jump world record. For almost 26 years that distance was the mark every woman in the event measured herself against.

## From Dnipro, via the long jump

Inessa Kravets was born on 5 October 1966 in Dnipropetrovsk, now Dnipro. She is the daughter and pupil of Z. Shuliak, who started her on the path into sport. Kravets first made her name in the long jump: she was tenth at Seoul in 1988, and in 1992 in Barcelona, competing for the Unified Team of former Soviet republics, she won Olympic silver.

The women's triple jump was only just arriving at the big championships, and Kravets became one of its first stars. As early as 1991 she set a world record of 14.95 metres, and in 1993 she won the World Indoor title.

## The first ever

In 1996 in Atlanta, women competed for Olympic medals in the triple jump for the first time. Kravets won with 15.33 metres, an Olympic record, and became the event's first Olympic champion. Her coaches on the way there were Mykola Kushnir and Anatolii Holubtsov.

Her world record fell only on 1 August 2021, at the Tokyo Olympics, when Venezuela's Yulimar Rojas jumped 15.67 metres, 17 centimetres further.

## Debates and assessments

Kravets's career was twice interrupted by doping cases. In 1993 she was banned for three months for using stimulants, and missed the World Championships where women contested the triple jump for the first time. In early 2000 she tested positive for anabolic steroids and received a two-year ban. Her 1995 world record was not affected: it remained the world record until 2021. Kravets returned to win long jump silver at the 2003 World Indoor Championships, and retired after she was not selected for Ukraine's 2004 Olympic team.

## Why it matters today

The first women's Olympic triple jump title in history belongs to a Ukrainian, and with it the name of a young, independent Ukraine rang out in the world's biggest stadiums. For more than a quarter of a century, Kravets's jump was the mark the best jumpers on the planet reached for. She showed that an athlete from Dnipro could not only win but set a standard the world could not match for decades. For anyone training in Ukraine today, it is a reminder that great results are possible even in an event that is only just being born.
