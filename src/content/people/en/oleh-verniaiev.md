## The wrong anthem in Nanning

Imagine you have just become world champion, you are standing on the top step of the podium, and the anthem that plays belongs to another country. That is what happened to Oleg Verniaiev in October 2014 in Nanning, China: he was handed his gold medal for the parallel bars to the anthem of Uzbekistan.

## From Donetsk to Kyiv

Oleg Verniaiev was born on 29 September 1993 in Donetsk. His mother had been a gymnast herself, and she took him to the gym when he was six. In 2009 he moved to Kyiv, where Hennadii Sartynskyi became his coach.

## Gold in Rio

At the 2016 Olympic Games in Rio de Janeiro, Verniaiev became Olympic champion on the parallel bars and won silver in the all-around. He had plenty of offers to switch countries: from russia, Azerbaijan, Germany and Kazakhstan. The best-paid one, he said, came from russia. He turned them all down and competed only for Ukraine.

## Medals for the displaced

After russia's full-scale invasion, in April 2022, Verniaiev said he had joined the Armed Forces of Ukraine. He put his Universiade medals up at a charity auction in the United States, and the money built two houses in Kyiv region for people displaced from the Donbas. He also switched to speaking Ukrainian.

## The comeback

The ban kept Verniaiev out of the Tokyo Olympics. Once he was back, in September 2023, he won the parallel bars straight away at a World Cup event in Turkey. In 2024 he won team gold at the European Championships, and at the Paris Olympics he finished fifth on the pommel horse and eighth on the parallel bars.

## Debates and assessments

On 26 August 2020 a sample from Verniaiev was found to contain meldonium, a banned substance. In July 2021 the Disciplinary Commission of the Gymnastics Ethics Foundation (GEF) banned him for four years, counted from November 2020. He denied any wrongdoing: he said the meldonium came from contaminated food, and that only one of nearly a dozen tests had come back positive. On 14 March 2023 the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) partly upheld his appeal and cut the ban in half, to two years. Verniaiev believes he proved his innocence, though the ban was shortened rather than overturned.

## Why it matters today

A boy from Donetsk became one of the strongest gymnasts of his time and raised Ukraine's flag at the Olympics. Offered a better deal under another flag, he chose Ukraine, and when the war came he stood with its defenders and helped people who had lost their homes. His story shows that loyalty to your country is a choice made every day, not only on the podium.
