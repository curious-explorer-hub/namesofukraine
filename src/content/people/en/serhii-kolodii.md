## Where the call sign “Rubizh” came from

In the summer of 2014, Captain Serhii Kolodii's company entered Donetsk airport and began fortifying positions by the new terminal. The soldiers named one of the new positions "Rubizh", Ukrainian for "the line" you hold. Soon they called their commander the same.

Serhii Kolodii was born on 4 February 1981 in the small town of Peresichne, in the Kharkiv region. After school he spent a year at a vocational school in Kharkiv, then decided to become a soldier. At the Kharkiv Polytechnic Institute he trained as a mechanical engineer and tactical-level command officer, finishing in 2004. One of his classmates was the future tank commander [Yevhen Mezhevikin](/en/people/yevhen-mezhevikin/), who would also defend Donetsk airport.

## Commander of the 5th Company

Kolodii served in the 93rd Separate Mechanized Brigade, rising to captain and commander of the 5th Company of its 2nd Mechanized Battalion. A former soldier remembered him as tough and demanding but fair, a model officer. From June 2014 the company fought in the Donbas: on 26 July it helped liberate Avdiivka, then Pisky.

On 4 August 2014, Kolodii's company entered Donetsk airport and took over positions from the 72nd Brigade. For about two months it held the new terminal under artillery and mortar fire. The brigade was commanded by Colonel [Oleh Mikats](/en/people/oleh-mikats/). "Rubizh" went out again and again to the hottest spots, and between battles he trained and encouraged his men.

## Twenty soldiers from Terminal C

On 28 September 2014, the enemy attacked the airport from several directions and cut off the soldiers holding a position by Terminal C. To bring them out, Captain Kolodii took the driver's seat of a BMP-2 infantry fighting vehicle himself and set off with the company's gunner and medic, covering the withdrawal with fire. All twenty soldiers made it back to their lines.

That same day, Captain Serhii Kolodii was killed at Donetsk airport when an enemy tank shell hit his vehicle. He was 33.

## Why it matters today

Serhii Kolodii could have given an order and stayed where he was, but he took the wheel himself, because his men were in Terminal C. Twenty soldiers came back alive, and the new terminal his company held for two months helped the airport stand that autumn. He showed that a real commander goes in front of the people he answers for. A street and the school in Peresichne now bear his name, and his bust stands at the Institute of Tank Troops in Kharkiv, where new officers are trained.
