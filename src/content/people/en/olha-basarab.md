## The girl who never cried

Olha Levytska was born in 1889 in the village of Pidhoroddia near Rohatyn, the daughter of a priest (sources give 24 July or 1 September). She lost her parents early and earned her keep by giving lessons. Small, reserved and stubborn, she finished a lyceum in Przemyśl and a business course in Vienna, and worked at a bank in Lviv. She also helped found the first girls’ groups of Plast, the Ukrainian scouts.

## A woman in a rifleman’s uniform

When the First World War began in 1914, Olha joined the first women’s platoon of the Ukrainian Sich Riflemen, together with [Olena Stepaniv](/en/people/olena-stepaniv/). That same year she married Dmytro Basarab. The next summer he was killed on the Italian front. In Vienna Olha cared for wounded soldiers and for civilians, and the Red Cross honoured her for that work.

## Diplomat and courier

Under the Ukrainian People’s Republic, Basarab served as secretary of the Ukrainian embassy in Finland and travelled to Sweden and Norway. She then worked for the government of the West Ukrainian People’s Republic, which operated from Vienna. In 1920 she joined the underground Ukrainian Military Organization (UVO) and became the personal courier of its leader, [Yevhen Konovalets](/en/people/yevhen-konovalets/).

In 1923 Olha returned to Lviv. By day she worked in the office of an oil company and gave private language lessons; in secret she gathered information for UVO intelligence.

## The arrest

On the night of 9 February 1924 the Polish police searched her flat and found UVO documents. Olha was arrested. Under interrogation she named no one. On the morning of 13 February she was found dead in her cell. She was 34.

## Debates and assessments

The Polish police announced that Basarab had taken her own life. Ukrainians in Lviv did not believe it: she is believed to have died under torture. After protests an investigation was opened, but it found nothing. How she died has never been fully established. The Polish authorities regarded Basarab as a spy, accusing her of gathering information on the Polish army.

## Why it matters today

Olha Basarab went from the first girls’ groups of Plast and the first women’s platoon of the Ukrainian Sich Riflemen to the UNR embassy in Finland and the UVO underground, where she became the personal courier of [Yevhen Konovalets](/en/people/yevhen-konovalets/). When she was arrested, she named no one: all the police wrote down was “I refuse to make any statement.” Thousands came to her funeral in Lviv, and her fate left a mark on a whole generation of Galician Ukrainians. Ukrainian women’s organizations abroad, starting in Canada, took her as their patron. Her story shows the strength of someone who will not betray her own.
