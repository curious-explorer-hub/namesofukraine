## A greatcoat and a bundle

Did you know that one of the most successful gymnasts in Olympic history arrived in Kyiv in a soldier's greatcoat, with everything he owned in a bundle? Borys Shakhlin was born on 27 January 1932 in Ishim, a town in Siberia (now Tyumen Oblast, Russia). He lost both parents at 12 and was raised by his grandmother.

After a physical education college in Sverdlovsk, his coach put him in touch with the Kyiv coach Oleksandr Mishakov. That is how Shakhlin came to Kyiv. He lived in the dormitory of the Institute of Physical Culture, in a gym fitted with 50 beds. Kyiv stayed his home for the rest of his life: he studied, trained and competed for a Kyiv club there, although abroad, like every athlete from Ukraine at the time, he competed for the Soviet team.

## "I'm not made of iron"

From 1954 Shakhlin won so often that journalists nicknamed him "the Man of Iron". He called it an invention. His secret, he said, was simple: he treated every attempt in training as if it were the decisive one, and he won not with the hardest moves but with technique and clean execution. Tall for a gymnast, he had an edge on the horizontal bar, but his height made the floor exercise harder.

## 13 Olympic medals

At the 1956 Olympics in Melbourne, Shakhlin won gold in the team event and on the pommel horse. In Rome in 1960 he became Olympic all-around champion and took seven medals: four gold, two silver and a bronze. In Tokyo in 1964 he won the horizontal bar. That made 13 Olympic medals, seven of them gold, a record for a man until another Soviet gymnast overtook it.

## After the podium

In 1966, at 34, Shakhlin retired: his gruelling last World Championships ended in a heart attack. He became an international judge, sat on the International Gymnastics Federation's Technical Committee from 1968 to 1992, and in 2004 judged at the Athens Olympics for Ukraine. He taught gymnastics at Kyiv's university of physical education and sport, and with his wife set up a foundation to help Ukrainian gymnasts. Kyiv made him an honorary citizen. Borys Shakhlin died in Kyiv on 30 May 2008.

## Why it matters today

Borys Shakhlin wasn't born in Ukraine, but it was Kyiv that made him an Olympic champion: a Kyiv coach, a Kyiv gym, a Kyiv institute. His medals were counted for the USSR, yet they grew out of the Kyiv school of gymnastics. After his own career he gave his experience back to Ukraine: he taught students, judged the Olympics for Ukraine and supported Ukrainian gymnasts. His story shows that champions aren't made of iron but of daily work, and that home is the place you give yourself to.
