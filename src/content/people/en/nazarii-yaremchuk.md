## A boy from Rivnia

Nazarii Yaremchuk was born in 1951 into a peasant family in the village of Rivnia in Bukovyna (now part of the town of Vyzhnytsia). After his father’s death he studied at the Vyzhnytsia boarding school, where he began singing in the choir club. From November 1969 he was a soloist of the band Smerichka at the Vyzhnytsia district House of Culture, led by Levko Dutkovskyi. He got into university only at the second attempt, in 1970.

## How “Chervona Ruta” made him famous

In Smerichka Yaremchuk sang in a duet with Vasyl Zinkevych and worked with the composer [Volodymyr Ivasiuk](/en/people/volodymyr-ivasiuk/). Wide fame came with the musical TV film Chervona Ruta (1971), in which he and Zinkevych sang Ivasiuk’s song of the same name. In 1973 Smerichka was invited onto the professional stage of the Chernivtsi Philharmonic, and from 1982 Yaremchuk led the band. He toured the whole USSR, performed in Canada, the United States, Brazil and India, and in 1986 he sang for the Chornobyl disaster cleanup workers, including inside the 30-kilometer zone.

His repertoire numbered more than 150 songs, about love, family and the native land. Among the best known are “Stozhary” and “Smerekova Khata” by Pavlo Dvorskyi and “Hai, Zelenyi Hai” and “Batko i Maty” by Oleksandr Zlotnyk. Nazarii Yaremchuk died of cancer in 1995 in Chernivtsi and is buried there.

## Why it matters today

Nazarii Yaremchuk proved that Ukrainian songs could fill concert halls: he sang almost exclusively in Ukrainian, sometimes giving two or three concerts a day. His more than 150 songs are about love, family and the native land, and [Volodymyr Ivasiuk](/en/people/volodymyr-ivasiuk/)’s “Chervona Ruta,” which he sang with Vasyl Zinkevych, made them famous. He took these songs across the USSR and to Canada, the US, Brazil and India, and in 1986 sang for Chornobyl cleanup workers inside the zone. A boy who started in a school choir club showed that you can sing in your own language far beyond your home region. Anyone who sings in Ukrainian today carries this on.
