## A boy from Rivnia

Nazarii Yaremchuk was born in 1951 into a peasant family in the village of Rivnia in Bukovyna (now part of the town of Vyzhnytsia). His older half-brother Dmytro (1914-2010) was 37 years older: a member of the OUN from 1937, he lived in Canada from 1949. After his father’s death he studied at the Vyzhnytsia boarding school, where he began singing in the choir club. From November 1969 he was a soloist of the band Smerichka at the Vyzhnytsia district House of Culture, led by Levko Dutkovskyi. He got into university only at the second attempt, in 1970.

## How “Chervona Ruta” made him famous

In Smerichka Yaremchuk sang in a duet with Vasyl Zinkevych and worked with the composer [Volodymyr Ivasiuk](/en/people/volodymyr-ivasiuk/). Wide fame came with the musical TV film Chervona Ruta (1971), in which he and Zinkevych sang Ivasiuk’s song of the same name. In 1973 Smerichka was invited onto the professional stage of the Chernivtsi Philharmonic, and from 1982 Yaremchuk led the band. In 1981 he won a diploma at the international Bratislava Lyre contest, in 1987 he was named People’s Artist of the Ukrainian SSR, and in 1988 he graduated in stage directing from the Kyiv Institute of Culture. He toured the whole USSR, performed in Canada, the United States, Brazil, India and Afghanistan, and in 1986 he sang for the Chornobyl disaster cleanup workers, including inside the 30-kilometer zone. On a tour abroad he met his brother Dmytro, and he dedicated the songs “Nas Bahato po Svitu” (There Are Many of Us Around the World) and “Leleka z Ukrainy” (A Stork from Ukraine) to him and to all emigrants.

His repertoire numbered more than 150 songs, about love, family and the native land. Among the best known are “Stozhary” and “Smerekova Khata” by Pavlo Dvorskyi and “Hai, Zelenyi Hai” and “Batko i Maty” by Oleksandr Zlotnyk. According to Zlotnyk, Yaremchuk sang a new song in front of an audience at least 30 times before he recorded it. From 1977 he kept a diary. Two or three weeks before his death, in June 1995, he wrote an entry titled “How long?!”: about Moscow pop music and television pushing out Ukrainian songs while Ukraine’s own singers were ignored, and he called on Ukrainians to stay vigilant. Nazarii Yaremchuk died of cancer in 1995 in Chernivtsi and is buried there.

## Why it matters today

Nazarii Yaremchuk proved that Ukrainian songs could fill concert halls: he sang almost exclusively in Ukrainian, sometimes giving two or three concerts a day. In 2021 he was named Hero of Ukraine, among other reasons for his part in shaping national identity. His songs about love, family and the native land, [Volodymyr Ivasiuk](/en/people/volodymyr-ivasiuk/)’s “Chervona Ruta” among them, carried Ukrainian words far beyond Bukovyna. A boy who started in a school choir club showed that you can sing in your own language for the whole world. Anyone who sings in Ukrainian today carries this on.
