## Who he was

Nazarii Yaremchuk was born in 1951 into a peasant family in the village of Rivnia in Bukovyna (now part of the town of Vyzhnytsia). After his father’s death he studied at the Vyzhnytsia boarding school, where he began singing in the choir club. From November 1969 he was a soloist of the vocal-instrumental band Smerichka at the Vyzhnytsia district House of Culture, led by Levko Dutkovskyi. In 1970, on his second attempt, he entered the geography faculty of Chernivtsi University.

## His story

In Smerichka Yaremchuk sang in a duet with Vasyl Zinkevych and worked with the composer [Volodymyr Ivasiuk](/en/people/volodymyr-ivasiuk/). Wide fame came with the musical TV film Chervona Ruta (1971), in which he and Zinkevych sang Ivasiuk’s song “Chervona Ruta.” In 1973 Smerichka was invited onto the professional stage of the Chernivtsi Philharmonic, and from 1982 Yaremchuk was its artistic director. He toured all the republics of the USSR and performed in Canada, the United States, Brazil, India and other countries, and in 1986 he sang for the Chornobyl disaster cleanup workers, including inside the 30-kilometer zone.

Yaremchuk’s repertoire numbered more than 150 songs, about love, family and the native land, and he sang almost all of them in Ukrainian. Among the best known are “Stozhary” and “Smerekova Khata” by Pavlo Dvorskyi and “Hai, Zelenyi Hai” and “Batko i Maty” by Oleksandr Zlotnyk. He was a People’s Artist of the Ukrainian SSR (1987), and in 1988 he also graduated from the stage directing faculty of the Kyiv Institute of Culture. Nazarii Yaremchuk died of cancer on 30 June 1995 in Chernivtsi and is buried there in the central cemetery.

## Why it matters today

Yaremchuk sang almost exclusively in Ukrainian, and he filled concert halls to overflowing, sometimes giving two or three concerts a day. Posthumously he received the Shevchenko Prize (1996) and the title Hero of Ukraine (2021), and since 1996 the Smerekova Khata museum-estate has operated in his family’s home in Vyzhnytsia.
