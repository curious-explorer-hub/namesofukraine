## One point in Beijing

What can a single arrow decide? On 15 August 2008, in the Olympic final in Beijing, Viktor Ruban faced South Korea's Park Kyung-mo. A win for Park would have given his country its first ever Olympic men's individual title. But with the last arrow of the final, Ruban snatched it, 113–112. He had barely made it that far: his semifinal ended in a 112–112 tie and was settled by a shoot-off.

## A bow made of willow

Viktor Ruban was born on 24 May 1981 in Kharkiv. According to his mother, he began shooting at the age of three: his grandfather made him a bow out of a willow branch, and he shot with it in the village all through his childhood. In 2004, at the Athens Olympics, Ruban won a bronze medal with the Ukrainian team.

Back home after Beijing, he described what he felt: "Pride in my country, and in the people who helped me achieve this."

## Twenty years, then a new technique

Ruban competed at four Olympic Games in a row, from Athens to Rio de Janeiro. In 2009 he won the Universiade and a World Championships bronze; in 2014 he and his teammates became world indoor champions, and in 2015 they won the team event at the European Games.

Imagine being an Olympic champion who has shot the same way for twenty years. That was Ruban when he decided to switch to the classic release: he explained that his results had stalled. At the Ukrainian championships in Kharkiv in early 2021 he competed with his new technique for the first time, reached the final and lost to Ivan Kozhokar.

## Why it matters today

Ruban proved that a Ukrainian can come out on top at the tensest moment there is: the last arrow of an Olympic final. He called his victory a source of pride in his country and in the people who had helped him. His path from a childhood willow bow to Olympic gold shows that a big goal can start with very simple things. And his readiness to rebuild his technique after twenty years of success is a lesson for anyone who doesn't want to stop at what they've already achieved.
