## The dancer who made costumes

Did you know that one of the voices of “Chervona Ruta” started out not as a singer but as a dancer and costume designer? Vasyl Zinkevych was born in 1945 in the village of Vaskivtsi in the Khmelnytskyi region. After his army service he went to Vyzhnytsia in Bukovyna and studied artistic metalwork at its school of applied art. As a student he danced in the folk ensemble Smerechyna, later led it, and designed its stage costumes himself. With the designer Alla Dutkovska he made costumes for the band Smerichka, and for the young [Sofia Rotaru](/en/people/sofiia-rotaru/) he created her first concert outfits, with Ukrainian patterns.

## The duet that sang “Chervona Ruta”

Smerichka’s leader, Levko Dutkovskyi, taught Zinkevych to sing, and soon he was one of the band’s soloists. There he sang in a duet with [Nazarii Yaremchuk](/en/people/nazarii-yaremchuk/). In 1971 the two of them and the composer [Volodymyr Ivasiuk](/en/people/volodymyr-ivasiuk/) sang “Chervona Ruta” at the Song of the Year TV festival in Moscow, and the song won. The same year the musical film Chervona Ruta came out, with Zinkevych as the lead, a young miner called Borys, and Sofia Rotaru as Oksana. In 1973 Smerichka moved onto the professional stage of the Chernivtsi Philharmonic.

## Lutsk and Svitiaz

In 1975 the duet broke up. Valerii Hromtsev, who had just taken over the band Svitiaz at the Volyn Philharmonic, recalled that Zinkevych phoned him himself and asked to join. So the singer moved to Lutsk, and from 1980 to 2000 he was Svitiaz’s artistic director. He sang songs by Ivasiuk, Ihor Poklad and Ihor Bilozir, among them “Balada pro Malvy” (Ballad of the Mallows), and toured Poland, Czechia, Hungary, Finland and Germany. Lutsk made him an honorary citizen; he received the Shevchenko Prize in 1994, the title Hero of Ukraine in 2009 and the National Legend of Ukraine award in 2025.

## Why it matters today

With Yaremchuk and Ivasiuk, Vasyl Zinkevych proved that a Ukrainian song could be heard across a vast country and win even in Moscow. He brought song, dance and Ukrainian-patterned costumes together, so Ukrainian pop looked and sounded like itself. A boy from a village in the Khmelnytskyi region, who came to a craft school to learn metalwork, became one of the voices generations grew up with. Anyone who goes on stage with a Ukrainian song today walks a road he helped to build.
