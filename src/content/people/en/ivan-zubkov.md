## From a commercial director back to the front

Ivan Zubkov was born on 1 November 1973 in Derazhnia, in the Khmelnytskyi region. In 1991 he entered the Lviv Higher Military-Political School, where he was in a company that trained specialists for the Navy. When it was closed, he was transferred to the Kyiv Higher Naval School and graduated in 1995. Of the 32 graduates in his class, only five swore the oath of allegiance to the people of Ukraine, and Zubkov was one of them. From 1995 to 1998 he served in the 19th Missile Division in Khmelnytskyi, then spent sixteen years away from the military, working as commercial director of a company in Khmelnytskyi and later running his own business.

In the winter of 2013-2014 he stood on the Maidan during the Revolution of Dignity, and in August 2014, when mobilization began, he volunteered. He was assigned to the 90th Separate Airborne Battalion "Zhytomyr" of the 81st Separate Airborne Brigade, becoming deputy commander for personnel of its fire-support company. His comrades valued him for another skill too: he could repair and tune any weapon in the unit.

## Donetsk airport

The battalion was deployed to the Donbas in November 2014. On 18 January 2015, Senior Lieutenant Zubkov took command of a fire-support company in assault operations meant to relieve the defenders of Donetsk airport - the men who would become known as "cyborgs," among them [Valerii Chybinieiev](/en/people/valerii-chybinieiev/) and [Ihor Branovytskyi](/en/people/ihor-branovytskyi/). His men broke through enemy positions, which let a reserve unit reach the new terminal. When the enemy took hold of part of the airport, Zubkov organized a counterattack, regained control of the new terminal and saw to the evacuation of the wounded. On 19 January he was wounded in the hand but stayed in the fight.

On 20 January 2015, Senior Lieutenant Ivan Zubkov was killed under the collapsed second floor of the airport's new terminal while covering his unit's withdrawal. He was 41.

## Why it matters today

Ivan Zubkov could have stayed a civilian, but after the Maidan he volunteered. He showed that a commander answers for everyone beside him: he made sure every comrade’s weapon worked, and at Donetsk airport he led his company to relieve the defenders who were cut off there. His path proves that experience gained long ago can be needed by the country again, and that it is never too late to stand up for it. The 90th Battalion he served in and the school in Derazhnia where he studied now bear his name, and Ukrposhta issued an envelope with his portrait in its "Glory to the Heroes!" series.
