## Who she was

Raisa Kyrychenko (née Korzh) was born in 1943 in the village of Koreshchyna in the Poltava region. At secondary school in the neighboring village of Zemlianky she was a soloist of the school choir. She worked at the Kremenchuk Automobile Plant, and after a performance at a district amateur arts show she became a singer in the plant’s folk choir.

## Her story

In 1962 Kyrychenko joined the ensemble Veselka of the Poltava Philharmonic, in 1963–1967 she sang with the ensemble Lonok of the Zhytomyr Philharmonic, and in 1968–1983 she was a soloist of the Cherkasy Folk Choir. In 1986–1994 she was a soloist of the Poltava Philharmonic, where the ensemble Churaivna was created on her initiative, and later she taught at the Poltava Music College. She trained as a choral conductor when she was already a well-known singer, graduating from the Kharkiv Institute of Arts in 1989.

Her repertoire included folk songs (“Quiet Water,” “Oh, I Am Pretty”) and songs by contemporary composers: “The White Cottage” and “Mother’s Cherry Tree” by Anatolii Pashkevych, and “Two Colors” and “The Sycamore and Its Mate” by Oleksandr Bilash to words by Dmytro Pavlychko. Kyrychenko toured Canada, the United States, Australia, Algeria, Tunisia and other countries. She was a People’s Artist of the Ukrainian SSR (1979), a Shevchenko Prize laureate (1986) and a Hero of Ukraine (2003). She died on 9 February 2005 in Kyiv and was buried in her native village.

## Why it matters today

Her unique voice and heartfelt singing won Raisa Kyrychenko the love of the whole country, and she is called the “guardian of Ukrainian song.” In 2005 a pop song festival named after her, Song Wings of Churaivna, was founded in Poltava, and a memorial plaque to her was installed on a building in Poltava.
