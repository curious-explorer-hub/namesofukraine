## Aristarchus of the Hay Market

At the end of 1918 or early in 1919, [Yurii Klen](/en/people/yurii-klen/) came back to a starving Kyiv and, near the Hay Market, ran into Borys Yakubskyi, an older friend from university. Yakubskyi took him home, where books stood in rows on the shelves. Klen recalled that poets gathered in this house to read their verse and hear the opinion of an expert on poetry. [Mykola Zerov](/en/people/mykola-zerov/) and [Pavlo Fylypovych](/en/people/pavlo-fylypovych/) came here, and Klen believed that this was probably where the roots of Neoclassicism lay. His friends respected Yakubskyi as a critic and called him Aristarchus, after the ancient Greek scholar who edited the texts of Homer. In 1923 Zerov dedicated the poem “Aristarchus” to him.

## The scholar who counted lines

Yakubskyi was born in 1889 in the small town of Illintsi (now in the Vinnytsia region). He graduated from Kyiv University in 1914 and taught there from 1921 to 1941; in 1932–1934 he was dean of the Faculty of Philology. In his article The Form of Shevchenko’s Poems (1921) he was one of the first Ukrainian verse scholars to make wide use of statistics, analysing the metre and rhythm of more than 20,000 lines of the Kobzar. Then came his books The Science of Versification (1922) and The Sociological Method in Literature (1923). He is considered one of the first to study Ukrainian literature with the sociological method. He edited the works of [Lesya Ukrainka](/en/people/lesya-ukrainka/) in 7 and in 12 volumes and wrote about [Mykola Voronyi](/en/people/mykola-voronyi/). In 1939 the first two volumes of [Taras Shevchenko](/en/people/taras-shevchenko/)’s complete works came out under his editorship. They were later withdrawn, and in 1951 and 1953 they were reissued without his name.

## Occupation and prison

In the 1930s he was attacked as a “formalist”, and after 1930 his work almost stopped appearing. During the German occupation Yakubskyi stayed in Kyiv. From December 1941 to September 1943 he worked at the newspaper New Ukrainian Word, which was published under the occupiers’ control. In the summer of 1944 Soviet state security arrested him and charged him with collaborating with the Germans. He died in prison on 28 December 1944.

## Why it matters today

Borys Yakubskyi showed that verse can be studied precisely, line by line, and that Shevchenko’s poetry has a rich and complex rhythm. For decades his name was removed from books, but in 2007 his Science of Versification was published again. Anyone who reads a poem closely today and asks how it is made carries on his work.
