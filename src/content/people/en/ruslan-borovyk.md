## Why “Bahdad”?

Ruslan Borovyk got the call sign “Bahdad” (Baghdad) from his past: long before the war in the Donbas, he had already seen combat in Iraq. He was born on 19 April 1981 in the village of Kropyvnia in the Zhytomyr region. After his conscript service he signed a contract with the Armed Forces of Ukraine, and in 2004-2005 he served on two missions in Iraq. He then left the army and spent twelve years as a cash-in-transit guard for a bank. During the Revolution of Dignity he went to central Kyiv almost every evening to help the protesters, and he photographed what he saw.

## A grenadier with a camera

After russia occupied Crimea, Borovyk asked several times to join the army and was turned down at first. In 2014 he was finally called up, as a grenadier in the 90th Separate Airborne Battalion, formed on the base of the 95th Brigade. He was sent to Donetsk airport twice: first in late November 2014, when the enemy took the old terminal, and again in mid-January 2015. In those January battles for the new terminal, his comrades from the 90th Battalion, [Ivan Zubkov](/en/people/ivan-zubkov/) and [Ihor Branovytskyi](/en/people/ihor-branovytskyi/), fought and were killed. Borovyk himself was wounded in the fighting for the airport: a machine-gun burst caught him as he climbed onto an infantry fighting vehicle during an evacuation, and a bullet went through his leg. He also suffered a concussion that left his hearing damaged.

All the while, Borovyk took pictures, even under sniper fire: he would rise for a moment to frame a shot, then take cover again. At first many of his comrades didn't want to be in his lens and asked him why he bothered. He said photographing at the airport helped him release the tension. He never planned an exhibition, but he wanted to record what he saw, for himself or for the generations to come.

## The photobook “DAP”

In 2015 his photographs, from training on the range to the defense of the airport, came out as a photobook called “DAP”, the Ukrainian abbreviation for Donetsk airport. It was presented in Kyiv in late August 2015, but the author couldn't be there: he was still in the combat zone. The print run was small, and Borovyk planned to spend the money from sales on thermal imagers and night sights for his battalion. Later a solo exhibition of his work was shown in the Verkhovna Rada, Ukraine's parliament. In June 2020 the Ukrainian Institute of National Memory and the news agency Ukrinform displayed about 50 of his photographs on nine banners in Kyiv: the defenders' daily life and positions, wrecked houses nearby, moments of despair and joy, and the faces of the men who came to be called "cyborgs."

## Back at the front

After he was demobilized, Borovyk worked at the Kyiv City Center for Assistance to ATO Veterans, ran marathons and joined the Defenders' Marches. From the first days of the full-scale invasion in 2022 he was fighting again, in the 95th Separate Air Assault Brigade, as a junior sergeant and squad leader. A month before his death he was concussed but stayed with his squad. Ruslan Borovyk was killed in battle near Izium, in the Kharkiv region, on 30 April 2022 (some sources give 29 April); he was 41. He was posthumously awarded the Order for Courage, 2nd class.

## Why it matters today

Ruslan Borovyk was both a defender and a witness: thanks to his photographs, the defense of Donetsk airport can be seen from the inside, not only the fighting but the daily life, the exhaustion and the faces of the men called "cyborgs." He took pictures not for fame but so that those who come after would know what it was like, and he even wanted to turn his book into thermal imagers for his comrades. In 2022, with Iraq, a wound and a concussion behind him, he went to defend Ukraine once more. His life shows that the truth about a war, recorded in time, also defends a country.
