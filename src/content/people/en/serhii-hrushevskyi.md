## The godson who took another road

His godfather was [Mykhailo Hrushevskyi](/en/people/mykhailo-hrushevskyi/). Yet as a young man Serhii Hrushevskyi worked against the Ukrainian movement, and he died among those his killers branded "Ukrainian bourgeois nationalists". He was born in 1892 in Kamianka, near Chyhyryn, the son of the priest Hryhorii Hrushevskyi, the historian's second cousin. In 1909 he finished school in Zlatopil with a gold medal, and his father asked Mykhailo to help the boy follow "your road".

## From Prosvita to the Double-Headed Eagle

At Kyiv University the first-year student helped the Prosvita society organise evenings in honour of Ukrainian writers. Within a few years he had become a leader of a russian nationalist student union and a member of the Black Hundreds group Double-Headed Eagle. The university gave him a silver medal for a study of Kolomna county based on 16th-century land registers. In 1917 Hrushevskyi belonged to the monarchist Vasilii Shulgin's group and spoke out against Ukrainian-language schools.

## Repentance and Luhansk

When the Bolsheviks won, he stayed at home. In 1921 he founded and ran an institute of public education in Zlatopil, then worked in Poltava. In 1923 his past caught up with him, and he had to renounce it publicly in a newspaper. From the mid-1920s Hrushevskyi was a professor and vice-rector of the Donetsk Institute of Public Education in Luhansk. In 1926 he founded and led the Scientific Society of the Donets Region. By then he was known there as a champion of Ukrainian-language education.

## A plan for Kuban

In the early 1930s Hrushevskyi moved to Kuban, home at the time to many Ukrainians. He headed the history department of the teachers' college in Krasnodar. In 1932 he and a colleague published a plan to make the college entirely Ukrainian. That December, Moscow banned Ukrainization in Kuban.

## Solovky and Sandarmokh

On 13 January 1933 Hrushevskyi was arrested in the invented case of a "Union of Kuban and Ukraine" and sentenced to ten years in the camps. He spent a year in a Rostov prison writing a history of the Black Sea Cossacks and hoping to be released, but he was sent to the camps instead. He ended up on Solovky, where Ukrainian prisoners were written into an invented "bloc" together with [Volodymyr Chekhivskyi](/en/people/volodymyr-chekhivskyi/). On 3 November 1937 Serhii Hrushevskyi was shot at Sandarmokh. He was 45. His family was given a false date of death. He was rehabilitated in 1960.

## Debates and assessments

In 1917 Ukrainian contemporaries called Hrushevskyi a renegade for moving from Prosvita to the Black Hundreds. Some scholars see his turns as careerism: under every regime he tried to be on the winning side. Others note that in Luhansk and Kuban he consistently built Ukrainian-language education. In their view, after many detours he came back to his own people and died a Ukrainian scholar.

## Why it matters today

Serhii Hrushevskyi's life shows how the empire and then the Soviet state broke Ukraine's educated class: first they lured people with careers, then demanded repentance, and in the end destroyed them. His road from rejecting everything Ukrainian to planning a Ukrainian college in Kuban shows that it is possible to come back to your people even after wrong choices. And the fact that the regime punished him precisely for his work for Ukrainian schools explains why learning and teaching in Ukrainian today is no small thing.
