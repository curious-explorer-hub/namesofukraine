## 27.18 seconds

How long can a swimming world record last? On 2 August 2002, at the European Championships in Berlin, Oleh Lisohor swam 50 metres breaststroke in 27.18 seconds. The race won him his second gold of the championships and took the world record from the American Ed Moses, who had swum 27.39. Nobody went faster until April 2009, when South Africa's Cameron van der Burgh broke it, almost seven years later.

## A boy from Brovary

Oleh Lisohor was born on 17 January 1979 in Brovary, just outside Kyiv. As a junior he was already winning medals at the European Championships: bronze in Copenhagen in 1996 and silver in Glasgow in 1997. He went on to graduate from the National University of Physical Education and Sport of Ukraine.

## Seven world titles

Lisohor's first World Championship medal was a bronze in the 50 m breaststroke in 2000. A year later, in Fukuoka, Japan, he was world champion. Six more titles followed: two in 2002, two in 2006, gold in Melbourne in 2007 and in Manchester in 2008. He won the European title many times, and he set world records three times.

At three Olympic Games, in Sydney, Athens and Beijing, Lisohor swam the 100 m breaststroke. He never won an Olympic medal: his best individual finish was 8th place in Athens in 2004. Ukraine made him an Honoured Master of Sport and awarded him the Order for Merit twice.

## Why it matters today

Lisohor proved that an athlete from a town outside Kyiv could be the fastest in the world at his distance and hold a world record for years. Seven times he brought Ukraine gold from the World Championships, in a sport decided by hundredths of a second. His wins kept Ukraine among the leaders of world breaststroke. For anyone training in an ordinary local pool, his story is a reminder that fifty metres can be swum faster than anyone else on the planet.
