## The daughter of an "enemy of the people"

Did you know that one of the world's first programming languages was created by a woman who was thrown out of university at 17? Kateryna Rvachova was born in 1919 in Chyhyryn into a family of teachers. In 1937, during her first year at Kyiv University, her father, a history teacher, was arrested as a "bourgeois nationalist", and later her mother was sentenced too. As the daughter of an "enemy of the people", Kateryna was expelled.

The university that took her was far away, in Samarkand, Uzbekistan, and she graduated in Tashkent in 1942. During the war she worked as a shot-firer in a coal mine, and in 1944 she returned to Ukraine to teach school.

## From probability theory to the first computer

In 1946 the mathematician Borys Hnedenko took her into the Lviv branch of the Institute of Mathematics. In 1950 Yushchenko moved to Kyiv to head a laboratory of computing methods. Just outside Kyiv the MESM was running, the first computer in continental Europe. Its working memory held only 31 numbers and 63 instructions, so every program was a puzzle of its own.

## A language that set programs free

In 1955 Yushchenko and Volodymyr Koroliuk developed the Address language, before Fortran, COBOL and ALGOL appeared. A program written in it no longer depended on where in memory it sat. The language could handle "addresses of the second rank": an address that points to another address. Her son, the researcher Yurii Yushchenko, sees this as a forerunner of pointers, which are usually credited to the American Harold Lawson (1964). For more than 20 years the Kyiv, Dnipro, Ural-1 and other computers were programmed in the Address language.

## A school of Kyiv programmers

In 1961 Yushchenko, Hnedenko and Koroliuk published the textbook Elements of Programming, later translated into several languages. In 1966 she became the first person in the USSR to earn a doctorate in physics and mathematics for work in programming. With [Viktor Hlushkov](/en/people/viktor-hlushkov/) she was among the founders of the Institute of Cybernetics, where she headed the programming theory department for 28 years. She trained 11 doctors of science and worked at the institute to the end of her life. Kateryna Yushchenko died in Kyiv in 2001.

## Why it matters today

Kateryna Yushchenko showed that Kyiv could change how people talk to machines: her Address language was one of the first in the world. She did not break when she was denied an education because her family was repressed, and she came back to science just as programming was being born. Her textbook and her school trained generations of programmers, whose work grew into Kyiv's computing tradition. Anyone who writes code in Ukraine today carries on what she began on a machine with room for 31 numbers.
