## Who she is

Ruslana Lyzhychko was born in 1973 in Lviv. In 1995 she graduated from the Higher Music Institute in Lviv, while also working as a sound engineer and producer at the Luxen studio. In 1993 she became a laureate of the Chervona Ruta festival, and the following year she won the Grand Prix of the Melodiia TV festival.

## Her story

In 1996 Ruslana launched the project Dzvinkyi Viter (Ringing Wind), which combined the medieval ballad with modern music, and old architecture with modern sound and lighting. In 1998 she toured Ukraine’s castles to draw attention to their restoration. In 2003 her album Dyki Tantsi (Wild Dances) came out, and in 2004 in Istanbul the song “Wild Dances,” in which Ruslana drew on Hutsul melodies, scored 280 points and brought Ukraine its first Eurovision victory. That same year the singer received the title People’s Artist of Ukraine.

In 2006–2007 Ruslana was a member of Ukraine’s parliament, the Verkhovna Rada. In the winter of 2013–2014 she became one of the most visible activists of the Euromaidan: according to the Atlantic Council, she sang the national anthem of Ukraine from the Maidan stage more than 500 times. In 2014 she was awarded the Vasyl Stus Prize, and the Atlantic Council gave her its award for humanitarian leadership.

## Why it matters today

Ruslana’s victory showed that Ukrainian folk motifs can sound modern on Europe’s biggest stage. And on the Maidan she used her fame to support the protesters and draw the world’s attention to Ukraine.
