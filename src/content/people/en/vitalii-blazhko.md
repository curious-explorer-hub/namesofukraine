## How do you fight without feet?

In spring 2019, on the Svitlodarsk salient in Donbas, Vitalii Blazhko was badly wounded and doctors had to amputate both his feet. He could have registered as disabled and left the service. But then he could no longer have held a combat post, so he refused. Less than three months later “Khvat” was back with Azov, this time on carbon prostheses.

## Khvat’s snipers

Vitalii was born in 1989 in Odesa, studied at an Interior Ministry academy in Kharkiv and served in law enforcement. In spring 2015 he joined Azov, and that summer he set up a sniper platoon in the unit. In 2018 the platoon was expanded into a sniper group. Blazhko commanded it the whole time: he trained the newcomers himself, planned every mission and preferred the positions to the headquarters, going out alongside his men.

## Mariupol

From the first day of the full-scale invasion, Khvat’s group defended Mariupol as part of the Azov regiment, commanded by [Denys Prokopenko](/en/people/denys-prokopenko/). The snipers blew up bridges in the enemy’s path and set ambushes to buy time for the city’s defence. When the fighting reached the streets, Blazhko scouted with a drone and directed his snipers. For their work in Mariupol he was promoted to major ahead of schedule. After his fourth wound he refused to be evacuated, including by helicopter, and stayed with his comrades.

Major Vitalii Blazhko was killed on 8 May 2022 at the Azovstal steelworks in Mariupol, when a Russian aerial bomb hit a bunker. He was 32.

## How he is remembered

In 2023 Vitalii Blazhko was posthumously made a Hero of Ukraine. He held all three classes of the Order for Courage, and for the defence of Mariupol he was also awarded the Order of Bohdan Khmelnytskyi after his death. In 2024 a street in his home city of Odesa was named after him.
