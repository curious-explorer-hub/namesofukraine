## Who he was

Levko Lukianenko was born in 1928 in the village of Khrypivka in the Chernihiv region, into a peasant family. At the end of 1944, aged 16, he was drafted into the army, and from then on his documents gave his year of birth as 1927, which is why some reference works, including the Encyclopedia of Modern Ukraine, use that date. After his service (1944–1953) he entered the Faculty of Law at Moscow University and graduated in 1958.

## His story

Sent to do party work in Lviv region, Lukianenko founded the underground Ukrainian Workers' and Peasants' Union together with Ivan Kandyba, Stepan Virun and others. Its goal was to take Ukraine out of the USSR by lawful means, through a referendum based on the constitutions of the USSR and the Ukrainian SSR. He was arrested in January 1961 and in May sentenced to death for "treason against the motherland." He spent 72 days on death row waiting for execution before the sentence was commuted to 15 years in the camps. He served it in labor camps in Mordovia and Perm and in Vladimir prison.

Released in 1976, he settled in Chernihiv, worked as an electrician, and that same year became one of the founders of the Ukrainian Helsinki Group. In December 1977 he was arrested again, and in 1978 he was sentenced as an "especially dangerous recidivist" to 10 years of imprisonment and 5 years of exile. In 1988, while still in exile in Tomsk region, he was elected in absentia as head of the Ukrainian Helsinki Group, and later of the Ukrainian Helsinki Union. Lukianenko was freed at the end of 1988 and returned to Ukraine in early 1989. In all, he spent more than a quarter of a century in camps, prisons and exile.

In 1990 Lukianenko became head of the Ukrainian Republican Party, a member of parliament, and a co-author of the Declaration of State Sovereignty of Ukraine. He wrote the Act of Declaration of Independence of Ukraine, adopted by the Verkhovna Rada on 24 August 1991. In the first presidential election later that year he came third. In 1992–1993 he was Ukraine's first ambassador to Canada, and he was later elected to parliament three more times. He was made a Hero of Ukraine in 2005 and received the Shevchenko Prize in 2016. He died in Kyiv on 7 July 2018.

## Why it matters today

Lukianenko defended Ukraine's right to leave the USSR at a time when it could cost him his life. Three decades after his death sentence, he was the one who wrote the document with which independent Ukraine began.
