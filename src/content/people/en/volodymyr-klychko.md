## Who he is

Wladimir Klitschko (Volodymyr Klychko in Ukrainian transliteration) was born in 1976 in Kazakhstan, then part of the Soviet Union, near Semipalatinsk, where his father, a Soviet military officer, was serving: according to the Encyclopedia of Modern Ukraine, in the military settlement of Solnechnyi (now Zhangiztobe); other sources say in Semipalatinsk itself. In the mid-1980s the family moved to Ukraine and settled in Kyiv. Like his elder brother [Vitali Klitschko](/en/people/vitalii-klychko/), Wladimir graduated from the Pereiaslav-Khmelnytskyi Pedagogical Institute (1996) and earned a PhD-level degree (candidate of sciences) in physical education and sport.

## His story

Wladimir was Ukrainian boxing champion several times, won the European junior championship in 1994, and in 1996 in Atlanta became Olympic super-heavyweight champion. That same year he turned professional. In 2000 Klitschko first became WBO world champion, and in 2003 he lost the title. In 2006 he won the IBF belt and then went unbeaten for nine and a half years: by 2011 he had added the WBO, The Ring and WBA belts, unifying most of the heavyweight titles. His reign ended in November 2015 with a points defeat by the British boxer Tyson Fury. In April 2017 he lost to Anthony Joshua at Wembley Stadium in London, and in August that year he announced his retirement. In all he had 69 professional fights and won 64 of them, 53 by knockout.

With his brother, Wladimir founded the promotion company K2 Promotions and the Klitschko Brothers Foundation. In 2017 he was awarded Ukraine's Order of Freedom. On 2 February 2022, before Russia's full-scale invasion, he signed up for the Territorial Defence Forces in Kyiv.

## Why it matters today

Wladimir Klitschko is one of the most decorated heavyweight boxers in history. His Olympic gold and his long reign as champion made Ukrainian boxing known all over the world.
