## “Master George of Rus’”

Yurii Kotermak was born around 1450 in Drohobych, in the Carpathian foothills. In Europe he was known by the name of his home town, George of Drohobych, and he signed his letters “Master Georgius of Rus’”. As a young man he entered Kraków University, and in 1473 he took his master’s degree there.

## Rector in Bologna

Drohobych then went to Italy, to the University of Bologna, one of the oldest in Europe. There he earned doctorates in philosophy and medicine, taught astronomy and medicine, and in 1481–1482 served as the university’s rector.

## A book older than the discovery of America

In 1483 his Latin book, Judgement of the Forecast for the Current Year 1483, came out in Rome. It was the first book by a Ukrainian published abroad, and it appeared nine years before Columbus reached America. In it Drohobych calculated solar and lunar eclipses, made forecasts and gave the geographic coordinates of Lviv and Drohobych. Only two copies of the book are known.

## Professor in Kraków

In 1487 Drohobych returned to Kraków and became a professor of medicine at the university. In the autumn of 1491 the young Nicolaus Copernicus enrolled there; some historians count him among Drohobych’s students. Yurii Drohobych died in Kraków on 4 February 1494.

## Why it matters today

Yurii Drohobych showed that a young man from a small Galician town could become a notable figure in the European science of his day. Drohobych has a monument to him, and the historian Yaroslav Isaievych wrote a book about him for the “Lives of the Famous” series (1972).
