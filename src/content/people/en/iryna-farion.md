## Who she was

Iryna Farion was born in Lviv in 1964. In 1987 she graduated from Lviv University, where she then worked as a laboratory assistant in the Department of General Linguistics. From 1991 she taught at Lviv Polytechnic National University; in 2015 she earned the higher doctorate in philology and became a professor in its Department of the Ukrainian Language.

## Her story

Farion studied the history of the Ukrainian language, language norms, spelling and the language of individual authors. She wrote on the surnames of the Lviv Subcarpathian area, on Markiian Shashkevych as a shaper of the language and on the language of Ivan Puluj's letters, a monograph on the social status of Old Ukrainian in the 14th–17th centuries, and a book on [Stepan Bandera](/en/people/stepan-bandera/) as a practitioner and theorist of the nationalist movement. The wider public knew her as a popularizer of the language: she ran the project Treasure of the Nation and presented the TV projects Greatness of Personality on the parliamentary Rada channel and Gene of Ukrainians on NTA, and in 2022–2024 taught Ukrainian courses on her YouTube channel, Studio Farion. From 2015 to 2024 she chaired the supervisory board of the Petro Yatsyk International Ukrainian Language Competition.

From 2005 Farion sat on the political council of the All-Ukrainian Union “Svoboda” (Freedom), a nationalist party. She was a member of the Lviv Oblast Council, and in 2012 she won the election to the Verkhovna Rada, Ukraine’s parliament, in district No. 116 in Lviv with 68 percent of the vote. In the 7th convocation (2012–2014) she chaired the subcommittee on higher education of the Committee on Science and Education.

On the evening of 19 July 2024 Iryna Farion was shot outside her home in Lviv; she died in hospital the same night. She was buried in Lychakiv Cemetery. On 25 July 2024 an 18-year-old, Viacheslav Zinchenko, was detained in Dnipro. He was charged with premeditated murder in connection with the victim’s performance of a civic duty and motivated by national intolerance, and with illegal handling of a firearm. Zinchenko denied guilt. On 1 October 2026 the Shevchenkivskyi District Court of Lviv found him guilty on these charges and sentenced him to life imprisonment. The defence said it would appeal; as of October 2026 the verdict had not yet taken legal effect.

## Debates and assessments

Farion was one of the most controversial public figures in Ukraine. Supporters saw her as an uncompromising defender of the Ukrainian language; critics saw a politician whose offensive remarks alienated Russian-speaking Ukrainians and harmed the cause of Ukrainization itself.

In February 2010, as a member of the oblast council, she visited Lviv kindergarten No. 67 and was filmed reproaching children for the Russian forms of their names (Masha, Misha, Liza). The philologist and columnist Serhii Lukianchuk wrote in Texty that she advised such children to “leave for Muscovy” and argued that such conduct discredited the very cause of supporting the Ukrainian language. She was summoned for questioning by the regional police unit against organized crime (UBOZ); Svoboda called this political persecution and said the Lviv city prosecutor’s office had found no signs of incitement to ethnic hatred in her actions. In 2013, after archival documents surfaced, Farion, who had denied it before, admitted that she had joined the Soviet Communist Party in 1988 “solely for reasons of academic career advancement” and left a year later; political scientist Yurii Shveda told Radio Svoboda that there were now “reservations” about her as a politician.

The loudest scandal came in November 2023. In an interview with journalist Yanina Sokolova, Farion said she could not call soldiers who do not speak the state language Ukrainians; she was speaking in particular about Russian-speaking fighters of the Azov brigade. Her words were sharply condemned by soldiers, including Maksym Zhorin, deputy commander of the 3rd Assault Brigade, and MP Yehor Cherniev asked the Security Service of Ukraine (SBU) to investigate. Farion replied to her critics that Russian-speaking soldiers “disgrace the Armed Forces of Ukraine.” On 15 November 2023 the SBU opened criminal proceedings under articles on violating citizens’ equality, insulting the honour and dignity of a serviceman, and violating privacy; the last concerned a screenshot she had posted of a letter from a pro-Ukrainian student in occupied Crimea showing his personal data, after which, according to the SBU, Russian security services began to persecute him. The same day, after student protests, Lviv Polytechnic dismissed Farion under Article 41 of the Labour Code for “an immoral act incompatible with continuing this work”; Education Minister Oksen Lisovyi backed the students’ demands.

Farion challenged her dismissal. In February 2024 the Halytskyi District Court of Lviv rejected her claim, calling her statements immoral misconduct. On 29 May 2024 the Lviv Court of Appeal annulled the dismissal orders as unlawful, reinstated her and ordered the university to pay about 124,000 hryvnias for the period of forced absence. In court Farion cited a linguistic expert report by Pavlo Hrytsenko, director of the Institute of the Ukrainian Language of the National Academy of Sciences, which found no humiliation of soldiers’ honour or dignity and no calls to violence in her words. The ruling took effect at once, and on 7 June 2024 the rector signed the order reinstating her. No Supreme Court ruling in the case could be found.

After her killing, many officials and cultural figures saw it as a blow against the defence of the Ukrainian language, and Svoboda blamed Moscow. The first-instance court found the motive to be national intolerance: according to the verdict, Zinchenko developed a personal hostility towards Farion because of her active defence of the Ukrainian language and culture. According to the prosecution, Zinchenko took part in online communities that promoted violence and national hatred, including ones linked to a Russian neo-Nazi organization.

## Why it matters today

Farion left scholarly works and teaching projects on the Ukrainian language, and her life and death became part of Ukraine’s public debate about language, identity and the limits of public speech in wartime. In August 2024 Ivano-Frankivsk City Council decided to name a street after her and approved a Ukrainization programme in her honour, and in Lviv an Iryna Farion Street next to the main building of Lviv Polytechnic was opened on 1 January 2025.
