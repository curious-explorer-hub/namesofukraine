## Who she was

Iryna Farion was born in Lviv in 1964. In 1987 she graduated from Lviv University, where she then worked as a laboratory assistant in the Department of General Linguistics. From 1991 she taught at Lviv Polytechnic National University; in 2015 she earned the higher doctorate in philology and became a professor in its Department of the Ukrainian Language.

## Her story

Farion studied the history of the Ukrainian language, language norms, spelling and the language of individual authors. She wrote on the surnames of the Lviv Subcarpathian area, on Markiian Shashkevych as a shaper of the language and on the language of Ivan Puluj's letters, a monograph on the social status of Old Ukrainian in the 14th–17th centuries, and a book on [Stepan Bandera](/en/people/stepan-bandera/) as a practitioner and theorist of the nationalist movement. The wider public knew her as a popularizer of the language: she ran the project Treasure of the Nation and presented the TV projects Greatness of Personality on the parliamentary Rada channel and Gene of Ukrainians on NTA, and in 2022–2024 taught Ukrainian courses on her YouTube channel, Studio Farion. From 2015 to 2024 she chaired the supervisory board of the International Ukrainian Language Competition named after the philanthropist [Petro Yatsyk](/en/people/petro-yatsyk/).

On the evening of 19 July 2024 Iryna Farion was shot dead outside her home in Lviv.

## Debates and assessments

Farion was one of the most controversial public figures in Ukraine. Supporters saw her as an uncompromising defender of the Ukrainian language; critics saw a politician whose offensive remarks alienated Russian-speaking Ukrainians and harmed the cause of Ukrainization itself. She was criticised in particular for reproaching kindergarten children for the Russian forms of their names, and for saying that she could not call Russian-speaking soldiers Ukrainians. After those words Lviv Polytechnic dismissed her, but a court later reinstated her.

## Why it matters today

Farion left scholarly works and teaching projects on the Ukrainian language, and her life and death became part of Ukraine’s public debate about language, identity and the limits of public speech in wartime. In August 2024 Ivano-Frankivsk City Council decided to name a street after her and approved a Ukrainization programme in her honour, and in Lviv an Iryna Farion Street next to the main building of Lviv Polytechnic was opened on 1 January 2025.
