## Who he is

Oleh Blokhin was born in Kyiv in 1952. In 1969 he made his debut for Dynamo Kyiv and stayed with the club for almost twenty years. In the 1970s [Valerii Lobanovskyi](/en/people/valerii-lobanovskyi/) took charge of the team, and Blokhin, a forward, became its chief goalscorer.

## His story

In 1975 Dynamo won the Cup Winners' Cup and the UEFA Super Cup, and Blokhin received the Ballon d'Or as Europe's best footballer, the first in the history of Ukrainian football. In 1986 the club won the Cup Winners' Cup a second time. With Dynamo Blokhin was Soviet champion seven times and won the Soviet Cup five times. In the Soviet league he played 432 matches and scored 211 goals, more than anyone else in the competition's history. He also played and scored more than anyone else for the USSR national team (Ukraine was then part of the Soviet Union); with it he won Olympic bronze in 1972 and 1976 and silver at the 1988 European Championship. At the end of his career he played in Austria and Cyprus.

In 1990–2002 Blokhin coached Greek clubs, among them Olympiacos, with whom he won the Greek Cup (1992). In 2003 he took charge of Ukraine's national team. Under him the team qualified for a World Cup for the first time (Germany, 2006) and reached the quarterfinals there; its captain was [Andriy Shevchenko](/en/people/andrii-shevchenko/). Blokhin later coached the national team a second time (2011–2012) and then Dynamo Kyiv (2012–2014).

In 1998–2006 Blokhin was a member of Ukraine's parliament, the Verkhovna Rada, for two terms: in 1998 he was elected on the list of the Hromada party, after which he sat in the Batkivshchyna and then the Communist Party factions; in 2002 he was elected on the Communist Party list, and a few months later he moved to the faction of the Social Democratic Party (united).

## Why it matters today

Blokhin was the first Ukrainian footballer to be named the best in Europe, and he still holds the Soviet football records. As a coach he led the team of independent Ukraine to its first World Cup.
