## The boy from Pereiaslav

Sholem Rabinovich was born in 1859 in Pereiaslav, in the Kyiv region, into a Jewish shopkeeper’s family. He spent his childhood in the nearby small town of Voronkiv. Later he turned his memories of it into Kasrilevke, an invented town that became the image of the Jewish shtetl. He studied at a kheder (a religious primary school) and then at the Pereiaslav district school, which he finished with distinction. He gave Russian lessons, and in 1881–1883 he was the state-appointed rabbi in Lubny.

## “Peace be with you!”

In 1883 his first works in Yiddish came out in a St Petersburg weekly. At the same time he first signed himself “Sholem Aleichem”: the everyday Yiddish greeting, literally “Peace be with you!”. Rabinovich wanted Yiddish literature to stand alongside literature in Hebrew. In 1888 he founded the yearbook Di yidishe folksbibliotek and gathered the best writers in it, among them Mendele Moykher Sforim and Isaac Leib Peretz.

## Tevye, Yehupets and Boiberik

The first Tevye story came out in 1894. Tevye sells his milk to rich summer visitors in Boiberik, outside Yehupets. Sholem Aleichem invented these names for real places: Yehupets is Kyiv, Boiberik is Boiarka, and Chornomorsk is Odesa. Most of his stories are set in Ukraine, with Ukrainian landscapes, Ukrainian words and folklore. His heroes are poor “little people” who keep their humour and their faith in justice even in hard times.

## From Kyiv to New York

In 1887 Rabinovich settled in Kyiv, played the stock exchange and within a few years went bankrupt. After the pogrom against Jews in Kyiv in October 1905 the family moved to Lviv, and in 1906 the writer went to New York for the first time. He travelled widely in Europe, reading to his audiences, and in 1908 he fell ill with tuberculosis. In December 1914 Sholem Aleichem moved to New York for good. There he finished Motl and worked on From the Fair, a novel about his childhood. He died there on 13 May 1916. Hundreds of thousands of people came to his funeral. He himself had wished to be buried in Kyiv, next to his father.

## Why it matters today

Sholem Aleichem’s works have been translated into 64 languages. In the 1960s the Tevye stories became the Broadway musical Fiddler on the Roof, which is staged all over the world. In Kyiv, from December 1989, [Bohdan Stupka](/en/people/bohdan-stupka/) played Tevye about 400 times in the Ivan Franko Theatre production Tevye-Tevel. Pereiaslav has had a museum in his honour since 1978, and in 2009 a museum flat dedicated to him opened in Kyiv.
