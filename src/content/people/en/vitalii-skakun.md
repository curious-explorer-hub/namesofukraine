## Welder, coder, combat engineer

Vitalii Skakun was born in 1996 in Berezhany, a town in the Ternopil region of western Ukraine. He trained as a welder in Lviv, then worked in the Polish town of Leszno while studying software engineering at Lviv Polytechnic. In November 2019 he signed up with the Ukrainian army. He trained as a combat engineer (a sapper, who lays and clears explosives) in Kamianets-Podilskyi, and from 2020 served in the engineering section of the 35th Marine Brigade. His comrades knew him by the call sign “Baton”. Even before the full-scale war he served on combat missions in the Donbas.

## The bridge at Henichesk

Henichesk, in the Kherson region, sits right next to Crimea. On 24 February 2022, the first day of the full-scale invasion, Vitalii’s unit was holding a position near the isthmus that links Crimea to the mainland. That day he volunteered to mine and blow up the road bridge between Henichesk and the Arabat Spit to hold back a Russian tank column. The blast slowed the column considerably, and the Ukrainian troops had time to regroup and set up a defence.

Sailor Vitalii Skakun was killed blowing up the bridge near Henichesk. He was 25.

## How he is remembered

Just two days later, on 26 February 2022, he was posthumously made a Hero of Ukraine. He was buried in his home town of Berezhany. He is an honorary citizen of Berezhany and of Leszno in Poland, where he once worked. Berezhany issued a postage stamp with his portrait, the Lviv vocational school where he studied put up a memorial plaque, and streets bear his name in Kyiv, Mukachevo, Berezhany, Kostiantynivka and Antonivka in the Kherson region.
