## Four languages and one war

Did you know that one of the "cyborgs" of Donetsk airport was a student of Japanese? Sviatoslav Horbenko spoke fluent English, could get by in French, read Polish and was learning Japanese. He went to the front at 19.

Sviatoslav was born on 26 December 1994 in Poltava. He finished the Ivan Kotliarevskyi Lyceum at 15, then studied in Kharkiv - philology at the pedagogical university and, by correspondence, history at Karazin University. In the winter of 2013-2014 he took part in the Revolution of Dignity and joined the self-defence of the Kharkiv Euromaidan. In spring 2014, when Maidan activists in Kharkiv faced threats and persecution, he transferred to Kyiv to study Japanese at the Institute of Philology of Taras Shevchenko University.

## The volunteer "Skeld"

In summer 2014 Sviatoslav passed his university military-training exams for the rank of junior lieutenant, and in August he volunteered for the 5th Battalion of the Ukrainian Volunteer Corps "Right Sector". He took the call sign "Skeld". From the end of September he fought at Donetsk airport; [Serhii Tabala](/en/people/serhii-tabala/), who also defended the airport, came from the same 5th Battalion.

On 3 October 2014, during the fighting for the airport, Sviatoslav and a comrade were carrying out the wounded. Sviatoslav Horbenko was killed that day at Donetsk airport while rescuing a wounded friend. He was 19. He is buried in Kyiv, at Berkovets Cemetery.

## How he is remembered

On 28 August 2021 Sviatoslav Horbenko was posthumously made a Hero of Ukraine with the Order of the Gold Star. In April 2015 a lecture room at the Institute of Philology of Kyiv University was named after him, and the institute set up a scholarship in his name for students of Japanese. A memorial plaque hangs on his old lyceum in Poltava, and since 2020 he has been an honorary citizen of Vyshhorod.
