## A novel in 100,000 copies

Picture a Ukrainian adventure story that comes out in instalments, like a TV series. That is how Maik Yohansen’s The Adventures of MacLayston, Harry Rupert and Others appeared in 1925: ten instalments, 100,000 copies in all. The same year saw his first book of short stories, Seventeen Minutes.

## A Kharkiv boy with a German surname

Mykhailo (Maik) Yohansen was born on 28 October 1895 in Kharkiv. His father, Hervasii Yohansen, taught German at Kharkiv gymnasiums. Some sources call him German; others, a Russified Swede. His mother, Hanna Kramarevska, was Ukrainian, from a Cossack family of the Starobilsk area. Maik went to a classical gymnasium and in 1917 graduated in history and philology from Kharkiv University with a master’s degree. As a schoolboy he wrote poems in Russian and German, but later burned them all. After the revolution he wrote only in Ukrainian; his first collection, D’hori, came out in 1921.

## A manifesto and VAPLITE

In 1921 Yohansen, [Mykola Khvylovy](/en/people/mykola-khvylovyi/) and Volodymyr Sosiura wrote the manifesto “Our Universal to the Ukrainian Workers and Ukrainian Proletarian Artists”. He belonged to the proletarian writers’ union Hart, and in 1925 he was one of the founders of VAPLITE. When the authorities forced VAPLITE to dissolve, Yohansen refused to join the new organization Prolitfront and set up the apolitical Techno-Artistic Group A instead; it was banned in 1930.

## Film, theater and language

With [Yurii Tiutiunnyk](/en/people/yurii-tiutiunnyk/), Yohansen wrote the script of Zvenyhora, the film made by [Oleksandr Dovzhenko](/en/people/oleksandr-dovzhenko/). With Mykola Khvylovy and [Ostap Vyshnia](/en/people/ostap-vyshnia/) he wrote the operetta Mikado and the revue Hello, on Wave 477! for [Les Kurbas](/en/people/les-kurbas/)’s Berezil theater. In his novel Doctor Leonardo’s Travels through the Switzerland of Slobidska Ukraine (1928) he made the landscape the main character and pushed the people into supporting roles. He was a linguist too: he knew European and Scandinavian languages, helped compile a Russian–Ukrainian dictionary (1926) and worked on the draft of the 1928 Ukrainian orthography.

## Books for children

By the early 1930s the authorities demanded “socialist realism” and nothing else. Rather than write to order, Yohansen turned to children’s books, such as Crabs and Kit-Chudylo (The Odd Cat), and to travel sketches.

## Arrest and execution

On 18 August 1937 Yohansen was arrested in Kharkiv, in the writers’ apartment building Slovo. On 26 October the Military Collegium of the USSR Supreme Court sentenced him to death for membership in a made-up nationalist organization. On 27 October 1937 he was shot in Kyiv, in the Lukianivska prison, three days after [Mykhail Semenko](/en/people/mykhail-semenko/). Researchers believe he was secretly buried in the Bykivnia forest. In 1958 the sentence was quashed for lack of any crime.

## Why it matters today

Maik Yohansen showed that Ukrainian literature could be adventurous, experimental and funny. His adventure novel came out in 100,000 copies, and in Doctor Leonardo’s Travels he made the landscape the main character. He co-wrote the script of Zvenyhora for [Oleksandr Dovzhenko](/en/people/oleksandr-dovzhenko/) and a revue for [Les Kurbas](/en/people/les-kurbas/)’s Berezil theater, helped compile a dictionary and worked on the 1928 orthography. Rather than write to order, he turned to children’s books. He could make ordinary things look marvelous, and the scholar Yurii Lavrinenko called him a “jeweler of words”. Anyone looking for play and surprise in the Ukrainian language will find them in his books.
