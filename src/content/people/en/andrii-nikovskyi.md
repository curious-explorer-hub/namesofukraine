## A boy from near Odesa

Andrii Nikovskyi was born in 1885 in the village of Malyi Buialyk in the Kherson province (now Ivanove, in the Odesa district). He graduated in history and philology from Novorossiysk University in Odesa. Language and literature became his life’s work, but journalism called first.

## The editor of Rada

Before the First World War Nikovskyi edited the Kyiv newspaper Rada, and in 1915 the journal Osnova in Odesa. When the revolution came in 1917, he became editor-in-chief of the newspaper Nova Rada and ran it until 1919.

## From the Central Rada to the ministry

Nikovskyi sat in the Ukrainian Central Rada, and on 23 June 1917 he was elected deputy to its head, [Mykhailo Hrushevskyi](/en/people/mykhailo-hrushevskyi/). In 1918 he became the first head of the Ukrainian National-State Union. From 26 May 1920 he was foreign minister of the Ukrainian People’s Republic, and he headed the commission that drafted the UNR constitution.

## Coming home to a dictionary

Imagine a former minister of the exiled UNR government going home to Soviet Ukraine. In 1924 Nikovskyi was allowed to return. He worked at the All-Ukrainian Academy of Sciences, headed its orthography and terminology commission, and in 1927 published a Ukrainian–Russian dictionary. He also translated Mykola Gogol and Jack London into Ukrainian.

## The SVU trial and Solovki

On 21 July 1929 Nikovskyi was arrested in the case of the “Union for the Liberation of Ukraine” (SVU). At the 1930 show trial, where the writer [Liudmyla Starytska-Cherniakhivska](/en/people/liudmyla-starytska-cherniakhivska/) was also tried, he was sentenced to 10 years of imprisonment, which he served on the Solovetsky Islands. He was released in April 1940. He died in 1942 in besieged Leningrad.

## Why it matters today

Nikovskyi lived two lives: a politician who helped build the Ukrainian People’s Republic, and a scholar who gathered the Ukrainian language into a dictionary. The Soviet regime cut both short, but his dictionary, articles and essays remain.
