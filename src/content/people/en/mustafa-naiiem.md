## The post that started the Maidan

Can one Facebook post bring people out onto a square? In November 2013 Mykola Azarov’s government decided to put off signing the Association Agreement with the European Union. On 21 November the journalist Mustafa Nayyem used his Facebook page to call on people to come to Maidan Nezalezhnosti, Kyiv’s Independence Square, in protest. More than a thousand answered. That was the start of the protest that became known as the Euromaidan, and later as the Revolution of Dignity.

At first Nayyem led the protest’s coordination centre; later he stayed on the Maidan only as a journalist.

## From Kabul to Kyiv

Mustafa Nayyem was born in 1981 in Kabul, Afghanistan, and moved to Ukraine in 1991. He graduated from the Kyiv Polytechnic Institute and from 2004 worked as a journalist in Kyiv. In 2006 he joined the online newspaper Ukrainska Pravda. In 2013 he was one of the initiators of the internet channel Hromadske TV, which went on air in the first days of the Euromaidan.

## From journalism to parliament

In 2014 Nayyem was elected to the Verkhovna Rada, Ukraine’s parliament, on the list of the Petro Poroshenko Bloc. He was deputy head of its parliamentary group and sat on the European integration committee. In 2015, as a member of the Interior Ministry’s board, he helped oversee the launch of the new patrol police. He did not stand in the 2019 election.

## Building instead of writing

In August 2021 Nayyem joined the Ministry of Infrastructure as a deputy minister, in charge of roads, transport safety and border infrastructure. In late January 2023 the government made him head of the newly created State Agency for Restoration and Infrastructure Development. On 10 June 2024 he announced his resignation, and on 18 June the Cabinet of Ministers dismissed him.

## Debates and assessments

Nayyem wrote that he had decided to leave on his own “because of systematic obstruction” that kept him from doing his job effectively. He said the government had cancelled the budget for rebuilding and maintaining roads, taken months to approve payment documents for protective structures, and cut the agency’s salaries, so that it lost a quarter of its staff. In his words, the prime minister’s decision to cancel his official part in the Ukraine Recovery Conference in Berlin confirmed this.

A government source gave a different account: the prime minister had not approved the trip because a meeting at the Cabinet on 12 June was to hear the agency’s report on protecting critical infrastructure and energy sites. The source called Nayyem’s statements “an attempt to avoid reporting on issues that matter today”, and said the prime minister himself was not going to the conference either.

It is noted that during his years in parliament Nayyem missed about a third of the sittings.

## Why it matters

Nayyem’s story shows how one journalist’s call can bring people out onto a square: the rally of 21 November 2013 grew into the Euromaidan, and the Euromaidan into the Revolution of Dignity.
