## A general’s son, not a general’s boy

Andrii Subotin was born in 1987 in the village of Tolbuzino on the Amur River, into a family of border guards. His father became a border guard general. Andrii wanted to follow the same path and was preparing to enter the National Academy of the Border Guard Service, but he failed the medical exam.

So he studied law instead, went through military training at university and became a junior lieutenant. He worked as a lawyer for several years. Then, in May 2019, he arrived in Mariupol with one backpack and a small travel bag to serve in the Donetsk Border Guard Detachment. Getting there on his own, without his father’s help, was a matter of principle for him.

## “Boroda” in a city under siege

That was his call sign: “Beard”. After 24 February 2022, Captain of Justice Subotin stayed to defend Mariupol. On 26 March mortar shrapnel wounded him in the leg and shoulder, but he went back on duty.

When the wounded were ferried across under fire by boat, Andrii and his comrades took them in and brought them to the field hospital at Azovstal. On 16 April he helped find a shelter where civilians were hiding and shared food and water with them. Thanks to the soldiers’ humanity and self-sacrifice, more than 80 civilians were kept alive.

## A wedding at Azovstal

For the engagement Andrii made two rings out of foil and packed them in a small metal box. On 5 May 2022, the anniversary of Azov’s founding, he married the regiment’s press officer [Valeriia “Nava” Karpylenko](/en/people/valeriia-karpylenko/) in a bunker at the plant. Azov’s commander, [Denys “Redis” Prokopenko](/en/people/denys-prokopenko/), witnessed the marriage.

Andrii Subotin was killed on 7 May 2022 at Azovstal in Mariupol. He was 34.

## Why it matters today

Andrii Subotin made his own way. After failing the medical exam for the border guard academy, he became a lawyer, then came to serve in Mariupol without his father’s help, on principle. In the besieged city he went back on duty after being wounded, carried the wounded from the boats to the Azovstal field hospital, and shared food and water with civilians in a shelter. More than 80 civilians were kept alive. Even under siege he made rings out of foil and married [Valeriia Karpylenko](/en/people/valeriia-karpylenko/). He showed that you can choose your own path and look after others along it. He was made a Hero of Ukraine posthumously.
