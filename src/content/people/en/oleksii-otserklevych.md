## Talks outside the airport

Oleksii Otserklevych was born on 28 September 1980 in Bolhrad, in the Odesa region. From 1998 to 2002 he studied at the Odesa Institute of the Ground Forces, then served in posts from platoon commander to deputy brigade commander.

In 2014 Captain Otserklevych, call sign "Kupol," was deputy commander of the 1st Mechanized Battalion of the 93rd Separate Mechanized Brigade, and later acted as its commander. The battalion fought for Donetsk airport both in the autumn of 2014 and in the winter of 2015. The airport's defense was then directed by the 93rd Brigade's commander, [Oleh Mikats](/en/people/oleh-mikats/).

In the autumn of 2014 a ceasefire under the first Minsk agreements was in force at the airport. Rotations to the terminals passed through militant checkpoints, and Ukrainian troops were forbidden to open fire. In December 2014, on the command's decision, Otserklevych went to negotiate with the militant commander "Motorola" so that the troops in the new terminal could be relieved, and the rotation went ahead without a single shot. By his account, a few days later, after enemy tanks hit the ammunition store in the new terminal, he went to talk again. While the cameras filmed the conversation, vehicles with ammunition slipped past the militants without being searched. The militants then threatened to take him prisoner, and he later called it his personal risk. At the time he could not explain his motives, because the ceasefire was still on. During the war he was wounded by shrapnel.

## The Svitlodarsk arc

From December 2015 Otserklevych commanded the 1st Mechanized Battalion of the 54th Separate Mechanized Brigade. Some of the soldiers who had fought alongside him near Donetsk airport signed contracts and followed him to the new battalion. In 2016 the battalion fought on the Svitlodarsk arc: in the fighting on 18 December alone, five of its soldiers were killed. At the end of 2016 Lieutenant Colonel Otserklevych was awarded the Order of Bohdan Khmelnytskyi, 3rd class, for personal courage, dedication and professionalism.

In 2017 he became deputy brigade commander. That year, after 13 months of fighting near Svitlodarsk, the brigade was moved to a training ground in the Kharkiv region, where the washing tents had no taps. The rear services promised to send them in a week at the earliest, so Otserklevych asked a volunteer for help, and the taps arrived the next day. When the volunteer wrote about it on social media, a commission came to the brigade, and Otserklevych was removed as deputy brigade commander and moved to staff work.

## The State Protection Department

On 29 May 2019 President Volodymyr Zelenskyy appointed Lieutenant Colonel Otserklevych head of the State Protection Department, the service that guards the country's top officials. The President's Office said he had been recommended by officers, veterans, volunteers and journalists, and noted that he had firmly stood up for his soldiers in disputes with his superiors. In June 2019 he graduated from the Command and Staff Institute of the National Defense University of Ukraine. On 16 October 2019 he was dismissed as head of the department and promoted to major general.

## Debates and assessments

A video of his meeting with "Motorola," shown by a russian TV channel in December 2014, drew sharp criticism in Ukraine: in it a Ukrainian officer shakes hands with the militant commander and speaks of a war nobody needs. Soldiers of the 93rd Brigade collected several dozen signatures demanding that "Kupol" be dismissed. A General Staff spokesman said at the time that the video showed a rotation carried out without a single shot, and that some phrases had been taken out of context. Otserklevych himself said he had not said the words shown in the video during the handshake, and that his main task was to bring his soldiers out unharmed. Oleh Mikats later explained that rotations through militant checkpoints had been agreed through the Joint Center for Control and Coordination, and that it was during that very meeting that a truck loaded with ammunition got through to the control tower.

## Why it matters today

The story of "Kupol" shows that courage in war is not only found in battle. To relieve exhausted soldiers and get ammunition to them, he went to talk to the enemy in front of the cameras, risking both his freedom and his good name, and for a long time he could not explain why he had done it. And the soldiers who had seen him near Donetsk airport later followed him to the Svitlodarsk arc.
