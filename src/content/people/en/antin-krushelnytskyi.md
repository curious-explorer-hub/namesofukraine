## A one-way ticket

On 8 May 1934 Antin Krushelnytskyi left Lviv, then part of Poland, for Soviet Kharkiv. With him went his wife, his sons, a daughter-in-law and a granddaughter. He believed a great Ukrainian culture was being built in Soviet Ukraine, and he wanted to work for it. The family settled in the Slovo building, a block of flats for writers. Within three and a half years the regime had shot him, four of his sons and his daughter.

## A pupil of Hrushevskyi and Franko

He was born in 1878 in the small town of Łańcut in Galicia, then part of Austria-Hungary and now in Poland, and grew up in Przemyśl. At Lviv University his mentor was [Mykhailo Hrushevskyi](/en/people/mykhailo-hrushevskyi/). As a student Krushelnytskyi was one of the leaders of Young Ukraine, a group that dreamed of an independent Ukraine, and he helped [Ivan Franko](/en/people/ivan-franko/) edit the journal Literaturno-naukovyi vistnyk.

## Readers for Ukrainian schools

He taught at secondary schools in Kolomyia and Stanislaviv, ran the Ukrainian school in Horodenka and compiled school readers and anthologies. He wrote stories, plays and novels about the First World War and the struggle for independence. In spring 1919 he served for a few months as the UNR's minister of education, and that autumn he went to Vienna to publish textbooks for Ukrainian schools.

## Between Poland and Kharkiv

After returning to Galicia in 1925, from 1929 Krushelnytskyi published Novi shliakhy, a journal sympathetic to Soviet Ukraine. The Polish authorities persecuted him: in 1932 they jailed him and banned the journal.

## What happened to the family

In November 1934 the secret police in Kharkiv arrested Antin and his sons Ivan and Taras, and later his other children. Ivan and Taras were shot in Kyiv in December 1934, along with [Hryhorii Kosynka](/en/people/hryhorii-kosynka/) and others convicted in a fabricated "terrorist" case. His wife Mariia died alone in hospital in 1935. Antin was sentenced to ten years and sent to the Solovki prison camp. On 3 November 1937 he was shot with his sons Bohdan and Ostap in the forest of Sandarmokh in Karelia. His daughter Volodymyra, a doctor, was shot the same year. Krushelnytskyi was rehabilitated in 1957.

## Debates and assessments

Some scholars stress that by the late 1920s Krushelnytskyi had become one of the leading pro-Soviet figures in Galicia and championed what he called "Soviet nationalism". Others note that he looked to Soviet Ukraine to protect Ukrainian schools and culture from Polish pressure. His death opened the eyes of many Galician intellectuals who had believed in Soviet Ukraine.

## Why it matters today

All his life Krushelnytskyi worked so that Ukrainian children could learn in their own language from good books: as a teacher, as a compiler of readers and as education minister. His fate shows where the belief that Soviet rule would let Ukraine flourish led: it destroyed him and almost his whole family of writers, scholars and doctors. To remember the Krushelnytskyis is to remember that only a free Ukrainian state can protect Ukrainian culture.
