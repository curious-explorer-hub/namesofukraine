## "The Ukrainian Picasso"

A tram on Lvivska Street in Kyiv breaks apart into shards of planes and lines, and the whole picture seems to move. It was painted in 1914 by Oleksandr Bohomazov, who is called "the Ukrainian Picasso".

## The agronomist who chose the brush

Bohomazov was born in 1880 in the Sumy region; sources give either Yampil or the village of Yankiv Rih. His father wanted him to be an agronomist, and he finished an agricultural school in Kherson. But in 1902 Oleksandr enrolled at the Kyiv Art School, where he studied under Oleksandr Murashko. Among his fellow students were [Alexander Archipenko](/en/people/oleksandr-arkhypenko/) and [Alexandra Exter](/en/people/oleksandra-ekster/). In 1905 he was expelled for joining student strikes.

## The four elements of a picture

In February 1914 the exhibition Kiltse (The Ring), which Bohomazov organised with Exter, opened in Kyiv. He showed 88 works there. That same year he finished his treatise Painting and Elements and dedicated it to his wife, the painter Wanda Monastyrska. In it he worked out how the object, the artist, the picture and the viewer are linked, and argued that art should convey movement and rhythm. Some of these ideas were later developed by Wassily Kandinsky and [Kazimir Malevich](/en/people/kazymyr-malevych/).

## From the Caucasus to the sawyers

In 1915–1916 Bohomazov taught at a school in the Caucasus, where he painted his brightest, almost abstract canvases. After the revolution he decorated Kyiv for public holidays and painted agitation trains and steamboats, and it was then that he caught tuberculosis. From 1922 he was a professor at the Kyiv Art Institute. His last project was a triptych about the work of sawyers: more than 300 studies, but he never began the third panel. Oleksandr Bohomazov died of tuberculosis in Kyiv on 3 June 1930, aged 50.

## A trunk on a handcart

After his death his name vanished from Soviet art history for decades. His widow kept his paintings: during the German occupation she wheeled a trunk of his works on a handcart to relatives in Sviatoshyn, and later brought it home. Only in 1966 did Kyiv see his first solo exhibition. Today his works are in the National Art Museum of Ukraine, the Museum of Modern Art and the Guggenheim in New York, and the Museum Ludwig in Cologne.

## Why it matters today

Bohomazov showed that the newest art was born not only in Paris or Milan but in Kyiv too. He didn't just paint; he explained how a picture works, so Kyiv had a theory of the new art of its own. His name was nearly erased, but one person saved a whole life's work, and now museums around the world show it. Anyone who thinks their city is too ordinary for great art can take a look at his Kyiv tram.
