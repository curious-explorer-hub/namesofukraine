## A journalist from a village near Kalush

How did a village boy from Galicia become his country's voice before the Entente? Mykhailo Lozynskyi was born in 1880 in the village of Babyne near Kalush. He finished the Academic Gymnasium in Lviv, studied law in Lviv and Vienna, and lived for a while in Zurich with his wife. Back in Lviv in 1903, he made his living with his pen: he wrote for the Kyiv daily Rada, from 1906 co-edited the Lviv newspaper Dilo, and published biographies of [Taras Shevchenko](/en/people/taras-shevchenko/) and [Mykhailo Drahomanov](/en/people/mykhailo-drahomanov/). In 1914 he earned a doctorate in law.

## A diplomat for a state under attack

When the Western Ukrainian People's Republic (ZUNR) was proclaimed in November 1918, Lozynskyi joined its parliament, the Ukrainian National Rada. He negotiated with the Poles and with an Entente mission that wanted Lviv and the oil fields near Drohobych handed to Poland. The Western Ukrainian delegation turned those terms down. In March 1919, on [Yevhen Petrushevych](/en/people/yevhen-petrushevych/)'s proposal, Lozynskyi was made deputy state secretary for foreign affairs. In April he led the republic's extraordinary delegation to the peace conference in Paris, travelling with Colonel [Dmytro Vitovskyi](/en/people/dmytro-vitovskyi/). But in June 1919 the Entente allowed the Polish army to occupy Galicia as far as the Zbruch river.

## A professor in Prague

In exile Lozynskyi taught international law for six years at the Ukrainian Free University in Prague. At [Mykhailo Hrushevskyi](/en/people/mykhailo-hrushevskyi/)'s suggestion he wrote Galicia in 1918–1920, one of the first studies of the region's struggle for independence, which he saw as his report to the public. In 1924 he became chairman of the Committee of Nations Oppressed by Poland.

## The road to Solovky

In 1927 Lozynskyi moved to Kharkiv, then the capital of Soviet Ukraine, and became a professor at its Institute of the National Economy. On 21 March 1933 he was arrested in the fabricated "Ukrainian Military Organisation" case and sentenced to ten years in the camps, which he served on the Solovky Islands. On 9 October 1937 an NKVD troika sentenced him to death, and on 3 November 1937 Mykhailo Lozynskyi was shot in the Sandarmokh forest in Karelia. He was 57. He was rehabilitated in 1957.

## Debates and assessments

Some scholars see the move to Kharkiv as a fatal mistake: like many Galician intellectuals, Lozynskyi overrated Ukrainisation and believed the Bolsheviks had changed. Others stress that in Paris he stood for a united Ukraine and accused his colleagues of Galician separatism. His contemporaries valued him as an expert on the events of 1918–1919, but faulted his faith in a separate Galician state between Poland and russia.

## Why it matters today

Lozynskyi showed that even an unrecognised state can speak the language of law: he argued Galicia's case at the negotiating table when the odds were heavily against it. His books and lectures kept for later generations the story of how and why Ukrainians won and lost their state in 1918–1920. And his fate is a reminder of where the belief led that one could work with the Bolshevik regime for Ukraine's sake.
