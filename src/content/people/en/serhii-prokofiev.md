## A boy from a steppe estate

Children in many countries know Peter and the Wolf. Its composer grew up on the steppe of the Donetsk region. Sergei Prokofiev was born on 23 April 1891 on the Sontsivka estate, which his father, an agronomist, managed. His mother, a fine pianist, taught at the village school and became her son's first teacher. The boy wrote his first piano piece at five, and while still a child he wrote an opera, The Giant. For two summers running the composer Reinhold Glière came to Sontsivka to give him lessons. Here Prokofiev heard Ukrainian folk songs from an early age, and their motifs would later echo in his ballet On the Dnieper and his opera Semyon Kotko.

## From St Petersburg to Paris

Sergei entered the St Petersburg Conservatory at 13, in 1904, and in 1914 he won its graduation prize playing his own Piano Concerto No. 1. After the revolution he travelled through Japan to the United States and later settled in Paris. He left for a few months and stayed abroad for 18 years.

## Back to Stalin's Moscow

Prokofiev had always seen emigration as temporary, and in 1936 he moved his family to Moscow: he wanted to write "music for the people". That same year he wrote Peter and the Wolf. Later he was no longer allowed to travel abroad. In 1948 the authorities publicly condemned him for "formalism", and his first wife, the singer Lina Prokofiev, was arrested on trumped-up charges. She spent eight years in the gulag.

## A funeral without flowers

Prokofiev died on 5 March 1953, about 50 minutes before Stalin. Every flower had been taken for the dictator's funeral, and the composer's coffin was carried by hand through side streets.

## A name in the Donetsk region

A Prokofiev museum opened in the school where his mother had taught. The Donetsk Music Academy, which moved to Kyiv in 2014, was named after him, and so was Donetsk airport, destroyed in the fighting of 2014–2015. After russian troops captured Sontsivka, the museum was destroyed and what was left of its collection was taken to occupied Donetsk.

## Debates and assessments

The world knows Prokofiev as a russian and Soviet composer. In Ukraine he is honoured as a native of the Donetsk region, and scholars hear echoes of Ukrainian folk songs in his music. The most precise way to put it: he was born and grew up in Ukraine, and worked in St Petersburg, Paris and Moscow.

## Why it matters today

Prokofiev proves that world-class talent can grow anywhere, even in a small village on the steppe. His music, with its echoes of Ukrainian steppe songs, still plays in concert halls and children's theatres around the world. His life shows that a totalitarian state spares no one, not even a famous artist: a world name did not protect him from persecution, or his wife from the camps. And the ruined museum in Sontsivka is a reminder that the cultural heritage of Donbas is part of Ukraine, and it needs defending too.
