## More than 150 versions of one song

That is how many times Mykola Leontovych’s “Shchedryk” has been rearranged for different voices and instruments. The English-speaking world knows it as “Carol of the Bells”. It all began with a Ukrainian New Year’s song that a composer from Podillia spent years perfecting.

## A priest’s son who polished every note

Leontovych was born in the village of Monastyrok in Podillia, the son of a village priest, and graduated from the theological seminary in Kamianets-Podilskyi. He taught singing and led school and village choirs. He was hard on his own work: he could spend years on a single arrangement before letting anyone see it.

## How “Shchedryk” went round the world

At the end of 1916 “Shchedryk” was first sung by the Kyiv University student choir under Oleksandr Koshyts, and it made the composer famous. Later Koshyts’s Ukrainian Republic Capella, set up with the backing of the Ukrainian People’s Republic, took it on tour across Europe and the Americas, and the melody rang out in New York’s Carnegie Hall. In 1936 Peter Wilhousky wrote English words for it, and “Carol of the Bells” was born.

On 23 January 1921 Leontovych was murdered in his parents’ home by an agent of the Soviet secret police.

## Why it matters today

“Shchedryk” is an example of cultural diplomacy: a Ukrainian melody that became part of Christmas around the world. At the same time, Leontovych’s fate is a reminder of how many Ukrainian artists the Soviet regime destroyed.
