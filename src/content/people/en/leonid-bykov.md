## Who he was

Leonid Bykov was born in the village of Znamianka in the Donetsk region into a working-class family, and grew up in Kramatorsk. After school he entered the Kharkiv Theater Institute, and while still a student he was invited by the director Marian Krushelnytskyi to join the Kharkiv Ukrainian Drama Theater named after Taras Shevchenko. Bykov performed there from 1951 to 1960.

## His story

Bykov became a film star in the 1950s with roles in Tamer of Tigers and Maksym Perepelytsia. In the 1960s he worked at the Lenfilm studio, where he directed the comedy Zaichyk and played its lead. From 1969 Bykov was an actor and director at the Oleksandr Dovzhenko Kyiv Film Studio.

His best-known work was Only “Old Men” Are Going to Battle (1973), a film about Second World War pilots, which Bykov directed and in which he played Captain Titarenko. The film was a huge success with audiences and in 1977 received the Taras Shevchenko State Prize of the Ukrainian SSR. He continued the theme with Aty-Baty, the Soldiers Were Going... (1977). On 11 April 1979 Bykov died in a car crash on a highway near Kyiv. In keeping with his farewell letter to his friends Ivan Mykolaichuk and Mykola Mashchenko, his funeral at Baikove Cemetery was held without official speeches.

## Why it matters today

Bykov’s films combine humor, lyricism and humanity. Generations of viewers still watch them, and his memory is honored with a monument in Kyiv and a squadron of a Ukrainian aviation regiment named after him.
