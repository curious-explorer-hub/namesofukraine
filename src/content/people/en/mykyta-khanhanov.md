## He didn’t hide whose side he was on

Mykyta Khanhanov was born in 2006 in Berdiansk and went to the town’s School No. 1. When russia occupied the city in 2022, he stayed at home and, like his friend [Tihran Ohannisian](/en/people/tihran-ohannisian/), openly backed Ukraine.

## A fabricated case

In October 2022 the occupiers tried to arrest Mykyta. Searches and interrogations followed, and then a case: he and Tihran were accused of plotting “sabotage” on the railway. In May 2023 the boys were formally charged and faced up to 20 years in prison.

## Europe stands up for them

On 15 June 2023 the European Parliament passed a resolution on the two teenagers from Berdiansk by 425 votes. It condemned the torture and the case against them, and demanded that the boys be allowed to leave the occupied territory.

## His death

In June 2023, 16-year-old Mykyta Khanhanov and Tihran were killed in occupied Berdiansk. The occupation authorities called them “pro-Ukrainian terrorists”; human rights defenders say the boys were shot by snipers. As of July 2025, their bodies had not been returned to their families.

## Why it matters today

Mykyta Khanhanov was an ordinary schoolboy, but in his occupied city he would not pretend he wasn’t on Ukraine’s side. His story shows that civic courage has nothing to do with age. The case against two teenagers from Berdiansk reached the European Parliament, and Europe heard how the occupiers persecute Ukrainian teenagers. He was posthumously awarded the Order of Freedom for that courage and his patriotism. Staying true to yourself where that is punished is also a way of standing up for Ukraine.
