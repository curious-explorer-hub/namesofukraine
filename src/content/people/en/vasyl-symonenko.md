## Who he was

Vasyl Symonenko was born in the village of Biivtsi in the Poltava region into a peasant family and lost his father early. In 1957 he graduated from the Faculty of Journalism of Kyiv University, where he began writing poetry and led a student literary studio. He worked for the Cherkasy newspapers Cherkaska Pravda and Molod Cherkashchyny and was the Cherkasy regional correspondent of Robitnycha Hazeta.

## His story

Because of Soviet censorship, Symonenko published little in his lifetime: his only collection, Silence and Thunder, came out in 1962, and that same year he was admitted to the Writers’ Union of Ukraine. His best-known poems were those that circulated in samvydav: satires on the Soviet system, poems about the hard life of the peasants, about resistance to despotism and great-power chauvinism, and a cycle of poems about his love for Ukraine.

Symonenko died in Cherkasy on 13 December 1963, at the age of 28. According to an unofficial version, his death came a few months after he had been beaten by the Cherkasy police. The collections Earth’s Gravity (1964), Wine from Roses (1965) and others were published posthumously; in the diaspora his works appeared in the collection The Shore of Waiting. At first Soviet critics suppressed his samvydav poems while praising him as a poet devoted to the Party line; later his works were suppressed entirely.

## Why it matters today

Symonenko’s poetry became one of the sources of the Ukrainian opposition movement of the 1960s and 1970s, and he himself one of the defining figures in the struggle for Ukraine’s state and cultural sovereignty in the second half of the 20th century. In 1995 he was posthumously awarded the Taras Shevchenko State Prize of Ukraine.
