## The flag and the superstition

Ukraine's Olympic team had a superstition: whoever carried the flag at the opening ceremony would leave the Games without a medal. At the opening of the Tokyo Olympics, Ukraine's flag was carried by Olena Kostevych. In the individual pistol event she finished fourth, one step off the podium. Two days later, paired with Oleh Omelchuk, she won bronze in the mixed team event. The superstition was broken.

## A shooting range in Chernihiv

Olena Kostevych was born on 14 April 1985 in Khabarovsk, Russia, and grew up in Chernihiv. "My home city is Chernihiv," she says. It was her mother who brought her to the sport: one day she suggested Olena try shooting at a shooting range. From 1996, at the age of 11, Olena trained at the Kolos sports school in Chernihiv, and in 2000 she made Ukraine's national team.

## Champion at 17 and at 19

In 2002, aged 17, Kostevych won the World Championships in Lahti, Finland. In 2004, at 19, she became Olympic champion in Athens. In 2011 she was named the best female shooter in the world. At the 2012 London Games she won two bronze medals. And in 2018, sixteen years after her first title, she became world champion again, in Changwon, South Korea.

## Six Olympics

From Athens to Paris 2024, Kostevych competed at six Olympic Games in a row. She has won many European titles and medals. In May 2026, at 41, she was European runner-up once more, in the 25 m standard pistol, and added another silver with the team.

## Why it matters today

For more than twenty years Kostevych has taken on the world's best shooters for Ukraine, and beaten them. Her path started at an ordinary shooting range in Chernihiv and led to Olympic podiums, where she stood under the Ukrainian flag. She showed that medals are decided not by superstition but by composure and precision. And that top-level sport is possible at 17 and after 40, as long as you keep working.
