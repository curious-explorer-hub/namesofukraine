## Where does “Madyar” come from?

Did you know the man who now commands an entire branch of Ukraine's armed forces got his call sign from his own family tree? Robert Brovdi was born in 1975 in Uzhhorod, in Ukraine's Zakarpattia region, to a family of Hungarian descent - hence "Madyar," Hungarian for Hungarian. Before 2022 he knew war mostly from the news: he studied economics at Uzhhorod University, traded grain, worked in construction, and funded a foundation promoting contemporary Ukrainian art.

On 7 February 2022, before the full-scale invasion began, the 46-year-old Brovdi joined a territorial defense unit in Kyiv's Obolon district. From 24 February he commanded a rifle platoon in the 206th battalion of the 28th Separate Mechanized Brigade. In the first weeks of the invasion his platoon helped evacuate people from Irpin, Bucha and Borodianka.

## From a reconnaissance platoon to a new branch of the army

In May 2022, within the 59th Separate Motorized Infantry Brigade, Brovdi put together a small reconnaissance drone unit known as "Madyar's Birds." Its scout and FPV drones helped find and destroy Russian equipment and positions. Brovdi also pioneered laying mines from octocopters - drones with eight rotors.

For 110 days, until March 2023, the unit held the defense of Bakhmut and Soledar. It kept growing - a company, then in January 2024 the 414th battalion, which moved to the Marines, a regiment in June, and by December 2024 the 414th Separate Strike Unmanned Aerial Systems Brigade. From June 2023 to January 2025, "Madyar's Birds" destroyed or hit more than 17,000 enemy targets.

On 8 May 2025, a presidential decree made Robert Brovdi a Hero of Ukraine. Less than a month later, on 3 June 2025, he took a job that hadn't existed before: commander of the Unmanned Systems Forces, a new branch meant to bring Ukraine's scattered drone units under one command.

## Why it matters today

Before 2022 Brovdi was no soldier, but even before the full-scale invasion he joined territorial defense to protect his country. His path shows how drones have remade this war: from a side tool to a branch of the military with its own chain of command. It also shows how one volunteer’s initiative can grow into a new part of an army. It is an example for anyone who thinks that without a military background they can’t change anything.
