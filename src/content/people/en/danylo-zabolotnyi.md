## The boy from Chobotarka

Danylo Zabolotnyi was born in 1866 into a peasant family in the village of Chobotarka in Podillia (now Zabolotne in the Vinnytsia region). He went to gymnasiums in Rostov-on-Don and Odesa, and in 1885 entered the university in Odesa to study natural sciences. In 1889 he was expelled for taking part in a student meeting and arrested; he was let go because of poor health.

That same year Zabolotnyi started work at the Odesa bacteriological station, the one [Ilya Mechnikov](/en/people/illia-mechnykov/) and his colleagues had founded in 1886. In 1891 he passed his master’s exams as an external student and went straight into the third year of medicine at Kyiv University, graduating in 1894.

## Cholera, tested on himself

How do you make sure a vaccine really protects? In 1893 Zabolotnyi and his colleague Ivan Savchenko tested it on themselves. After taking a vaccine by mouth, they drank a live culture of the cholera bacterium, and did not fall ill. Zabolotnyi was also among the first to show that healthy people, who never get sick themselves, can carry cholera germs.

## The plague hunter

In 1897 Zabolotnyi went to India, where plague was raging. From then on he studied infections on expeditions to Arabia, Mongolia, China, Mesopotamia, Scotland, Morocco, Manchuria and other lands. In 1898 he worked at the Pasteur Institute in Paris, and then for over 20 years headed the department of medical bacteriology at the Women’s Medical Institute in Saint Petersburg, the first department of its kind in the Russian Empire.

One of his key discoveries came while he was looking for where plague comes from. In 1911 Zabolotnyi proved that its germs pass to people from wild rodents. One such carrier turned out to be the tarbagan, the Mongolian marmot.

## Odesa and Kyiv

From 1919 to 1923 Zabolotnyi was rector of the Odesa Medical Institute, where in 1920 he founded the world’s first department of epidemiology, the science of how diseases spread and how to stop them. In 1928 he moved to Kyiv and was elected president of the All-Ukrainian Academy of Sciences. That year he also founded the Institute of Microbiology and Epidemiology. Zabolotnyi died in Kyiv on 15 December 1929. He was buried in his home village, next to his wife.

## Why it matters today

Zabolotnyi’s findings about the carriers of cholera and plague helped show how to stop epidemics. The Kyiv institute he founded is now the Zabolotnyi Institute of Microbiology and Virology of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine, and his home village bears the name Zabolotne. A museum to the scientist is there.
