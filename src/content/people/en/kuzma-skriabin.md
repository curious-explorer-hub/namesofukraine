## Who he was

Andrii Kuzmenko, known as Kuzma or Kuzma Skriabin, was born in 1968 in Sambir in the Lviv region. From 1985 he studied at the Karelian Medical Institute in Petrozavodsk, and in 1993, like [Volodymyr Ivasiuk](/en/people/volodymyr-ivasiuk/) two decades before him, he graduated from the Lviv Medical Institute.

## His story

In 1989 Kuzmenko founded the band Skriabin and remained its frontman for the rest of his life. His first notable success came in 1991 at the Chervona Ruta festival in Zaporizhzhia, and his song “Na Dakhu” (On the Roof) was included on a CD of the festival’s winners released in 1992 by the Canadian label Yevshan. Later the band won awards at the Tavriiski Ihry and Pisnia Roku festivals. Over a quarter of a century Skriabin recorded 16 studio albums, from Chuiesh Bil (1989) and Ptakhy (1995) to Tango (2005), Moia Evoliutsiia (2009), Radio Liubov (2012) and 24 (2014). Among the best-known songs is “Stari Fotohrafii” (Old Photographs) from the album Tango.

Kuzma was also a host: he presented a music slot on the show Bomba on UT-2 (1996), the radio show CD ROM on Supernova (1997), the chart show Hariacha Simka (2000–2002), and in 2003–2008 the TV shows Shans (with Nataliia Mohylevska) and Sheikanemo on Inter. In 2006 he published the book Ya, Pobieda i Berlin, with novellas and the band’s lyrics.

On 1 February 2015 Skriabin played a concert in Kryvyi Rih. The next morning, near the village of Lozuvatka, the car Kuzmenko was driving collided with a milk truck; according to the police, the car crossed into the oncoming lane on a bend. The singer died at the scene. He was buried in Briukhovychi near Lviv. On the day of his death thousands of people in cities across Ukraine came out into the squares to sing Skriabin’s songs.

## Why it matters today

Kuzma’s songs are still covered by Tina Karol, Pianoboy, Boombox, Antytila, KAZKA and others, and monuments to him stand in Lutsk and Kyiv. Posthumously he received the Order of Merit, 1st class (2015) and the title Hero of Ukraine (2020). The latter sparked controversy on social media: to supporters he was a cult singer and a sharp critic of those in power, while critics point out that in 2004 he toured in support of Viktor Yanukovych.
