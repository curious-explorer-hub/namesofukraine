## The fifth time at the airport

In 2017 the director Akhtem Seitablaiev was shooting "Cyborgs", a film about the defenders of Donetsk airport, and the men whose stories it was built on came to the set. One of them was Lieutenant Colonel Andrii Hrechanov, call sign "Rakhman". The first time he stepped into the set, he said it felt like walking into the airport for the fifth time. He had gone into the real terminal four times, and the director made him one of the prototypes for the film's characters.

## Back from the reserve

Andrii Hrechanov was born in 1975 in the Dnipropetrovsk region. He graduated from the Kyiv Military Institute of Ground Forces, served 17 years in the army, and left for the reserve as a major in 2009.

When russia seized Crimea and moved into the Donbas, Hrechanov had a civilian job. Some of his former comrades from the service were already fighting, and he had to choose which side to stand on. On his birthday, 17 June 2014, he quit his job and went to the enlistment office, and before long he was commanding a reconnaissance platoon.

## Four times into the terminal

Hrechanov first reached Donetsk airport in the autumn of 2014, and in January 2015 he went back as head of intelligence of the 81st Brigade. A month earlier, in December 2014, he had been awarded the Order of Bohdan Khmelnytskyi, 3rd class. That January other soldiers of the 81st Brigade fought in the new terminal too, among them [Ivan Zubkov](/en/people/ivan-zubkov/) and [Ihor Branovytskyi](/en/people/ihor-branovytskyi/).

It was Rakhman's last time at the airport. After a blast in the terminal he helped pull comrades out of the rubble and led nearly two dozen soldiers out to Ukrainian positions. He was badly wounded himself: doctors later counted 12 pieces of shrapnel in his body. Surgeons in Dnipro saved his hand and his eye.

## Four months in captivity

In the summer of 2015, on his way back from a reconnaissance mission, Hrechanov was ambushed near Novoluhanske and taken prisoner by the militants. He was tortured during interrogations. He spent four months in captivity.

On 30 November 2015 Rakhman was freed in exchange for the russian major Volodymyr Starkov, who had been caught at a Ukrainian checkpoint driving a truck of ammunition for the militants and sentenced to 14 years in prison. President Petro Poroshenko pardoned Starkov and said it had taken 18 attempts to bring Hrechanov home.

## Back in the ranks

After captivity Hrechanov was offered senior posts, but he turned them down and asked to go back to the troops. He served in the marines, then in the National Guard, and then in the Armed Forces again. As of 2021 he is a lieutenant colonel.

## Why it matters today

Andrii Hrechanov already had a career in the army and a quiet civilian life behind him, yet in 2014 he went back of his own accord. At Donetsk airport he went again and again where it was hardest and pulled his comrades out from under the rubble. Not even captivity made him step back: he returned to the ranks. His story lives on in the film "Cyborgs", and it reminds anyone that courage is a choice a person makes again and again.
