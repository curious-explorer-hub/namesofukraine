## Who she was

Nina Matviienko was born in 1947 in the village of Nedilyshche in the Zhytomyr region into a large family. From the age of 11 she studied at boarding schools, and after them she worked at the Khimmash plant in Korosten. In 1966 she joined the vocal studio of the Hryhorii Veriovka Ukrainian Folk Choir, and in 1975 she graduated from the philology faculty of Kyiv University.

## Her story

From 1968 to 1991 Matviienko was a soloist of the Veriovka Choir and at the same time sang in the trio Zoloti Kliuchi (Golden Keys). For her first 30 years on stage her repertoire was mostly ritual folk songs (New Year songs, carols, spring songs, Kupala and harvest songs) and lyrical songs. Composers wrote for her voice, which the Encyclopedia of Modern Ukraine describes as having a “silver, flute-like timbre”: Yevhen Stankovych wrote songs that became part of his folk opera The Fern Flower, Myroslav Skoryk made an arrangement of “I Will Light a Candle,” and Oleksandr Kyva wrote chamber cantatas to words by Tychyna and Shevchenko.

From 1991 Matviienko was a soloist of the ensemble Kyivska Kamerata. In 1995 in New York she played 16 performances with the American theater La MaMa. She appeared in films (Straw Bells, The Lost Letter) and TV plays, including Marusia Churai and Kateryna Bilokur. In the 2000s she sang with Okean Elzy, Tanok na Maidani Kongo and Oleh Skrypka, and in 1999–2014 she taught at the Kyiv University of Culture and Arts. She was a People’s Artist of Ukraine (1985), a Shevchenko Prize laureate (1988) and a Hero of Ukraine (2006). Nina Matviienko died on 8 October 2023 in Kyiv, two days before her 76th birthday.

## Why it matters today

Matviienko brought ancient ritual songs to modern listeners and showed that folk singing can stand alongside new classical music and rock. In 2024 she was posthumously given the distinction National Legend of Ukraine.
