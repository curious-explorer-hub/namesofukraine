## A letter from a city under siege

In April 2022 a marine commander who, in his own words, had been fighting in complete encirclement in Mariupol for more than 50 days wrote to Pope Francis. “I am not a Catholic, I am Orthodox. I believe in God and I know that light always defeats darkness,” began Serhii Volynskyi, known by his call sign Volyna. He wrote that women and children were living in the bunkers under the steelworks, and asked the Pope to help evacuate them and tell the world the truth.

## A boy from Poltava who kept his oath

Volynskyi was born in Poltava in 1992. After ninth grade he went to a military lyceum in Kremenchuk, then to the National Army Academy in Lviv. He served with the marines in Crimea, and in 2014, when Russia occupied the peninsula, he returned to the mainland with the servicemen who stayed loyal to their oath to Ukraine. He went on serving in the 36th Separate Marine Brigade in Mykolaiv and fought in the Donbas.

## The breakthrough to Azovstal

From the start of the full-scale invasion in 2022, Volynskyi defended Mariupol. On 13 April part of the 36th Brigade under his command broke through to the Azovstal steelworks and joined forces with [Denys Prokopenko](/en/people/denys-prokopenko/)’s Azov. In April and May he was acting commander of the brigade. He appealed to world leaders, calling for the trapped soldiers and civilians to be rescued the way the Allies were at Dunkirk in 1940. On 20 May Volynskyi was among the last to lay down his arms, and was taken prisoner by Russia.

## After captivity

On 21 September 2022 he was freed in an exchange together with Prokopenko, [Sviatoslav Palamar](/en/people/sviatoslav-palamar/) and other commanders; they stayed in Türkiye until July 2023. On 1 October 2022 Volynskyi was made a Hero of Ukraine, the country’s highest title. While still in Türkiye he started the Stalevi foundation, which looks after marines and their families. He later left the army for the reserve and works with the foundation.

## Why it matters

His letter to the Pope and the video appeals from the besieged steelworks were among the ways Mariupol’s defenders spoke to the world. The Stalevi foundation carries that work on for the marines serving today.
