## The square that changed art

A black square on a white ground. No people, no landscape, no objects. In 1915 Kazimir Malevich showed this painting and announced a new movement, Suprematism: an art of pure geometric form and color. Black Square became a symbol of 20th-century abstract art.

## A boy from Kyiv

Malevich was born in 1879 in Kyiv to a family of Polish origin. As a teenager in Konotop he painted his first picture, Moonlit Night, and sold it; with the money, he recalled, he bought lots of rings of homemade sausage. He then studied at Mykola Murashko’s Kyiv Drawing School. He went on to work in Moscow, Petrograd and Vitebsk, and in 1923–1926 he headed the Institute of Artistic Culture in Leningrad. He wrote theoretical books and articles, and El Lissitzky was among his students. In 1927 Malevich took his paintings to exhibitions in Warsaw and Berlin, visited the Bauhaus school and met its founder, Walter Gropius.

Ukraine kept pulling him back. In 1928–1930 Malevich was a professor at the Kyiv Art Institute, and in 1930 he had a solo exhibition in Kyiv. He often painted peasants, and scholars see traces of folk art and icon painting in his work. As early as 1915, embroiderers in the village of Verbivka near Kyiv worked from his Suprematist designs: an exhibition catalogue from that year lists two of his scarf designs and one for a pillow. Malevich saw the Ukrainian countryside being destroyed and depicted the Holodomor in several works. One pencil drawing is titled after a line from a folk song, “Where the hammer and sickle are, there is death and famine” (1933): the three figures in it have a sickle, a hammer, a cross and a coffin in place of faces.

When the repression of the intelligentsia began in Ukraine, he went back to Leningrad. In 1930, because of his contacts abroad, he was arrested and accused of spying for Poland, and many of his manuscripts were destroyed. Malevich died in Leningrad in 1935.

## Debates and assessments

Whose artist he is remains disputed. Malevich was born in Kyiv to a Polish family (sources differ on his mother’s origin) and did most of his work in Moscow and Petrograd, so for a long time he was seen mainly as a Russian avant-garde artist. The Polish tradition stresses his Polish roots; Ukrainian scholars stress his Kyiv roots, his work in Ukraine and the influence of folk art. He himself described himself at different times as Polish and as Ukrainian. In documents, under “nationality”, he wrote “Ukrainian”, including on the questionnaire after his 1930 arrest, and in letters he wrote in Ukrainian he also called himself Ukrainian. Since 2022 a number of Western museums have revised their labels for his works, but art historians have not reached a consensus.

## Why it matters today

Malevich showed that a painting need not depict anything at all: it can be pure form and color. His Black Square became a symbol of 20th-century abstract art, and his ideas still shape painting, design and architecture. Born and trained in Kyiv, he kept coming back to teach and exhibit, and scholars see traces of folk art and icon painting in his work. His path shows that one bold idea can change the art of the whole world.
