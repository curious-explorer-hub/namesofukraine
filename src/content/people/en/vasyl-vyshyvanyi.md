## Who he was

Wilhelm von Habsburg was not Ukrainian by birth or descent. He was born in 1895 in Pula on the Adriatic (then Austria-Hungary, now Croatia) into the family of an Austrian archduke of the ruling Habsburg dynasty. He became Ukrainian by choice: he learned the language, published poetry in Ukrainian and considered himself Ukrainian for the rest of his life.

In 1915 Wilhelm graduated from the military academy in Wiener Neustadt and began serving in the 13th Uhlan Regiment, made up mostly of Ukrainians, fighting with it near Kovel, Lutsk and Lityn. From 1917, as a member of the upper house of the Austrian parliament, he worked with the Ukrainian deputies Yevhen Petrushevych, Kost Levytskyi and Yuliian Romanchuk and pressed for Galicia to be divided into Ukrainian and Polish parts.

## His story

From April 1918 Wilhelm commanded the Legion of Ukrainian Sich Riflemen, a Ukrainian volunteer unit of the Austro-Hungarian army. After the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk he led a group of Austro-Hungarian troops in southern Ukraine, and in May–August 1918 he was garrison commander in Oleksandrivsk (today Zaporizhzhia), and in the autumn in Bukovyna. Some Ukrainian political and military figures saw him as a possible candidate for hetman, but he rejected a plan put forward by Volodymyr Petriv and Petro Bolbochan to remove [Pavlo Skoropadskyi](/en/people/pavlo-skoropadskyi/) by force.

In 1919 Vyshyvanyi became a colonel of the Army of the Ukrainian People’s Republic (UPR) and worked in the foreign relations department of its war ministry. In 1920, in protest against [Symon Petliura](/en/people/symon-petliura/)’s military alliance with Poland, he left Ukrainian service and settled in Vienna, where he kept in touch with [Yevhen Konovalets](/en/people/yevhen-konovalets/), Viacheslav Lypynskyi and Yevhen Chykalenko. His book of poems Days Go By was published there in 1921. In 1928 in Berlin he held fruitless talks with Skoropadskyi about uniting the conservative forces of the Ukrainian emigration.

In 1947 Vyshyvanyi was arrested in Vienna by the Soviet secret services (sources differ on the date: August or September). He was taken to Kyiv and in 1948 sentenced to 25 years in prison for “nationalist and anti-Soviet activity”. On 18 August 1948 he died of tuberculosis in the hospital of Kyiv’s Lukianivka Prison (the Encyclopedia of Modern Ukraine gives 18 September).

## Debates and assessments

The monarchist plans around Vyshyvanyi are the most debated part of his story. According to the historian Olesia Isaiuk (Radio Svoboda), the Habsburgs counted on Wilhelm becoming the “future monarch” of the Ukrainians, and in 1918 some Ukrainian political and military circles saw him as a candidate for hetman. In The Red Prince (2008), the biography by the American historian Timothy Snyder, as summarized by LIGA.net, Wilhelm never gave up hope of a throne in a restored Ukrainian state until his death. At the same time, the Encyclopedia of Modern Ukraine stresses that he did not move against Hetman Skoropadskyi by force, and that in 1928, in emigration, he sought an understanding with Skoropadskyi himself.

His conduct in the 1930s is also contested. According to Gazeta.ua, he was in contact with the leaders of the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN), Yevhen Konovalets and Andrii Melnyk, and looked for ties with Germany, but broke them off once he understood that the Nazis had no interest in Ukrainian independence. In Snyder’s account (as summarized by Kirkus Reviews), Wilhelm was briefly taken with Hitler, hoping he would help Ukraine, but by the start of the Second World War he was a firm opponent of the Nazis. Snyder, as quoted by Radio Svoboda, writes that during the war Wilhelm’s Vienna flat “quickly became a center of anti-Nazi intelligence”. After the war, according to Kirkus Reviews and Radio Svoboda, he again kept up contacts with Ukrainian nationalists; under interrogation he himself said he had worked with British intelligence, but against Hitler, not against the USSR. Radio Svoboda stresses that this subject still needs thorough research.

## Why it matters today

Vasyl Vyshyvanyi is the example of a man born far from Ukraine, into one of Europe’s most powerful dynasties, who deliberately chose the Ukrainian cause. In 2020 Ukraine marked the 125th anniversary of his birth, and the world knows his story from Timothy Snyder’s biography.
