## Flowers and weapons

That is the title of Valeriia Karpylenko’s collection of poems. She was born in Mariupol, studied journalism at Mariupol State University, earned a PhD in social communications and taught there. In 2014, when war came to the Donbas, she turned to military journalism.

## The voice of Azov

In 2015 Valeriia joined the press service of the Azov regiment as a volunteer, working with journalists. From 2017 she headed the press service of the Donetsk border guard detachment, and in 2020 she left the military.

When the full-scale invasion began, she went back to Azov. On 27 February 2022 she was brought to the Azovstal steelworks in Mariupol. There she worked in the regiment’s press service, led by [Dmytro Kozatskyi](/en/people/dmytro-kozatskyi/): it was largely through their work that the world learned what was happening in the encircled city. At the plant Valeriia was promoted to captain, and on 17 May 2022 the President awarded her the medal “For Military Service to Ukraine”.

On 5 May 2022, in a bunker at Azovstal, she married a border guard, [Andrii Subotin](/en/people/andrii-subotin/). Two days later he was killed.

## Captivity and books

In May 2022, when the Ukrainian troops left Azovstal under orders, Valeriia was taken prisoner by Russia. She came home in an exchange in April 2023, after almost a year in captivity.

After her release she wrote about what she had been through. In 2024 the Folio publishing house brought out her books “Captivity” and “Azovstal: The Steel Press Service”, about the press service’s work during the defence of the plant. She signs them Valeriia “Nava” Subotina.

## Why it matters today

During the siege, Azov’s press service was one of the few voices getting the truth about Mariupol out to the world, and Valeriia was among those doing that work. A scholar and lecturer, she turned to military journalism in 2014 and went back to Azov when the full-scale invasion began. Almost a year in captivity did not silence her: after her release she wrote “Captivity” and “Azovstal: The Steel Press Service”, books that now keep that story for those who come after. Her words that “Ukraine could not abandon you” are worth hearing for anyone waiting for someone to come home from captivity.
