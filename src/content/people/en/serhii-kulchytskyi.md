## The general who trained the volunteers

Serhii Kulchytskyi was born on 17 December 1963 in the German city of Weimar, where his father, an officer in the Soviet armed forces, was stationed. His own career belonged to Ukraine: after a Suvorov military school in Ussuriysk and an officer academy in the Far East, he served in the marine infantry, then in 1992, as the National Guard of newly independent Ukraine was just taking shape, transferred in as a deputy battalion commander in Ternopil.

## "Soldiers applauded a general"

Over the next two decades he rose to major general and became head of the National Guard's directorate of combat and special training. In spring 2014, after the Revolution of Dignity, it was Kulchytskyi who built and trained the Guard's first volunteer battalions, drawn from former Maidan activists - and he went to the front with them himself. Interior Minister Arsen Avakov later recalled visiting the front line and watching soldiers spontaneously applaud the general: something, he said, he had never seen before.

On 29 May 2014, near Sloviansk in the Donetsk region, the Mi-8 helicopter carrying Kulchytskyi to his troops was shot down. He was 50. He became the first Ukrainian general killed in the war with Russia. A decree of 20 June 2014 posthumously made him a Hero of Ukraine.

## How he is remembered

Kulchytskyi is buried in the Field of Honour at Lychakiv Cemetery in Lviv. A National Guard battalion carries his name, as do streets in Lviv, Ivano-Frankivsk, Drohobych and Kyiv, and monuments to him stand in Sloviansk and Chortkiv.
