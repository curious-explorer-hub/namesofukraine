## The boy from Volhynia

Valerian Polishchuk was born in 1897 into a peasant family in the village of Bilche in Volhynia (now in the Rivne region). He went to the gymnasium in Lutsk and then in Katerynoslav. While still a schoolboy, in December 1914, he published his first poem.

## Years of upheaval

In 1917 Polishchuk entered the Petrograd Institute of Civil Engineers, but the revolution cut his studies short. Each change of power brought another arrest: by the Hetman’s men, by Petliura’s, by Denikin’s. He took part in the insurgent movement of the Directorate of the Ukrainian People’s Republic and was secretary to the UNR politician Mykyta Shapoval. Later he studied at the university in Kamianets-Podilskyi. In Katerynoslav Polishchuk worked for the journal Sich, the same one that printed [Valerian Pidmohylnyi](/en/people/valerian-pidmohylnyi/)’s first stories in 1919. In 1921 the two of them, with Petro Yefremov, brought out the miscellany Vortex of the Revolution.

## Poetry of factories and mines

In the autumn of 1920 Polishchuk founded the art group Hrono in Kyiv, and in 1921 he moved to Kharkiv. There he joined the writers’ union Hart, and in 1925 he founded his own literary group, Avanhard. He tried to combine the best of Impressionism, Futurism and other movements, and called his programme “constructive dynamism”. He wrote in free verse and sang of factories, mines and electricity, as in Electric Glow (1929) and A Tale of Metal and Coal (1931), about the Donbas. He also wrote a short novel about [Hryhorii Skovoroda](/en/people/hryhorii-skovoroda/) (1929) and translated Rimbaud, John Reed and Blok.

## Sadness between the cheerful lines

In time, hopeless moods crept into his cheerful poems. Polishchuk took hard the forced collectivization, the Holodomor of 1932–1933, and the suicides of Mykola Skrypnyk and [Mykola Khvylovy](/en/people/mykola-khvylovyi/). Official critics accused him of nationalism and of praising “wreckers” and “nationalists” such as [Mykola Zerov](/en/people/mykola-zerov/) and Serhii Yefremov.

## Solovki and Sandarmokh

At the end of 1934 Polishchuk was arrested on a fabricated charge of belonging to an anti-Soviet “Borotbist” organization. In March 1935 he was sentenced to 10 years in the camps, which he served on the Solovetsky Islands. In October 1937 a special NKVD troika sentenced him to death. On 3 November 1937 he was shot in the Sandarmokh forest in Karelia; [Mykola Kulish](/en/people/mykola-kulish/), Valerian Pidmohylnyi and [Oleksa Slisarenko](/en/people/oleksa-slisarenko/) were killed there the same day. He was later rehabilitated.

## Why it matters today

The Rivne region remembers Polishchuk: there is a memorial plaque in Bilche, and since 1987 a regional literary prize in his name has been awarded there every year on his birthday. Streets in Rivne and Mlyniv bear his name. In 1997 a book of his writings and memoirs about him came out in Rivne.
