## The flight others didn't come back from

When Oleksandr Shemet was told that he would be the next to fly into encircled Mariupol, one crew had already failed to come back. From March 2022 helicopters were the only way to bring medicine, food and ammunition to the defenders of the Azovstal steelworks and to fly the wounded out. With every run the Russians met them with heavier fire. “I prayed to God that everything would be all right,” the lieutenant colonel recalled.

## The sixth flight

The flight was set for the night of 5 April. Both crews reached Mariupol, unloaded their cargo at Azovstal and took the wounded on board: Shemet’s helicopter carried eight badly wounded men. On the way back the helicopters came under fire from a Russian ship that had moved out into the Sea of Azov. “The helicopter was tossed around like a toy by the blast waves,” the pilot said. He kept manoeuvring and brought the aircraft and everyone in it home. The second crew, with wounded on board, went missing seven kilometres short of the front line, and a rescue helicopter sent to help was shot down too. It was the last successful air run to Azovstal.

On 7 April 2022 Shemet, navigator-pilot Oleksandr Liashenko and flight technician Andrii Shohan were made Heroes of Ukraine, the country’s highest title.

## From Koriukivka to Lviv region

Oleksandr Shemet was born on 13 December 1970 in Koriukivka in Chernihiv region. After military college he was posted to Lviv region, where he spent most of his life. He flew with the 12th Separate Army Aviation Brigade, fought in the anti-terrorist operation in eastern Ukraine from 2015, and defended Kyiv at the start of the full-scale invasion.

## The last mission

On the evening of 17 December 2025 the Mi-24 crew under Shemet’s command took off to intercept Russian Shahed attack drones and did not return. All four crew members were killed. The helicopter came down in Cherkasy region; the preliminary version is that it collided with a drone.

## Why it matters today

The helicopters were the only thread between the besieged steelworks and the rest of Ukraine, and the pilots flew there knowing that not all of them would return. Oleksandr Shemet went: he delivered the cargo to the defenders and brought the aircraft and eight badly wounded men home. It was the last successful air run to Azovstal. “Freedom comes at a high price,” he said of comrades who don’t come back from missions. His service ran from 2015: the anti-terrorist operation, the defence of Kyiv, and later intercepting Shahed drones. He showed that even encircled comrades can be reached when someone is willing to take on the risk.
