## Beyond 70 metres

Until 1975 no woman in the world had thrown the discus beyond 70 metres. Faina Melnyk (often spelled Melnik in Soviet-era results) was the first, with 70.20 m. In all she set 11 world records in the discus, and in 1987 the International Association of Athletics Federations named her the greatest female discus thrower of all time.

## From Bakota to Munich

Faina was born in 1945 into a Jewish family in Bakota, a village on the Dniester in Podillia, western Ukraine. The village no longer exists. She came to athletics late, at almost 25. She competed for the Sevan club in Yerevan, graduated from the Yerevan Institute of Physical Culture and was coached by Kim Bukhantsov.

Her rise was fast. In 1971 she became European champion, and in 1972 in Munich, Olympic champion. From 1973 she competed for Spartak Moscow, and in 1974 she won the European title again. At the 1976 Olympics in Montreal, despite her records, she finished only fourth.

## A second career

After her sporting career Faina Melnyk lived in Moscow. She graduated from a medical dental institute and worked as a dentist, and she also coached. Two of her pupils, Natalya Lisovskaya and Svetlana Krivelyova, became Olympic shot put champions. She died in Moscow in 2016.

## The discus that stayed at home

Her home region has not forgotten her. Since 2010, Kamianets-Podilskyi has held a Khmelnytskyi Oblast throwing championship every April in honour of two 1972 Olympic champions, and Faina Melnyk is one of them. And in 2021 Mykhailo Brudin, a 16-year-old discus thrower from Druzhkivka in Donetsk Oblast and her great-nephew, said his family keeps her discus and her training diaries at home, and that her notes help him train.

## Why it matters today

A girl from a small village in Podillia that is no longer on the map became the greatest female discus thrower of all time and the first woman to break the 70-metre barrier. She started late, at almost 25, and showed that it is never too late for top-level sport. Her knowledge outlived her career: she raised Olympic champions, and her training diaries pass that experience on to a new generation of throwers in Ukraine. Anyone who wonders whether it's too late to start can think of the girl from Bakota.
