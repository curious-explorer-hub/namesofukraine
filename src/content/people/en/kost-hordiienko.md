## The rival who became an ally

Did you know that the kish otaman who brought the Zaporozhians over to Mazepa had long been his opponent? Kost Hordiienko distrusted the hetman while he served the Muscovite tsar. But when [Ivan Mazepa](/en/people/ivan-mazepa/) turned against Moscow, Hordiienko stood by him, personal dislike and all.

## From student to kish otaman

Hordiienko is believed to have come from the Poltava region and studied at the Kyiv-Mohyla Collegium before joining the Platnyrivskyi kurin, a unit of the Sich. In 1702 the Zaporozhians first elected him kish otaman, their chief commander, and re-elected him 11 or 12 times, sources give. He defended the Host's rights and liberties and resisted Muscovite fortresses and garrisons on Zaporozhian land.

## Eight thousand Zaporozhians

In March 1709 Hordiienko brought about 8,000 Zaporozhians to Mazepa and King Charles XII of Sweden. Contemporaries recalled that he greeted the king with a speech in Latin. On 8 April at Velyki Budyshcha, through Mazepa, he agreed with Charles XII that the king would protect the Zaporozhians and the allies would fight Moscow together. The Zaporozhians beat a tsarist detachment near Tsarychanka and drove Muscovite garrisons from towns on the Orel and Vorskla. Peter I struck back: in May 1709 his troops destroyed the Chortomlyk Sich.

## A Sich in exile

After the defeat at Poltava, Hordiienko helped the Swedes and Mazepa cross the Dnipro near Perevolochna and withdrew to Bender. After Mazepa's death he backed the new hetman, [Pylyp Orlyk](/en/people/pylyp-orlyk/), and in 1711 joined his failed campaign into Right-Bank Ukraine. In 1712 he parted with Orlyk and later led the Oleshky Sich, under the Crimean khan's protection. Until 1728 he was among those who refused to return under Moscow's rule. He died in May 1733 at the Kamianka Sich. His grave, near today's village of Respublikanets in the Kherson region, has survived.

## Why it matters today

Hordiienko showed that a community's rights and freedom can matter more than personal grudges: for a common cause he stood beside a hetman he had long distrusted. The Zaporozhians paid for that choice with their Sich, but they did not submit. For almost twenty years he kept the Zaporozhian Host out of Moscow's rule. Today the 57th Separate Motorized Infantry Brigade bears his name, and its recruits have taken their oath at his grave.
