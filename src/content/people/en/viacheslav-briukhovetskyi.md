## Who he is

Viacheslav Briukhovetskyi was born in 1947 in Vladikavkaz (then Dzaudzhikau, Russian SFSR), where his parents had moved from Ukraine after the war. Just two weeks after his birth the family returned to Ukraine and settled in Cherkasy. In 1974 he graduated from Kyiv University, worked at the newspaper Literaturna Ukraina and, from 1980, at the Institute of Literature of the Academy of Sciences of Soviet Ukraine, where he headed the department of literary theory. He holds a doctorate in philology and has been a member of the Writers’ Union since 1982. He taught at the University of Manitoba (Canada) and Rutgers University (USA).

## His story

As a literary scholar Briukhovetskyi studied the nature and purpose of literary criticism, which he defined as a distinct activity on the border of literature, scholarship and journalism. He wrote books on [Lina Kostenko](/en/people/lina-kostenko/) and [Mykola Zerov](/en/people/mykola-zerov/) (both 1990) and translated from English Ivan Fizer’s monograph on the linguist Oleksandr Potebnia.

In 1991, by an ordinance of the Chairman of the Verkhovna Rada (Ukraine’s parliament), the historical Kyiv-Mohyla Academy was re-established on its historical grounds as an independent university. Briukhovetskyi initiated this revival and became its organizing rector; the university officially opened in August 1992. In 1994 a decree of President Leonid Kravchuk gave it national status, and Briukhovetskyi became the university’s first elected president, leading it until 2007.

The new “Mohyla” was built on the model of North American universities: students were admitted through rigorous entrance tests, teaching was in Ukrainian and English, and it was the first in Ukraine to introduce a four-year bachelor’s degree (1992), a 100-point grading scale and a two-year master’s degree (1996). The Encyclopedia of Ukraine says the university is generally considered the least corrupt of the country’s classical universities, and Briukhovetskyi its “founding father”. In 2007 he was made a Hero of Ukraine “for an outstanding contribution to the national revival of Ukraine”, and since then he has been honorary president of the university (as of October 2026).

## Why it matters today

The revived Kyiv-Mohyla Academy has become one of Ukraine’s leading universities: rankings consistently place it among the best, especially in the humanities, social sciences and computer science. The Encyclopedia of Ukraine describes its student body as the most selective among the country’s classical universities.
