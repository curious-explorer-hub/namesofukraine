## Who he was

Yurii Dadak was born in 1980 in Berezhany, Ternopil Oblast, into a scientist’s family, and grew up in Lviv. He finished Lviv Lyceum No. 93 in 1997 and in 2002 graduated from the Ukrainian National Forestry University in woodworking technology. In 2008 he defended a Candidate of Sciences dissertation on capturing dust in wood processing and became an associate professor in the department of sawmilling, joinery and wooden building products.

## His story

He began writing poetry at 14, and in 2011, while preparing his first collection, *Crimson Lyrics* (*Bahriana liryka*), he took the pen name Yurii Ruf. Then came the collections *Time of Revolution* (2014) and *At the Turn of Epochs* (2015), which is dedicated to the soldiers of the Russo-Ukrainian war and names military units and battles, the children’s book *The Storyteller* (*Kazkovyk*, 2018) and *Vanilla or Steel?!* (2021). Ruf criticized poetry that, in his view, portrayed Ukrainians only as victims, and saw his own patriotic, military-themed poetry as an answer to it. In 2015 he founded the NGO “Literary and Educational Project Dukh Natsii”, which became popular in nationalist circles, and for many years he hosted the Kholodnyi Yar festival.

On 24 February 2022 Ruf volunteered for the 24th King Danylo Separate Mechanized Brigade and fought his first battle as early as 3 March; his unit fought around Popasna in Luhansk Oblast. He was killed by enemy mortar fire on 1 April 2022. He was buried at Lychakiv Cemetery in Lviv and posthumously awarded the Order for Courage, 3rd class.

## Why it matters today

Ruf combined science, poetry and service: an engineer and researcher, he wrote poems to raise fighting spirit, and when the full-scale invasion began he went to the front himself. Streets in Lviv, Khmelnytskyi and other cities bear his name, and in 2024 a mural in his honor was unveiled in Lviv.
