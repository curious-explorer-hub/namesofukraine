## Who she was

Mariia Prymachenko (Pryimachenko) was born in 1909 in the village of Bolotnia in the Kyiv region, near Ivankiv, and lived there almost all her life. As a child she had polio and was left disabled. She taught herself to paint, decorating houses and embroidering. In 1935–1938 she studied and worked in Kyiv at the Central Experimental Workshops at the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, where her mentors were folk masters and professional artists.

## Her story

In 1936 Prymachenko’s work was shown at the First Republican Exhibition of Folk Art in Kyiv, Moscow and Leningrad, where she won first prize, and in 1937 at the World’s Fair in Paris. It is often said that Pablo Picasso saw her work there and said he bowed before “the artistic miracle of this brilliant Ukrainian woman”, but no primary source for the phrase is given.

Prymachenko painted flowers in unusual colors, birds as symbols of goodness and peace, and fantastic “beasts” that became her signature. She began with watercolor, later worked in gouache, and from the mid-1960s moved from white backgrounds to colored ones. She illustrated children’s books, made a series about the Chornobyl disaster (1986) and created more than 800 works in all. In 1966 she received the Shevchenko Republican Prize, and in 1988 the title People’s Artist of the Ukrainian SSR. For the 100th anniversary of her birth, UNESCO declared 2009 the Year of Mariia Prymachenko.

## Why it matters today

In late February 2022, in the first days of the full-scale invasion, a Russian shell hit the local history museum in Ivankiv. The museum’s guard and his wife carried Prymachenko’s works out of the burning building under fire, and they survived. Since then her “beasts” and doves have become a symbol of Ukraine’s resilience, in museums around the world and on stamps, murals and posters.
