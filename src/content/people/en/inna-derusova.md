## “They loved her and called her Mom”

That is how her comrade Petro Lysenko remembered Inna Derusova. She came to the battalion with the third wave of mobilization, in a private’s post in the medical service, but ended up doing the work of three people: head of the medical service, head of the aid station and medical instructor. “For every soldier she became a kind of guardian angel,” he said.

## A nurse who trained medics

Inna was born in 1970 in Kryvyi Rih, an industrial city in central Ukraine. She graduated from a medical college and worked as a nurse at kindergarten No. 46 run by the Kryvyi Rih-Holovnyi railway station. In 2015 she joined the Armed Forces, became a combat medic in the 58th Separate Motorized Infantry Brigade, named after Hetman Ivan Vyhovskyi, and served in Donbas until 2018, in Avdiivka, Pisky, Zolote, Toretske and elsewhere. Many medics who came to the war from civilian life passed through her hands. Her comrade said that within two weeks she could turn bewildered young women into real combat medics. In 2016 her son signed a contract and began serving in her unit, and Inna was very proud of him. In 2021 Inna was awarded the Defender of the Motherland Medal.

## Two days in Okhtyrka

On the day russia launched its full-scale invasion, 24 February 2022, Sergeant Derusova, senior combat medic of the 15th Separate Motorized Infantry Battalion, was on her way back to her unit from leave. She stopped in Okhtyrka in the Sumy region, which russian forces were shelling. There were many wounded, and Inna stayed with them. In two days she saved more than ten soldiers.

Inna Derusova was killed by artillery fire in Okhtyrka on 26 February 2022 while giving first aid to the wounded. She was 51.

## Why it matters today

Inna Derusova saved people with more than her own hands: her skill lived on in every medic she trained. On the first day of the full-scale war she did not travel on, because the wounded were there, and in two days she saved more than ten soldiers. Her life shows that courage can mean choosing to stay where you are needed. She was made a Hero of Ukraine posthumously, and the President called her the first woman to receive the title that way. Streets in Mukachevo and Kryvyi Rih now bear her name, her home city made her an honorary citizen posthumously, and a memorial plaque was put up on her school.
