## From furniture to the assault line

Did you know that Hero of Ukraine Vladyslav Datskyi made furniture between two wars? Born in 1998, he signed up as a contract marine in 2016 and fought in the anti-terrorist operation in eastern Ukraine. After his service he worked on building sites in Poland, then took up furniture making.

## “Datsyk” of the 3rd Assault

When Russia launched its full-scale invasion on 24 February 2022, Datskyi took up arms again. He defended Kyiv, then fought in the south. Later he joined the 3rd Separate Assault Brigade, where he is known by the call sign Datsyk. In 2024 he took command of the 2nd Company of the brigade's 2nd Assault Battalion.

## Avdiivka and six prisoners

In February 2024, in Avdiivka in the Donetsk region, Datskyi's assault group killed and wounded about 200 Russian troops. He was wounded himself, yet he gave first aid to one of his soldiers and carried him out personally.

In December 2024, in the Kharkiv region, his group pulled off an operation that sounds made up: at first the Russians took the Ukrainian assault troops for their own. The six Russians only realised they had been captured once they reached a Ukrainian position. The prisoners gave up valuable intelligence.

## Hero of Ukraine

Within two years Datskyi received the Order for Courage, 3rd and then 2nd class. On 5 May 2025 Junior Lieutenant Datskyi was made a Hero of Ukraine, the country's highest title, for personal courage and heroism in defending Ukraine. By early 2026 he was in command of the 2nd Assault Battalion.

## Why it matters today

Vladyslav Datskyi is a former marine, builder and furniture maker who went back to the army when his country needed him. His path from fighter to battalion commander shows that in Ukraine's army it is the people who take responsibility for those beside them who step forward. Even wounded, he thought of his soldier first. His story is a reminder that heroes are ordinary people who don't back down.
