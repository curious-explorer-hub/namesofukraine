## A speech that became a programme

Can you demand independence for a country that isn't on any map? In the winter of 1900, at gatherings in memory of [Taras Shevchenko](/en/people/taras-shevchenko/) in Poltava and Kharkiv, the lawyer Mykola Mikhnovskyi gave speeches about exactly that. The same year they were printed in Lviv as a pamphlet, Independent Ukraine. Its core idea: Ukrainians must have a state of their own, and the only thing left to discuss with Russia was "the means and methods of the struggle". The pamphlet became the programme of the Revolutionary Ukrainian Party.

## A priest's son from Turivka

Mikhnovskyi was born on 31 March 1873 in the village of Turivka (now in Kyiv region), the son of a village priest. He studied law at Kyiv University, and as a student in 1891 he was one of the founders of the secret Brotherhood of Taras. In 1899 he moved to Kharkiv and worked as a lawyer.

## A party for independence

When most of the Revolutionary Ukrainian Party moved away from independence, Mikhnovskyi helped found the Ukrainian People's Party in 1902. He wrote its programme and its "Ten Commandments", and later co-wrote a draft constitution for an independent Ukraine.

## 1917: an army first

After the fall of the tsar, Mikhnovskyi argued that only an army of its own could keep Ukraine free. He founded the Polubotok Ukrainian Military Club and helped form the Bohdan Khmelnytskyi Regiment. The leaders of the Central Rada, [Mykhailo Hrushevskyi](/en/people/mykhailo-hrushevskyi/) and [Volodymyr Vynnychenko](/en/people/volodymyr-vynnychenko/), still hoped for a federation with a democratic Russia and did not back him; in the summer of 1917 Mikhnovskyi was sent to the Romanian front. In 1918, when Hetman [Pavlo Skoropadskyi](/en/people/pavlo-skoropadskyi/) proclaimed a federation with Russia, Mikhnovskyi joined the uprising against him.

Under the Bolsheviks he was imprisoned; later he lived in the Kuban and worked as a teacher. In the spring of 1924 he came back to Kyiv and was promptly arrested by the Soviet secret police (GPU). On 3 May 1924 Mikhnovskyi was found hanged in the garden of his friend Volodymyr Shemet's house. His death is linked to relentless persecution by the Soviet authorities.

## Debates and assessments

In his lifetime, moderate Ukrainian politicians mostly saw Mikhnovskyi as a dangerous and eccentric extremist. Historians note his difficult, fiery character: he quarrelled often and never built a strong movement around himself.

The most contested text is the "Ten Commandments" of the Ukrainian People's Party (1903). The second one reads: "All people are your brothers, but Muscovites, Poles, Hungarians, Romanians and Jews are enemies of your people for as long as they rule over us and exploit us." Even his contemporaries criticised it as chauvinism. His supporters stressed the words "for as long as they rule", and scholars note that his draft constitution placed no limits on citizens of other nationalities, with one exception: ministers had to be Ukrainian by birth. In 1904 he took part in an attempt to blow up the Pushkin monument in Kharkiv.

Some historians see him as more far-sighted than the leaders of the Central Rada and find his warnings about Russia relevant today. Others write about his intolerance of others and note that his ideas found little support in 1917–1918.

## Why it matters today

When most of the national movement asked only for autonomy, Mikhnovskyi insisted on full independence from Russia, guarded by an army of Ukraine's own. His ideas shaped the generation of the 1920s and 1930s, above all in Western Ukraine and in the emigration. A century after his death, the question of how to live next to Russia has again become a question of survival.
