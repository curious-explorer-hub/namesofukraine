Can a city surrounded on every side hold out for 86 days? Mariupol, a port on the Sea of Azov that was home to more than 400,000 people before the war, came under siege in the first days of the full-scale invasion. It was defended by Azov fighters, marines, National Guardsmen, border guards, police officers, volunteers and territorial defence. For 82 of those days they fought fully encircled, and the city's last fortress was the Azovstal iron and steel works.

## Why it happened

By taking Mariupol, russia would open a land route from occupied Donbas to Crimea and could move its troops to other parts of the front. The city had known war before: in the spring of 2014 pro-russian militants seized it, and on 13 June 2014 Ukrainian volunteer battalions, Azov among them, freed it. On 24 February 2022 russian troops moved on Mariupol from the east, north and west.

## What happened

Within days the city was without power and water, and in early March the russians closed the ring (sources give 1 or 2 March). On 9 March an airstrike destroyed the maternity hospital and a hospital in the centre. On 16 March an aerial bomb hit the Drama Theatre, where hundreds of civilians were sheltering. On both sides of the building people had written "children" in huge letters, visible from the sky. How many died there is still not known: estimates range from at least a dozen to about 600.

The defenders fought for every block. In early April the commander of the 36th Marine Brigade, [Volodymyr Baraniuk](/en/people/volodymyr-baraniuk/), led a column in an attempt to break out of the city and was captured. Part of the marines, led by [Serhii Volynskyi](/en/people/serhii-volynskyi/), fought their way through to Azovstal and joined [Denys Prokopenko](/en/people/denys-prokopenko/)'s Azov. In the plant's underground shelters, alongside the defenders and the wounded, civilians were hiding, women with children among them. Between 30 April and 8 May the UN and the Red Cross took them out to Zaporizhzhia in several convoys.

On 4 May the russians began storming the plant. In mid-May the commander of the National Guard's 12th Brigade, [Denys Shleha](/en/people/denys-shleha/), said there were about 600 wounded at Azovstal. On 16 May the General Staff announced that the Mariupol garrison had fulfilled its combat mission, and the commanders at the plant were ordered to save their soldiers' lives. The badly wounded began to be taken out that day, and on 20 May Prokopenko announced the order to stop defending the city. The last defenders left the plant and were taken prisoner; Ukrainian officials said they had bought the country time to prepare its reserves.

How many civilians died in Mariupol is not known either: estimates range from at least 8,000 to more than 20,000. The world saw the siege through the eyes of AP journalists who worked in the city for 20 days; their film "20 Days in Mariupol" won Ukraine's first Oscar in 2024.

## Captivity and Olenivka

Many of the prisoners were taken to a penal colony in occupied Olenivka. On the night of 28–29 July 2022 explosions tore through the barracks where Azovstal defenders were held: more than 50 prisoners were killed. A UN investigation rejected russia's claim that a Ukrainian rocket had hit the barracks. On 21 September 2022, in an exchange, 215 soldiers came home, among them Prokopenko, Volynskyi, Shleha and other commanders. Not everyone came back: many of Mariupol's defenders remained in captivity for a long time.

## Why it matters today

Mariupol showed that even when fully surrounded, without power, water or any certainty about tomorrow, people can hold out to the end and look after one another. Every day the city held was a day gained for all of Ukraine. The Drama Theatre and the maternity hospital are reminders of why attacks on civilians must never be forgotten or left unpunished. And those still in captivity should know that they are awaited at home.
