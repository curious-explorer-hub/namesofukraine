Why does all of Ukraine know a ruined control tower in Donetsk? Beneath it, for 242 days, from 26 May 2014 to 22 January 2015, Ukrainian soldiers held the airport, even though the city itself had already been seized by militants. The enemy never managed to break them and called them “cyborgs”. In the end the concrete terminals collapsed, and ever since people have said of the defenders: “They held out, the concrete did not.”

## Why it happened

In the spring of 2014, after seizing Crimea, russia stirred up a war in the Donbas. In April pro-russian armed groups backed by russia seized the Donetsk regional administration, and later the city. The airport stayed Ukrainian. On 26 May militants and mercenaries entered the new terminal, but Ukrainian special forces would not give up the airfield without a fight and, backed by jets and helicopters, drove them out.

## What happened

Over eight months more than twenty army and volunteer units took turns holding the airport. From late August to 3 October 2014 Colonel [Oleksandr Trepak](/en/people/oleksandr-trepak/) commanded the defence on the ground, and from September the commander of the 93rd Brigade, [Oleh Mikats](/en/people/oleh-mikats/), directed the defence of the airport.

The Minsk agreements were signed on 5 September, but the assaults only grew fiercer. On 28 September Captain [Serhii Kolodii](/en/people/serhii-kolodii/) took the wheel of an infantry fighting vehicle himself to bring out comrades who had been cut off, and was killed that day, while the crew of the tank commander [Yevhen Mezhevikin](/en/people/yevhen-mezhevikin/) knocked out enemy tanks. That autumn the volunteers [Sviatoslav Horbenko](/en/people/sviatoslav-horbenko/), who was carrying out the wounded, and 18-year-old [Serhii Tabala](/en/people/serhii-tabala/), who was defending the control tower, were killed here. Paratroopers of the 79th Brigade, [Viacheslav Zaitsev](/en/people/viacheslav-zaitsev/), [Valerii Chybinieiev](/en/people/valerii-chybinieiev/) and [Pavlo Chaika](/en/people/pavlo-chaika/), fought in the terminals, and the grenadier [Ruslan Borovyk](/en/people/ruslan-borovyk/) photographed the defence even under sniper fire. At the end of November the defenders pulled back from the old terminal to the new one.

On 9 December a ceasefire began, and rotations passed through militant checkpoints. To replace the soldiers and bring in ammunition, [Oleksii Otserklevych](/en/people/oleksii-otserklevych/), an officer of the 93rd Brigade, went out to negotiate with a militant leader.

After the New Year the fighting resumed, and on 13 January 2015 the control tower fell. The combat medic [Ihor Zinych](/en/people/ihor-zinych/) treated the wounded right under fire, and on 18 January Senior Lieutenant [Ivan Zubkov](/en/people/ivan-zubkov/) led his company in a breakthrough to reach the cut-off defenders. On 19 and 20 January militants blew up the new terminal, and its floors collapsed. Dozens of soldiers died under the rubble, among them Zinych, Zubkov and [Petro Polytsiak](/en/people/petro-polytsiak/). [Ihor Branovytskyi](/en/people/ihor-branovytskyi/) and [Andrii Havryliuk](/en/people/andrii-havryliuk/) held the terminal to the end and were killed in captivity on 21 January. The scout [Andrii Hrechanov](/en/people/andrii-hrechanov/), badly wounded himself, led nearly two dozen soldiers back to Ukrainian lines.

On 22 January the last defenders left the new terminal; this day is taken as the end of the defence. A small group of paratroopers held out in the fire station building for about two more days, so by some counts the defence lasted 244 days. Sources give between 97 and 109 Ukrainian soldiers killed and more than 400 wounded.

## Why “cyborgs”

The nickname appeared in September 2014. According to the most common account, it came from the enemy, who could not explain why the airport would not fall to their assaults. The defenders took up the word, and it became a sign of endurance. Since 2022, 20 January has been the Day of Remembrance of the Defenders of Donetsk Airport in Ukraine.

## Why it matters today

The airport was shelled every day, and yet for eight months its defenders would not give it up. They showed that Ukraine would not give up its land without a fight. The word “cyborg”, which the enemy used to explain its own helplessness, became for Ukrainians a name for the unbreakable. To remember these people is to know that endurance rests not on concrete, but on people who stand up for one another.
