## Applied
- **Bohun** (ERROR): at Zhvanets (1653) the Cossack-Tatar army besieged the Polish camp; he "fought at Zhvanets", not "defended" it (ЕІУ).
- **Sirko**: "most researchers" regard the Letter to the Sultan as a literary work (only uk.wiki states the scholarly consensus explicitly).
- **Skoropadskyi**: "opened or Ukrainianized" 150+ gymnasiums (IEU Hetman government; UINP); diplomatic relations "with the Central Powers and several neutral countries" (IEU); first buried in Metten, later reburied in Oberstdorf (ЕІУ, UINP); Wannsee coordinates; UINP 150th-anniversary materials added as a source; summary shortened to stay within 300 characters.
- **Petliura**: victim figures now sourced to IEU "Pogrom" (Gergel: 31,000+ killed; other estimates up to 60,000); UPR units credited with about 40% of pogroms per Gergel (ЕІУ: «значна частина погромів»); critical side named (Zosa Szajkowski); uk extradition sentence corrected (Poland was not obliged to hand him over; it refused); comma typo. IEU "Pogrom" added to sources.
- **Makhno**: Eichenfeld (November 1919) per Patterson (JMS 2014): carried out by a Makhnovist cavalry squadron with local peasants; no direct order from Makhno found, but his anti-"kulak" rhetoric encouraged violence; some Makhnovist units did commit pogroms despite the command's efforts. Source link now points to the article PDF.
